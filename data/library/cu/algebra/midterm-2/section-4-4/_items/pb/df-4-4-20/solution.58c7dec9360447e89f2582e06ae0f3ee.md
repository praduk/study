**(a) Automorphisms preserve the defining family.** Let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(P)$ and let $A\le P$ be abelian. Its image $\alpha(A)$ is a subgroup and is abelian, since images of commuting elements commute. An isomorphism preserves the minimum number of generators: the images of a generating set generate the image, giving $d(\alpha(A))\le d(A)$, and applying $\alpha^{-1}$ gives the reverse inequality. Thus $d(\alpha(A))=d(A)$. Consequently $\alpha$ permutes exactly the abelian subgroups attaining $m(P)$. The subgroup generated by this family is therefore preserved: $\alpha(J(P))=J(P)$. This is characteristicity.

**(b) Q₈.** The proper nontrivial subgroups are $\langle-1\rangle,\langle i\rangle,\langle j\rangle,\langle k\rangle$, and each is cyclic. The whole group is nonabelian. Thus $m(Q_8)=1$, and all four listed subgroups attain it. In particular, the order-$2$ subgroup must be included, not just the maximal cyclic subgroups.

**D₈.** The two subgroups

$$
\langle r^2,s\rangle,\qquad\langle r^2,sr\rangle
$$

are Klein four groups and require two generators. The rotation subgroup and all smaller cyclic subgroups require at most one, and $D_8$ itself is nonabelian. Its subgroup lattice therefore shows $m(D_8)=2$ and these are exactly the attaining abelian subgroups.

**D₁₆.** Use $|r|=8$, $s^2=1$, $srs=r^{-1}$. An abelian subgroup contained in $\langle r\rangle$ is cyclic. If an abelian subgroup contains a reflection $sr^j$, any rotation $r^k$ in it must satisfy

$$
sr^j r^k=r^k sr^j,
$$

which is equivalent to $r^k=r^{-k}$, so $k\equiv0$ or $4\pmod8$. Two reflections $sr^i,sr^j$ commute exactly when $2(i-j)\equiv0\pmod8$, so their indices differ by $0$ or $4$. Hence any noncyclic abelian subgroup with a reflection is exactly one of

$$
\langle r^4,sr^j\rangle\quad(j=0,1,2,3).
$$

Each is $V_4$ and needs two generators. These four, and no others, attain $m(D_{16})=2$.

**QD₁₆.** The relation gives $\tau\sigma\tau=\sigma^3$. Every element outside $\langle\sigma\rangle$ is $\tau\sigma^i$, and

$$
(\tau\sigma^i)^2=\sigma^{4i}.
$$

If $i$ is even this is an involution; if $i$ is odd it has order $4$ and its square is $\sigma^4$. A rotation $\sigma^k$ commutes with $\tau\sigma^i$ exactly when $2k\equiv0\pmod8$, so $k\equiv0,4$. For two outside elements, their products have exponents $3i+j$ and $3j+i$, respectively. They commute exactly when $i-j\equiv0\pmod4$.

It follows that an abelian subgroup containing an outside element lies in $\langle\sigma^4,\tau\sigma^i\rangle$: its rotations are $1,\sigma^4$, and its other outside elements can only be $\tau\sigma^i$ and $\tau\sigma^{i+4}$. For odd $i$ this group is cyclic of order $4$, because $(\tau\sigma^i)^2=\sigma^4$. For even $i$ it is $V_4$. Thus the only abelian subgroups needing two generators are

$$
\langle\sigma^4,\tau\rangle,\qquad\langle\sigma^4,\tau\sigma^2\rangle,
$$

and $m(QD_{16})=2$.

**Technique:** an invariant definition gives characteristicity. For the explicit lists, separate cyclic rotation subgroups from subgroups containing an outside element, then calculate exactly when generators commute.
