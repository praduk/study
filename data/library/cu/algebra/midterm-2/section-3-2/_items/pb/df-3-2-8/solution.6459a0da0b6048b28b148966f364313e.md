The intersection is a subgroup of both $H$ and $K$. By Lagrange, $|H\cap K|$ divides $|H|$ and also $|K|$. The only positive common divisor is $1$, so $|H\cap K|=1$. A subgroup of order $1$ consists only of the identity, proving the result.

**Check the converse.** Trivial intersection does not force coprime orders: distinct order-$2$ subgroups of $C_2\times C_2$ intersect trivially. The statement is a sufficient criterion, not an equivalence.

**Technique:** the same subgroup supplies a divisor of two different orders.
