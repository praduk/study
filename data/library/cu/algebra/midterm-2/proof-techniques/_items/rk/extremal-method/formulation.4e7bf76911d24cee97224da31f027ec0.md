For a minimal nontrivial normal subgroup $N$, first prove the subgroup you have constructed is normal in the entire ambient group and lies in $N$. Only then may you conclude it is $1$ or $N$.

For a maximal proper subgroup $M$, first exhibit an intermediate subgroup containing $M$. Its only possible values are $M$ and $G$. Examples are $N_G(M)$ in 4.3.23 and the setwise stabilizer of a block in 4.1.7.

To show a complement is maximal, take an arbitrary overgroup $K>H$, produce a nonidentity element of $N\cap K$ by writing $k=nh$, and reuse the minimal-normal-subgroup argument.

**Common mistake:** normality in $N$ is weaker than normality in $G$. In the additional problem, abelianness of $N$ and the factorization $G=NH$ together supply the missing ambient normality.
