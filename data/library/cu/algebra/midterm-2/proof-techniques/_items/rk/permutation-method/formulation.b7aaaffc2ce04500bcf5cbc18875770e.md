Permutation products act right to left. Conjugation relabels cycles:

$$
\tau(a_1\ \cdots\ a_k)\tau^{-1}=(\tau(a_1)\ \cdots\ \tau(a_k)).
$$

To prove this, evaluate both sides at each $\tau(a_i)$; both send it to $\tau(a_{i+1})$, with indices cyclic, and fix the remaining points.

Generation problems often start with one transposition and conjugate it to obtain adjacent or star transpositions. For $A_n$, group an even-length transposition expression into pairs. Distinguish identical pairs, pairs sharing one point, and disjoint pairs.

For automorphisms, orders alone may not distinguish transpositions from double or triple transpositions. Class size and intersections of supports supply the extra constraints in 4.4.18.

**Common mistake:** “even permutation” concerns its sign, not whether its element order is even. A double transposition is even and has order $2$.
