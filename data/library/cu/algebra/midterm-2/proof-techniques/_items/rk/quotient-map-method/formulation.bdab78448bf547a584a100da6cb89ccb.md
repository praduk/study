When the target resembles a quotient, begin with a homomorphism from the original group. State its domain and codomain, prove it preserves products, solve the kernel equation, and identify its image. Then invoke the First Isomorphism Theorem.

For a map out of a quotient, verify that changing the representative does not change the image. Equivalently, when starting with $f:G\to H$, check the proposed denominator is contained in $\ker f$.

Examples: the triangular-matrix projections in 3.1.11; the map $g\mapsto(gM,gN)$ in 3.3.7; and the sign of the regular representation in 4.2.12.

**Common mistake:** two surjective coordinate maps do not necessarily make a map onto their product. In 3.3.7, $G=MN$ is the hypothesis proving joint surjectivity.
