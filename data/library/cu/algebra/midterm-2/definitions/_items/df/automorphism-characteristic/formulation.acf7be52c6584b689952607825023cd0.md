An automorphism of $G$ is an isomorphism $G\to G$. The group $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ uses composition as multiplication. For $g\in G$, $c_g(x)=gxg^{-1}$ is an inner automorphism; the subgroup of all these is $\operatorname{Inn}(G)$.

A subgroup $H\le G$ is characteristic if $\alpha(H)=H$ for every $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$. A normal subgroup only requires this for inner automorphisms. Therefore characteristic implies normal, but not conversely: any order-$2$ subgroup of $V_4$ is normal, while automorphisms of $V_4$ permute its three such subgroups.

Characteristicity is transitive. Also $H$ characteristic in $N$ and $N$ normal in $G$ imply $H$ normal in $G$, because conjugation by any $g\in G$ restricts to an automorphism of $N$. Replacing the first characteristicity assumption by normality can fail.
