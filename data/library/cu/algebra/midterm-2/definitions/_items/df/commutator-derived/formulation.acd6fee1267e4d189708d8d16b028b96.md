Use the textbook convention

$$
[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy.
$$

For subsets $A,B\subseteq G$, $[A,B]$ is the subgroup generated by all $[a,b]$ with $a\in A,b\in B$. In particular $G'=[G,G]$ is the commutator or derived subgroup; define $G^{(0)}=G$ and $G^{(j+1)}=(G^{(j)})'$.

A group is abelian if and only if $G'=1$. A group is perfect if and only if $G'=G$. The trivial group is both abelian and perfect. A nontrivial abelian group is not perfect.

The lecture uses generators $xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$ for its commutator subgroup. The generated subgroup is the same: the lecture expression equals the textbook $[x^{-1},y^{-1}]$, and inverses also range over all group elements. Preserve the textbook convention for individual commutator calculations; equality of generated subgroups does not mean the two individual expressions are equal.
