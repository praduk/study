For a subset $S\subseteq G$,

$$
C_G(S)=\{g\in G:gs=sg\text{ for all }s\in S\},\qquad N_G(S)=\{g\in G:gSg^{-1}=S\}.
$$

For a single element $x$, write $C_G(x)=C_G(\{x\})$. The center is $Z(G)=C_G(G)$. These are subgroups. Centralizing is pointwise commutation, whereas normalizing requires preservation of the set and may permute its elements.

For $H\le G$, $H\le N_G(H)$, and $H\nsubg G$ precisely when $N_G(H)=G$. A subgroup is self-normalizing if $N_G(H)=H$.

Example: in $D_8$, $\langle r\rangle$ is normal, but $s$ does not centralize $r$. Thus normalization does not imply centralization.
