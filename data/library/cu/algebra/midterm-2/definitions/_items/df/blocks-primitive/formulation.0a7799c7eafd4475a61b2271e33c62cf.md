For a transitive action on a nonempty set $A$, a block is a nonempty $B\subseteq A$ such that for every $g\in G$, $gB=B$ or $gB\cap B=\varnothing$. The action is primitive if its only blocks are singletons and $A$.

For $|A|>1$, a transitive action is primitive exactly when every point stabilizer is maximal. The proof is in the assigned Exercise 4.1.7. The condition $|A|>1$ is necessary because on a singleton the point stabilizer is the whole group and is not a maximal proper subgroup.

In the natural action of $S_4$, no subset of size $2$ or $3$ is a block. In the square action of $D_8$, opposite pairs are blocks. “Block” concerns every translate, not just invariance under one generator.
