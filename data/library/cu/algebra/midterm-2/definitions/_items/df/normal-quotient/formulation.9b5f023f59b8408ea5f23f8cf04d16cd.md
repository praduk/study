For $N\le G$, write $N\nsubg G$ if $gNg^{-1}=N$ for every $g\in G$. Equivalently $gN=Ng$ for every $g$. The quotient group $G/N$ consists of cosets $gN$ with product $(gN)(hN)=ghN$, identity $N$, and inverse $g^{-1}N$. Normality is exactly what makes this product independent of representatives.

For $H\le G$, the index $[G:H]$ is the number of left cosets; $H$ need not be normal for its index to exist. If $G$ is finite, $[G:H]=|G|/|H|$.

The order of $gN$ is the least positive integer $k$ such that $g^k\in N$, and is infinite if no such $k$ exists, because $(gN)^k=g^kN$.

Example: in $C_8=\langle x\rangle$, the element $x\langle x^4\rangle$ has order $4$ although $x$ has order $8$. Nonexample: a reflection subgroup of $D_8$ is not normal, so its left cosets form a set for an action but do not form a quotient group under the displayed rule.
