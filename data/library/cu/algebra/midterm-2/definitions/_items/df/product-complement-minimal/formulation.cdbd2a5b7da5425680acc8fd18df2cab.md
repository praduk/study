The external direct product $H\times K$ consists of pairs with $(h,k)(h',k')=(hh',kk')$. Its two coordinate subgroups commute elementwise. For internal subgroups $H,K\le G$, one has $G\cong H\times K$ through $(h,k)\mapsto hk$ if $G=HK$, $H\cap K=1$, and both are normal in $G$.

For $N\nsubg G$, a complement is a subgroup $H$ with $G=NH$ and $N\cap H=1$. A complement need not be normal, so these conditions alone do not give a direct product.

A maximal subgroup $H$ is proper and has no strict intermediate subgroup $H<K<G$. A minimal nontrivial normal subgroup $N$ is nontrivial, normal, and has no $1<L<N$ normal in $G$. This does not mean $N$ has no proper nontrivial subgroups; it only restricts those normal in the ambient group.

Example: $\langle r\rangle$ and $\langle s\rangle$ in $D_8$ have trivial intersection and product $D_8$, but $srs=r^{-1}\ne r$, so this is not $C_4\times C_2$.
