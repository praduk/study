A group $G$ is solvable if there exists a finite chain

$$
1=G_0\nsubg G_1\nsubg\cdots\nsubg G_t=G
$$

with every quotient $G_{i+1}/G_i$ abelian. These factors need not be simple. Equivalently, the derived sequence reaches $1$ after finitely many steps; this equivalence is proved in the theorem collection.

Every abelian group is solvable, using $1\nsubg G$, and $A_4$ is solvable using $1<C_2<V_4<A_4$. A nonabelian perfect group is not solvable, since its derived sequence never decreases. In particular nonabelian simple groups are not solvable.

The definition is not restricted to finite groups. Finiteness is required for the additional composition-factor characterization discussed in the book.
