The subgroup lattice of $G$ is its subgroups ordered by inclusion. The meet of $H,K$ is $H\cap K$; their join is $\langle H,K\rangle$, the smallest subgroup containing both. The join need not equal the set $HK=\{hk:h\in H,k\in K\}$.

A Hasse diagram places larger subgroups higher. Connect $K$ to $H$ exactly when $K<H$ and no subgroup lies strictly between them; this is a cover relation. Transitive inclusions are read along paths and are not drawn as additional edges.

A partial lattice diagram may display only selected subgroups; a line then need not be a cover. An isomorphism of subgroup lattices preserves inclusion, meets, and joins, but it need not determine the group up to isomorphism: the assigned modular-group example illustrates this distinction.
