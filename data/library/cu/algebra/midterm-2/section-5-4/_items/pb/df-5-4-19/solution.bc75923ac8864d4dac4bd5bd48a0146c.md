(a) If $G$ is simple, $[G,G]\in \set{\id_G, G}$.  But $[G,G]\neq \id_G$ because $G$ is not abelian, so $[G,G]=G$.

(b) Let $\set{H_i}_{i\in I}$ be a collection of perfect groups and $\genby{H_i}=G$.  We have $H_i =[H_i,H_i]\subg [G,G]$.  So $\genby{H_i: i\in I} \subg [G,G]$.  But $[G,G]\subg G$ so $[G,G]=G$ and $G$ perfect.

(c) Conjugation of a subgroup $H$ is an isomorphism.  It is then sufficient to show if one group is perfect in an isomorphism of groups, both are perfect.

(d) Let $P=\genby{P_j}$, $P_j$ perfect.  Conugation of a perfect subgroup is perfect.
