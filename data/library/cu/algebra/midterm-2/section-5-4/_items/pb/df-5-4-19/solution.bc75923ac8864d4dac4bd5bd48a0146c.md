**(a) Simplicity forces the commutator subgroup to be everything.** The commutator subgroup $H'$ is normal in $H$, so simplicity gives $H'=1$ or $H'=H$. If $H'=1$, every commutator is the identity, so every pair of elements commutes and $H$ is abelian. This contradicts the nonabelian hypothesis. Therefore $H'=H$.

**(b) Generation preserves perfectness.** Let $L=\langle H,K\rangle$. Commutators of elements of $H$ are also commutators of elements of $L$, so $H'\le L'$. Since $H'=H$, we have $H\le L'$. Likewise $K\le L'$. A subgroup containing both $H$ and $K$ contains the subgroup they generate, so $L\le L'$. The reverse inclusion $L'\le L$ is automatic, since commutators of elements of $L$ are elements of $L$. Thus $L'=L$.

For a family $(H_i)_{i\in I}$ of perfect subgroups, the same reasoning gives $H_i=H_i'\le L'$ for every $i$, where $L=\langle H_i:i\in I\rangle$. Thus $L\le L'$ and equality follows. The empty family generates $1$, which is also perfect, so no exception is needed.

**(c) Conjugation carries derived subgroups to derived subgroups.** For $g\in G$,

$$
[gh_1g^{-1},gh_2g^{-1}]=g[h_1,h_2]g^{-1}.
$$

Taking the subgroups generated by both sides gives $(gHg^{-1})'=gH'g^{-1}$. If $H'=H$, this becomes $(gHg^{-1})'=gHg^{-1}$, proving the conjugate perfect.

**(d) Generate from all perfect subgroups.** Let $P$ be the subgroup generated by every perfect subgroup of $G$. This is a set of subgroups, and it includes $1$. Part (b) shows $P$ itself is perfect. By construction every perfect subgroup is contained in $P$. Thus $P$ is a greatest perfect subgroup, a stronger statement than maximality. If $Q$ were any maximal perfect subgroup, then $Q\le P$ and $P$ is perfect, so maximality would force $Q=P$. This proves uniqueness.

For $g\in G$, part (c) says conjugation permutes all perfect subgroups. Consequently it preserves their generated subgroup, so $gPg^{-1}=P$. Hence $P\nsubg G$. Indeed every automorphism preserves perfectness by the same commutator calculation, so $P$ is characteristic as well.

**Technique:** commutator subgroups are monotone under subgroup inclusion. Generating all subgroups with a property can provide a greatest subgroup, provided that property survives generation.
