Let $t=(1\ 2)$, $c=(1\ 2\ \cdots\ n)$, and $L=\langle t,c\rangle$. Conjugating a transposition just relabels its two points, so for $0\le j\le n-2$,

$$
c^jtc^{-j}=(j+1\ \ j+2).
$$

Thus $L$ contains every adjacent transposition.

**Why adjacent transpositions suffice.** Let $s_i=(i\ i+1)$. For $i<j$, the permutation $w=s_i s_{i+1}\cdots s_{j-2}$ fixes $j$ and sends $j-1$ to $i$ (products act right to left). Hence

$$
ws_{j-1}w^{-1}=(i\ j).
$$

So every transposition belongs to the subgroup generated by adjacent transpositions. Every cycle is a product of transpositions:

$$
(a_1\ a_2\ \cdots\ a_k)=(a_1\ a_k)(a_1\ a_{k-1})\cdots(a_1\ a_2).
$$

Disjoint-cycle decomposition therefore writes every permutation as a product of transpositions. Consequently $L$ contains all of $S_n$. Since $L\le S_n$ by construction, equality follows. For $n=2$, $t=c$ already generates $S_2$.

**Technique:** conjugate one simple generator to obtain a familiar generating family, then explicitly show that family generates.
