The identity always forms a singleton conjugacy class. If there are exactly two classes, all nonidentity elements form one class. Write $n=|G|$; then $n\ge2$, and for any $x\ne1$,

$$
n-1=|\operatorname{Cl}_G(x)|=[G:C_G(x)].
$$

A subgroup index divides the order of a finite group, so $n-1$ divides $n$. It therefore divides $n-(n-1)=1$. Since $n-1$ is positive, $n-1=1$ and $n=2$. A group of order $2$ is cyclic, hence $G\cong C_2$.

Conversely $C_2$ is abelian and has two elements, each of which is its own conjugacy class. It has exactly two classes. The trivial group has only one class and is excluded.

**Technique:** one large orbit produces a divisor only one smaller than the whole group order.
