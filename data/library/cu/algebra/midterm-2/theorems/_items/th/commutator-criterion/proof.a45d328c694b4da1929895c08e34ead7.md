For any $x,y\in G$, the cosets $xN,yN$ commute exactly when $(xN)^{-1}(yN)^{-1}(xN)(yN)=N$, equivalently $[x,y]\in N$. Thus all quotient cosets commute exactly when all generating commutators of $G'$ lie in $N$, equivalently $G'\le N$.

For $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$,

$$
\alpha([x,y])=[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)].
$$

Since $\alpha$ is bijective, it permutes the set of all commutators. It therefore preserves the subgroup they generate, proving $G'$ characteristic.

If $N\nsubg G$, conjugation by any $g\in G$ restricts to an automorphism of $N$. Applying the preceding argument inside $N$ shows that conjugation preserves $N'$. Hence $N'\nsubg G$. This last conclusion can also be checked directly on commutator generators, as in the solution of 3.4.11.
