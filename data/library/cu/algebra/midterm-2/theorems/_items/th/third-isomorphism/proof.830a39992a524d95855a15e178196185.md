Define $f:G/H\to G/K$ by $f(gH)=gK$. If $gH=g'H$, then $g'^{-1}g\in H\le K$, so $gK=g'K$. This verifies representative independence.

The map is a homomorphism because $f(ghH)=ghK=(gK)(hK)$. It is surjective because every $gK$ is the image of $gH$. Its kernel consists of cosets $gH$ with $g\in K$, exactly $K/H$. Being a kernel, $K/H$ is normal in $G/H$. The First Isomorphism Theorem applied to $f$ produces the stated quotient-of-a-quotient isomorphism.

**Working rule:** define the obvious map and check its kernel; avoid treating quotient notation as literal fraction cancellation.
