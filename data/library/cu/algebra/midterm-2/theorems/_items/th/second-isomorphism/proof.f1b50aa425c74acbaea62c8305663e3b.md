The product theorem gives $HK\le G$. An element $hk\in HK$ normalizes $K$, because $h$ does by hypothesis and $k$ does because it is in $K$. Thus $K\nsubg HK$.

Define $f:H\to HK/K$ by $f(h)=hK$. This is a homomorphism as a restriction of the quotient projection. Every coset in $HK/K$ is $(hk)K=hK$, so $f$ is surjective. Its kernel is the set of $h\in H$ lying in $K$, namely $H\cap K$. Kernels are normal, so $H\cap K\nsubg H$. The First Isomorphism Theorem gives the desired isomorphism.

The diamond has top $HK$, middle vertices $H,K$, and bottom $H\cap K$. It summarizes the two sides identified by the map, but does not assert the displayed inclusions are covers.
