Choose $g,h$ with $gh\ne hg$. At $a=1$,

$$
(gh)\cdot1=gh,\qquad g\cdot(h\cdot1)=hg.
$$

Thus the compatibility axiom fails. The identity axiom does hold, but both axioms are necessary. The same formula is a valid right action when written $a\cdot g=ag$.

**Technique:** disprove a universal law with one witness; use the identity to expose the order of multiplication.
