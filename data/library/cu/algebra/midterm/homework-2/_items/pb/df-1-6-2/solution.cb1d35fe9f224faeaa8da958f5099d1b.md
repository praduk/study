For every positive integer $k$, $\varphi(x)^k=\varphi(x^k)$. By injectivity, this is $1$ exactly when $x^k=1$. The sets of positive identity exponents coincide, so the orders coincide, including infinite order. The bijection $\varphi$ therefore restricts to a bijection between the elements of any specified order.
For a homomorphism alone, order may decrease: the trivial map from $C_2$ to the trivial group sends an element of order two to one of order one. In general a finite order image has order dividing $|x|$ when $|x|$ is finite.

**Technique:** compare identity powers in both directions; identify exactly where injectivity enters.
