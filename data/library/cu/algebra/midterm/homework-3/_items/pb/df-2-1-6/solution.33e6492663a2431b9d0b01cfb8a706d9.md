The identity belongs to $T$. If $x^m=y^n=1$, commutativity gives $(xy^{-1})^{mn}=x^{mn}y^{-mn}=1$, so $xy^{-1}\in T$. The subgroup criterion proves the claim.
For a counterexample, use the group of bijections of $\Z$. The maps $a(t)=-t$ and $b(t)=1-t$ have order two, while $(ab)(t)=t-1$ has infinite order because $(ab)^m(t)=t-m$ for positive $m$. Thus finite-order elements are not closed under products. These two maps also generate a nonabelian group, since $ba(t)=t+1\ne ab(t)$.

**Technique:** combine finite orders using a common multiple; expose a missing commutativity hypothesis using two involutions with infinite-order product.
