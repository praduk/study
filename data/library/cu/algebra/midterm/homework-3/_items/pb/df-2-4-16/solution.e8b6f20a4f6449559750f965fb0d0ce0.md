(a) Among the proper subgroups of $G$ containing $H$, choose one, $M$, of largest cardinality. Such a choice exists because the collection is nonempty (it includes $H$) and finite. If $M<K<G$, then $|K|>|M|$, a contradiction. Hence $M$ is maximal.

(b) In $D_{2n}$ the rotation subgroup $R=\langle r\rangle$ is proper. If $R<K\le D_{2n}$, choose an element $sr^i\in K\setminus R$. Since $r^{-i}\in K$, we have $s=(sr^i)r^{-i}\in K$. Thus $K$ contains $r,s$ and equals $D_{2n}$. Equivalently, $R$ has index two and there is no intermediate subgroup.

**Technique:** maximize cardinality in a finite collection; prove maximality by adjoining any element outside the subgroup.
