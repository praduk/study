If one is contained in the other, the union equals the larger subgroup.
Conversely, suppose the union is a subgroup but neither containment holds. Choose $h\in H\setminus K$ and $k\in K\setminus H$. Closure puts $hk$ in $H$ or $K$. If $hk\in H$, then $k=h^{-1}(hk)\in H$, contradiction. If $hk\in K$, then $h=(hk)k^{-1}\in K$, contradiction.

**Technique:** choose witnesses outside each set and force their product into an impossible case. In particular a group cannot be the union of two proper subgroups.
