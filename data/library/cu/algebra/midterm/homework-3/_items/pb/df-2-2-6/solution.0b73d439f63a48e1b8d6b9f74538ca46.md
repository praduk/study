(a) If $h\in H$, closure gives $hHh^{-1}\subseteq H$. Conversely $u=h(h^{-1}uh)h^{-1}$ for $u\in H$, and $h^{-1}uh\in H$, so $H\subseteq hHh^{-1}$. Hence $h$ normalizes $H$.

For a counterexample take $r,s\in D_6$.  $r$ and $s$ don't commute so $N_G(\set{r,s})$ does not contain $r$ or $s$.

(b) The inclusion means that each element of $H$ commutes with every element of $H$, exactly the definition of abelian.

**Technique:** prove set equality by two containments; expand the quantifiers in centralizer definitions.
