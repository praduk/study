A subset $H\subseteq G$ is a **subgroup**, written $H\le G$, exactly when it is nonempty and closed under multiplication and inverses, with the operation inherited from $G$. It is **proper**, written $H<G$, if additionally $H\ne G$.
Both $\{1\}$ and $G$ are subgroups. The identity and inverses in $H$ agree with those in $G$. A subset that is a group under an unrelated operation need not be a subgroup.
Example: $2\Z\le(\Z,+)$; positive integers are closed under addition but are not a subgroup.
