**Use when:** there are very few maximal subgroups or a union is claimed to be a subgroup.

For a union, assume neither containment and choose $h\in H\setminus K$, $k\in K\setminus H$. Force $hk$ into one side and cancel to contradict the choices.
For a maximal subgroup, assume $M<K\le G$ and prove $K=G$. A useful way is to take any $x\in K\setminus M$ and show $\langle M,x\rangle=G$.
For the additional problem, argue contrapositively: noncyclic means every $\langle g\rangle$ is proper; finiteness puts it inside a maximal subgroup; hence maximal subgroups cover $G$; one or two proper subgroups cannot cover a group.

**Assigned uses:** 2.1.8; 2.4.16(a,b); additional maximal-subgroup problem.

**Common failure:** maximal is an inclusion condition. Do not assume all maximal subgroups have the same size or that a maximal subgroup contains every smaller subgroup.
