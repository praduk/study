**Use when:** an element order, cyclic generator, or power-map question appears.

Prove both that a proposed power gives $1$ and that no smaller positive power does. Use $x^m=1\iff|x|\mid m$ for finite order; use $|x^k|=|x|/\gcd(|x|,k)$ for powers. For commuting finite-order elements, a common multiple gives an upper bound on their product order, not always equality. For disjoint cycles or direct-product coordinates, independent identity conditions give a least common multiple.

Use Bézout $ku+nv=1$ when $\gcd(k,n)=1$: $g^u$ is a $k$th root if $g^n=1$.

**Assigned uses:** 1.3.6, 10, 15, 18; 1.5.1; 1.6.2; 2.1.6; 2.3.25.

**Common failure:** Lagrange gives necessary divisibility, not an existence theorem for elements of every divisor order. A bijective power map need not preserve multiplication.
