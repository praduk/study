**Use when:** a problem asks whether a weakened assertion is true or asks for a nonexample.

Choose the smallest transparent construction that breaks the exact required property, then verify every remaining hypothesis. Useful examples here:

- $S_3$ for noncommutativity and noncommuting transpositions.
- $s,sr\in D_{2n}$ for two involutions whose product has order $n$.
- The bijections $t\mapsto-t$ and $t\mapsto1-t$ of $\Z$ for two finite-order elements with infinite-order product.
- A trivial homomorphism for loss of order or lack of surjectivity.
- Dyadic rationals for a proper noncyclic additive subgroup of $\Q$.
- $H=\{1\}$ inside a nonabelian $G$ for a nonabelian centralizer of an abelian subgroup.

**Assigned uses:** 1.6.2, 3; 1.7.14; 2.1.6, 14; 2.2.6(a); 2.4.3, 15.

**Common failure:** a counterexample must obey the stated ambient group and operation; a nonclosed set is not itself a group counterexample unless it is being tested as a subset.
