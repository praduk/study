**Use when:** a group acts on points, subsets, tuples, polynomials, or itself.

To prove **faithfulness**, show every nonidentity group element moves at least one object; the object may depend on that element. To disprove it, find one nonidentity element fixing every object.
For **transitivity**, given two objects, construct a group element taking one to the other. This is a different quantifier pattern. For a **pointwise stabilizer**, fix every point individually; for a **setwise stabilizer**, preserve the subset as a whole.

In 1.7.10(a), a moved point $i\mapsto j$ suggests choosing a subset containing $i$ but not $j$. This works only for $k<n$. For tuples, a moved first coordinate is enough.

**Assigned uses:** 1.7.8, 10, 14, 15, 18; 2.2.6, 10, 12.

**Common failure:** a nonidentity element may fix many points in a faithful action. Faithfulness rules out fixing **all** points.
