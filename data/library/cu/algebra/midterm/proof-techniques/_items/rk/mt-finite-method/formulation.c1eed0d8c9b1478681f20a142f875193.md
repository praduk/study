**Use when:** an injective self-map, repeated powers, or maximal subgroup is involved.

A self-map of a finite set is injective iff it is surjective. This is the key step in 1.6.23: prove $x\mapsto x^{-1}\alpha(x)$ injective, then represent every element this way. In a finite nonempty collection of proper subgroups, choose one of largest cardinality to obtain a maximal subgroup containing a specified proper subgroup.

**Assigned uses:** 1.4.5; 1.6.23; 2.4.12, 16(a); the additional maximal-subgroup problem.

**Common failure:** on an infinite set, injectivity need not imply surjectivity (for example $m\mapsto2m$ on $\Z$). Do not drop the finite hypothesis from the argument.
