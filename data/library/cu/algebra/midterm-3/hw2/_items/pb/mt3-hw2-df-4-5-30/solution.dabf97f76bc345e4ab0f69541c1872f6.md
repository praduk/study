**Definitions and results used:** @[Sylow counting and prime-order intersection counting]mt3-sylow; @[simple groups and Lagrange’s theorem]mt3-foundations.

**Technique:** Count prime-order Sylow subgroups, then multiply by p−1; justify disjointness.

Since $168=2^3\cdot3\cdot7$, Sylow gives $n_7\mid24$ and $n_7\equiv1\pmod7$, hence $n_7=1$ or $8$. Simplicity excludes $1$, so $n_7=8$. Each Sylow $7$-subgroup is cyclic of prime order and contributes six nonidentity elements. Distinct such subgroups intersect trivially. Therefore there are $8\cdot6=\boxed{48}$ elements of order $7$.
