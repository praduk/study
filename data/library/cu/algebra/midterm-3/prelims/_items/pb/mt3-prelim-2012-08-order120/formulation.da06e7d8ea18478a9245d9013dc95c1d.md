The original problem asks you to prove that no group of order $120$ is simple. The following four-part scaffold is an adaptation designed to practice the corollary of Sylow $(N)^+$ in part (b); it is not the wording or structure of the original prelim.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $G$ is a simple group of order $120$.

**(a)** Determine the possible Sylow $2$-numbers. Use actions on Sylow subgroups to prove that $n_2=15$. Explain why a faithful permutation action of $G$ has image in the alternating group.

**(b)** Use the corollary of Sylow $(N)^+$ to find distinct Sylow $2$-subgroups $P,Q$ whose intersection $D$ has order $4$.

**(c)** Prove that $P,Q\le N_G(D)$ and that $|N_G(D)|$ is $24$, $40$, or $120$.

**(d)** Obtain a contradiction in each case from (c), using normality or the action on cosets of $N_G(D)$. Conclude that no simple group of order $120$ exists.

Source: [CU Boulder Algebra prelim, August 2012](https://math.colorado.edu/documents/graduate/prelim/Algebra_Aug_2012.pdf), Problem 1, PDF page 1. The date follows the official archive; the PDF itself is undated.
