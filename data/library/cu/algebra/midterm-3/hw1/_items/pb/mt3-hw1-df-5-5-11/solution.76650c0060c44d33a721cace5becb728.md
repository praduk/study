**Definitions and results used:** @[Sylow counts and uniqueness iff normal]mt3-sylow; @[Groups of order p² are abelian]mt3-pgroups; @[Abelian groups of order 4 and coprime cyclic products]mt3-abelian; @[Internal semidirect recognition and automorphisms of cyclic groups]mt3-semidirect; @[Equivalent actions and distinguishing semidirect products by invariants]mt3-classify.

**Technique:** For order 4p, establish normal C_p and classify maps C₄ or C₂² → Aut(C_p).

Since $n_7\mid4$ and $n_7\equiv1\pmod7$, the Sylow $7$-subgroup $A\cong C_7$ is normal. A Sylow $2$-subgroup $B$ has order $4$, so it is $C_4$ or $C_2^2$, and $G=A\semid B$.

Since $\Aut(C_7)\cong C_6$, an action of $B$ has trivial image or its unique order-$2$ subgroup, generated by inversion.

- $B=C_4$, trivial action: $C_{28}$.
- $B=C_4$, inversion action:

$$
\langle a,b\mid a^7=b^4=1,\ bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle.
$$

- $B=C_2^2$, trivial action: $C_{14}\times C_2$.
- $B=C_2^2$, nontrivial action: choose generators $b,c$ so $b$ inverts $a$ and $c$ centralizes it; this gives $D_{14}\times C_2$ (also isomorphic to $D_{28}$). Here $D_{2n}$ means a dihedral group of order $2n$.

For $C_4$ there is only one nontrivial homomorphism into $C_6$. For $C_2^2$, all three nontrivial maps have order-$2$ kernels, and changes of basis permute those kernels; hence they give one type. The four groups are distinct: abelianity separates the trivial from nontrivial actions, and the Sylow $2$-subgroup type separates the two groups within each pair.
