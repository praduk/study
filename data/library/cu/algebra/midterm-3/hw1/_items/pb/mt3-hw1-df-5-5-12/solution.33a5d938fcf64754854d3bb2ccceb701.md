**Definitions and results used:** @[Sylow counts and uniqueness iff normal]mt3-sylow; @[Groups of order p² are abelian]mt3-pgroups; @[Abelian groups of order 4 and coprime cyclic products]mt3-abelian; @[Internal semidirect recognition, cyclic automorphisms, and action presentations]mt3-semidirect; @[Action equivalence and center/Sylow-type invariants]mt3-classify.

**Technique:** Classify the image orders of the action, then distinguish the two nonabelian C₄ cases by their centers.

The Sylow count $n_5\mid4$, $n_5\equiv1\pmod5$ forces $A\cong C_5$ normal. A Sylow $2$-subgroup $B$ is $C_4$ or $C_2^2$, and $G=A\semid B$.

Here $\Aut(C_5)\cong C_4$, generated by $a\mapsto a^2$.

For $B=\langle b\rangle\cong C_4$, the three action types are represented by

$$
G_r=\langle a,b\mid a^5=b^4=1,\ bab^{-1}=a^r\rangle,\qquad r=1,4,2.
$$

They give $C_{20}$, the inversion product $C_5\semid C_4$, and the faithful product $C_5\semid C_4$, respectively. The fourth residue $r=3$ is inverse to $2$ modulo $5$ and gives the same type by $b\mapsto b^{-1}$.

For $B=C_2^2$, the image has order $1$ or $2$. The trivial action gives $C_{10}\times C_2$. All nontrivial maps are equivalent under changes of basis in $B$ and give $D_{10}\times C_2\cong D_{20}$, with dihedral subscripts denoting group orders.

These five types are distinct. Sylow $2$-subgroup type separates the $C_4$ cases from the $C_2^2$ cases. Abelianity separates trivial from nontrivial actions. Finally the inversion $C_4$ case has center $\langle b^2\rangle$ of order $2$, while the faithful case has trivial center: centrality of $a^i b^j$ requires $r^j=1$ and $(r-1)i=0$ modulo $5$.
