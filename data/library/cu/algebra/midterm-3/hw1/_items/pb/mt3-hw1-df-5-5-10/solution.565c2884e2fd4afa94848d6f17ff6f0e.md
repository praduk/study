**Definitions and results used:** @[Sylow theorem]cu:algebra:midterm-3:sylow:th:mt3-sylow; @[Unique, normal, and characteristic Sylow subgroups]cu:algebra:midterm-3:sylow:th:mt3-sylow-normality; @[Groups of order p² are abelian]cu:algebra:midterm-3:sylow:th:mt3-p-square-abelian; @[Fundamental theorem of finite abelian groups]cu:algebra:midterm-3:foundations:th:mt3-abelian-classification; @[Semidirect product construction and internal recognition]cu:algebra:midterm-3:semidirect:th:mt3-semidirect-recognition; @[Actions of one finite cyclic group on another]cu:algebra:midterm-3:semidirect:th:mt3-cyclic-actions; @[Automorphisms and order of an elementary abelian group]cu:algebra:midterm-3:foundations:th:mt3-linear-automorphisms; @[General linear group and change of basis]cu:algebra:midterm-3:foundations:df:mt3-general-linear; @[Vector spaces, bases, and linear maps over F_p]cu:algebra:midterm-3:foundations:df:mt3-linear.

**Technique:** Normal Sylow subgroup → internal semidirect product → classify actions up to change of basis and change of complement generator → distinguish resulting groups by invariants.

Write $C_m$ for a cyclic group of order $m$.

**(a)** The Sylow factors of an abelian group give exactly $C_{49}\times C_3\cong C_{147}$ and $C_7\times C_7\times C_3$.

**(b)** $n_7\mid3$ and $n_7\equiv1\pmod7$ force $n_7=1$. Let $A$ be this normal subgroup and $\langle z\rangle$ a Sylow $3$-subgroup. Since their intersection is trivial and their orders multiply to $147$, $G=A\semid\langle z\rangle$.

**(c)** If $A=\langle a\rangle\cong C_{49}$, the action is $zaz^{-1}=a^u$ with $u^3\equiv1\pmod{49}$. The roots are $1,18,30$: reduce modulo $7$ to $1,2,4$ and substitute $u=v+7k$ to find the unique lift of each root. The nontrivial roots satisfy $18^2\equiv30\pmod{49}$, so replacing $z$ by $z^{-1}$ identifies the two nontrivial actions. Thus the unique nonabelian type with cyclic $A$ is

$$
\langle a,z\mid a^{49}=z^3=1,\ zaz^{-1}=a^{18}\rangle.
$$

**(d)** The diagonal matrices $t_1,t_2$ commute, each has order $3$, and their cyclic subgroups meet trivially. Hence $T\cong C_3^2$ has order $9$. Also

$$
|\GL_2(\F_7)|=(7^2-1)(7^2-7)=2016=2^5\cdot3^2\cdot7,
$$

so $T$ is Sylow. Every order-$3$ subgroup is contained in some Sylow $3$-subgroup, which is conjugate to $T$.

**(e)** Put $A=\langle x,y\mid x^7=y^7=1,\ xy=yx\rangle$. For each $i$, impose $z^3=1$ and the indicated conjugation relations:

- $G_1$: $zxz^{-1}=x^2$, $zyz^{-1}=y$.
- $G_2$: $zxz^{-1}=x$, $zyz^{-1}=y^2$.
- $G_3$: $zxz^{-1}=x^2$, $zyz^{-1}=y^2$.
- $G_4$: $zxz^{-1}=x^2$, $zyz^{-1}=y^4$.

Interchanging $x,y$ gives $G_1\cong G_2$.

**(f)** In each nontrivial action, no element $a z^j$ with $j\not\equiv0\pmod3$ centralizes all of $A$, since its conjugation on $A$ is the nonidentity matrix $t^j$. Thus the center is exactly the fixed subspace of the action inside $A$. It is $\langle y\rangle$ in $G_1$ and trivial in $G_3,G_4$.

**(g)** All order-$7$ subgroups lie in $A$. They are the one-dimensional subspaces of $\F_7^2$. The scalar matrix $2I$ preserves every line, so all are normal in $G_3$. In $G_4$, the line generated by $(1,1)$ is sent to the line generated by $(2,4)$, which is different; equivalently $\langle xy\rangle$ is not normal. Therefore $G_3\not\cong G_4$.

**(h)** A group of order $49$ is abelian, so $A$ is cyclic or elementary abelian. The cyclic case is covered by (a),(c). For $A\cong C_7^2$, a trivial action gives the second abelian type. A nontrivial action has order-$3$ image in $\GL_2(\F_7)$. By (d), change of basis puts that image in $T$. The four order-$3$ subgroups of $T$ give the four displayed actions; either generator of any such subgroup yields isomorphic products by replacing $z$ with $z^{-1}$. Parts (e)–(g) leave exactly three nonabelian types with elementary abelian $A$. Cyclic versus elementary abelian Sylow $7$-subgroups separates these from (c). Total: **two abelian and four nonabelian types**.
