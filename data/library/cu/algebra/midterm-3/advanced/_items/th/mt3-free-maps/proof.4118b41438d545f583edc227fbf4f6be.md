**Definitions and result used:** @mt3-free, @mt3-presentations, and @mt3-factor-quotient.

Given $f:S\to G$, evaluate a word by replacing each letter $s^{\pm1}$ with $f(s)^{\pm1}$ and multiplying. Cancelling adjacent inverse letters does not change its value, so this gives a well-defined homomorphism on reduced words. Any extension must have this value on every word, proving uniqueness.

A homomorphism factors through a quotient exactly when its kernel contains the quotient's defining normal subgroup. Its kernel is normal, so containing every relator is equivalent to containing their normal closure. This proves both directions of the factorization criterion.
