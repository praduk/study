**Definitions and results used:** @[Sylow number congruence and divisibility]mt3-sylow; @[The Sylow (N)+ corollary]mt3-intersection; @[Index-p normality and solvability via nilpotence]mt3-pgroups; @[Solvable normal-subgroup extensions]mt3-solvable.

**(a).** $96=2^5\cdot3$; $n_2\mid3$ and $n_2\equiv1\pmod2$, so $n_2=1$ or3. If1, the subgroup $P$ of order32 is normal.

**(b).** $3\not\equiv1\pmod4$. The $(N)^+$ corollary gives $[P:H]=[Q:H]=2$. Since $|P|=|Q|=32$, $|H|=16$.

**(c).** An index2 subgroup is normal, so $H\trianglelefteq P,Q$ and $P,Q\le N_G(H)$. As $P\ne Q$, this normalizer strictly contains $P$. Its order divides96 and is a multiple of32 larger than32; the only possibility is96. Thus $N_G(H)=G$, i.e. $H\trianglelefteq G$.

**(d).** If $n_2=1$, then $P$ is a solvable 2-group and $G/P$ has order3 and is cyclic. If $n_2=3$, then $H$ is a solvable 2-group and $G/H$ has order6. Any order6 group has a normal Sylow3 subgroup with quotient of order2, so is solvable. In both cases the extension criterion makes $G$ solvable, and the exhibited proper nontrivial normal subgroup proves nonsimplicity.
