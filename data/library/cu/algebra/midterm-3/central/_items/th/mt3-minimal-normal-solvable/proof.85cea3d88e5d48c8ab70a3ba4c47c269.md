**Definitions and results used:** @mt3-minimal-normal, @mt3-solvable, @mt3-elementary-abelian, @mt3-characteristic-normal, @mt3-cauchy, @mt3-sylow-normality, and @mt3-abelian-powers.

The subgroup $M$ is solvable. Its derived subgroup $M'$ is characteristic in $M$, hence normal in $G$, so minimal normality gives $M'=1$ or $M$. Solvability excludes $M'=M\ne1$. Thus $M$ is abelian.

For any prime $p\mid|M|$, its unique Sylow $p$-subgroup is characteristic in $M$, hence normal in $G$. Minimality makes this nontrivial subgroup all of $M$, so $M$ is a $p$-group.

The power map $m\mapsto m^p$ is a homomorphism on $M$. Cauchy supplies a nontrivial kernel, so finiteness makes its image $M^p$ proper. This image is characteristic in $M$, hence normal in $G$, so minimality forces $M^p=1$. Therefore $M$ is elementary abelian.
