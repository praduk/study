For **finite** $G$, the following are equivalent: nilpotent; every proper subgroup is strictly contained in its normalizer; every Sylow subgroup is normal; $G$ is the direct product of its Sylow subgroups; every maximal subgroup is normal.

For arbitrary groups, $Z_c(G)=G$ iff $L_c(G)=1$. Subgroups and quotients of a nilpotent group are nilpotent. Finite direct products of nilpotent groups are nilpotent. If $G/Z(G)$ is nilpotent, so is $G$.

**Failure of extensions:** $C_3\trianglelefteq S_3$ and $S_3/C_3\cong C_2$ are nilpotent, but $S_3$ is not. Thus “normal subgroup and quotient nilpotent” does not suffice. Replacing “nilpotent” by “solvable” does work.

**Dihedral quick test:** $D_{2n}$ is nilpotent iff $n$ is a power of2 (including1 if that degenerate presentation is allowed). The homework provides the proof.
