A group is solvable iff it has a finite series $1=G_0\trianglelefteq G_1\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq G_d=G$ with abelian factors. Equivalently, its derived series reaches1.

- Subgroups and quotients of solvable groups are solvable.
- If $N\trianglelefteq G$ and $N,G/N$ are solvable, then $G$ is solvable.
- Nilpotent implies solvable; the converse fails for $S_3$.
- A nontrivial finite simple solvable group has prime order.
- Every minimal normal subgroup of a finite solvable group is elementary abelian: $C_p^r$ for some $r\ge1$.

**Elementary abelian** means abelian and $x^p=1$ for every $x$; equivalently a vector space over $\F_p$, with group multiplication as vector addition and integer powers as scalar multiplication. Exponent $p$ alone does not imply abelian for odd $p$.

**Deep results, stated separately:** Burnside's $p^a q^b$ theorem says all groups of that order are solvable. Feit–Thompson says every finite odd-order group is solvable.
