**Results used:** @mt3-series-functorial and @mt3-central-series-equivalence.

If $L_c(G)=1$, then $L_c(H)\le L_c(G)=1$ for every $H\le G$, and a quotient map sends $L_c(G)$ onto the quotient's $c$th lower term. The equivalence theorem proves both closure statements.

Commutators in a direct product are computed coordinatewise, so $L_i(\prod_jG_j)=\prod_jL_i(G_j)$. For finitely many nilpotent factors, choose $i$ at least all their classes.

Finally, if $G/Z(G)$ has lower term $c$ trivial, then $L_c(G)\le Z(G)$ and $L_{c+1}(G)=1$. Thus $G$ is nilpotent, of class at most $c+1$.
