An image of a generator must generate, and $a^u$ generates exactly when $u$ is invertible modulo $n$. Conversely each such choice defines a bijective homomorphism. The count for $p^a$ excludes the $p^{a-1}$ residue classes divisible by $p$.

For the final cyclicity assertion, more generally let $B$ be a finite subgroup of a field's multiplicative group and let $m$ be the least common multiple of its element orders. For each prime power dividing $m$, take a power of a suitable element to obtain that prime-power order. Since $B$ is abelian, multiplying these elements of pairwise coprime orders gives an element of order $m$: for commuting $x,y$ of coprime orders, $(xy)^k=1$ implies $x^k=y^{-k}$ belongs to both cyclic subgroups, whose intersection is trivial by @mt3-lagrange. Thus both orders divide $k$. Every element of $B$ is a root of $X^m-1$, so the polynomial root bound gives $|B|\le m$. That bound follows by factoring out $X-a$ at each root and inducting on the degree. The element just constructed gives $m\le|B|$, hence it generates $B$. Apply this to $\F_q^\times$.

**Use when:** an action on $C_q$ has image order dividing both the acting group order and $q-1$. If these are coprime, @mt3-first-isomorphism and @mt3-lagrange force trivial action.
