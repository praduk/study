Every upper-central, lower-central, and derived term is characteristic in its group. If $f:G\to K$ is a homomorphism, then

$$
f(L_i(G))\le L_i(K),\qquad f(G^{(i)})\le K^{(i)}.
$$

Both are equalities if $f$ is surjective. In particular, inclusion $H\le G$ gives $L_i(H)\le L_i(G)$ and $H^{(i)}\le G^{(i)}$.

There is no analogous assertion $f(Z_i(G))\le Z_i(K)$ for arbitrary homomorphisms; an element central in a subgroup need not be central in the ambient group.
