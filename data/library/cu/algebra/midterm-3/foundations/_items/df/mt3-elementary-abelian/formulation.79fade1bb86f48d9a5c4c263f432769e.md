An **elementary abelian $p$-group** is an abelian group $E$ such that $x^p=1$ for every $x\in E$. The trivial group is allowed.

Every finite abelian $p$-group is isomorphic to $(\Z/p\Z)^n$.

It is the same structure as a vector space over $\F_p$: multiplication is vector addition, and $x^a$ is scalar multiplication by the residue of $a$. This is well defined because $x^p=1$; the vector-space axioms follow from commutativity and exponent laws. Conversely, the additive group of an $\F_p$-vector space has these properties. A finite such group is $C_p^r$, where $r$ is its dimension.

The hypothesis **abelian** is essential: exponent $p$ alone does not imply commutativity for odd $p$.
