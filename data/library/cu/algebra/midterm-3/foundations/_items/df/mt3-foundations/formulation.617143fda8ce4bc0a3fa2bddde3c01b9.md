Throughout, $G$ is a group with identity $1$, and $p,q,r$ denote primes. A group is assumed **finite only when stated**; all Sylow arguments here are finite-group arguments. $C_n$ is cyclic of order $n$, $S_n$ the permutation group on $n$ letters, and $A_n$ its even-permutation subgroup. Products of permutations act rightmost first. $D_{2n}$ has **order $2n$**, with $r^n=s^2=1$ and $srs^{-1}=r^{-1}$; $Q_8=\{\pm1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\}$.

- $H\le G$: subgroup. $H\trianglelefteq G$: normal, meaning $gHg^{-1}=H$ for all $g$. $H\operatorname{char}G$: every automorphism preserves $H$. Characteristic implies normal. If $K\operatorname{char}H\trianglelefteq G$, then $K\trianglelefteq G$; normality alone is **not** transitive.
- $\langle S\rangle$: the subgroup generated by $S$. The **normal closure** additionally includes all conjugates. A generating set is **minimal** if deleting any of its members stops it generating; this does not mean minimum cardinality in an arbitrary group.
- $Z(G)=\{z:zg=gz\text{ for every }g\in G\}$; $C_G(H)=\{g:gh=hg\text{ for every }h\in H\}$; $N_G(H)=\{g:gHg^{-1}=H\}$. Thus $H\trianglelefteq N_G(H)$ and $C_G(H)\trianglelefteq N_G(H)$.
- $|G:H|$: index, the number of cosets. For finite $G$, $|G|=|H||G:H|$ (Lagrange). If $p\mid |G|$, an element of order $p$ exists (Cauchy).
- A **maximal subgroup** is proper, with no subgroup strictly between it and $G$. A **minimal normal subgroup** is nontrivial and normal in $G$, with no smaller nontrivial subgroup normal in $G$. It need not be simple as an abstract group.
- A **simple group** is nontrivial with no proper nontrivial normal subgroup. An abelian simple group is cyclic of prime order: any nonidentity element generates it, and a composite-order cyclic group has a proper nontrivial subgroup.
- $G/N$ requires $N\trianglelefteq G$. A homomorphism $f:G\to K$ has kernel $\ker f\trianglelefteq G$, and $G/\ker f\cong\im f$.

**Three quotient tools.** If $N\trianglelefteq G$, subgroups of $G/N$ correspond to subgroups of $G$ containing $N$, preserving normality and index. If $H\le G$, then $HN/N\cong H/(H\cap N)$. If $N\le M\trianglelefteq G$ with $N\trianglelefteq G$, then $(G/N)/(M/N)\cong G/M$.

**Product tools.** If one of $H,K$ normalizes the other, $HK$ is a subgroup; in the finite case $|HK|=|H||K|/|H\cap K|$. If both are normal, their commutators lie in $H\cap K$; trivial intersection therefore makes them commute.

**Conjugation tool.** The homomorphism $N_G(H)\to\Aut(H)$ has kernel $C_G(H)$, so

$$
N_G(H)/C_G(H)\hookrightarrow\Aut(H).
$$

For $H=C_q$, $|\Aut(H)|=q-1$. If a $p$-group acts on $C_q$ and $p\nmid q-1$, its action is trivial.

For later use, $\Aut(G)$ is the group of bijective homomorphisms $G\to G$, with composition. For prime $q$, $\Aut(C_q)\cong(\Z/q\Z)^\times\cong C_{q-1}$ (Dummit–Foote §4.4); for $C_{p^a}$ its automorphism group has order $p^{a-1}(p-1)$, by counting residue classes not divisible by $p$.

**Factor through a quotient.** If $f:G\to K$ is a homomorphism and $N\trianglelefteq G$ lies in $\ker f$, then a unique homomorphism $\overline f:G/N\to K$ satisfies $\overline f(gN)=f(g)$. It is well-defined because changing $g$ by an element of $N$ does not change $f(g)$; uniqueness follows because every coset has a representative.
