A **homomorphism** $f:G\to K$ satisfies $f(xy)=f(x)f(y)$ for all $x,y\in G$. Its **kernel** is $\ker f=\{g:f(g)=1\}$ and its **image** is $\operatorname{im}f=\{f(g):g\in G\}$. An isomorphism is a bijective homomorphism. The kernel is normal: $f(gxg^{-1})=1$ whenever $f(x)=1$. The image is a subgroup. A homomorphism is injective exactly when its kernel is trivial, since $f(x)=f(y)$ is equivalent to $xy^{-1}\in\ker f$.
