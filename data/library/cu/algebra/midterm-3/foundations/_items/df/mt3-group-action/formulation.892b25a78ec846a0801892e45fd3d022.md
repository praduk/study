An **action** of a group $G$ on a set $X$ is a homomorphism $\rho:G\to\Sym(X)$. Write $g\cdot x=\rho(g)(x)$; thus $1\cdot x=x$ and $(gh)\cdot x=g\cdot(h\cdot x)$.

The **orbit** of $x$ is $Gx=\{g\cdot x:g\in G\}$. Its **stabilizer** is $G_x=\{g:g\cdot x=x\}$. The action is **transitive** if it has one orbit and **faithful** if $\ker\rho=1$. A fixed point is an $x$ with $G_x=G$; write $X^G$ for all such points.

Conjugation is the action $g\cdot x=gxg^{-1}$ on $G$. Its orbits are conjugacy classes and the stabilizer of $x$ is $C_G(x)$. An automorphism action on subgroups fixes a subgroup when it preserves that subgroup setwise; its elements need not be fixed individually.
