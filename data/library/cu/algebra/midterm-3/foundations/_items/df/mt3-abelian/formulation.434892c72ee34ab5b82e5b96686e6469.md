For an abelian group $A$, a **complex linear character** is a homomorphism $\chi:A\to\C^\times$. The **character group** or **dual group** is $\widehat A=\Hom(A,\C^\times)$, with pointwise multiplication:

$$
(\chi\psi)(a)=\chi(a)\psi(a).
$$

The identity character is constantly $1$ and the inverse is $\chi^{-1}(a)=\chi(a)^{-1}$. The target's commutativity makes pointwise products homomorphisms, so the dual is abelian.

This preserves the guide's complex-valued character convention. Dummit–Foote Exercise 5.2.14 instead specifies the subgroup $\mu_\infty\le\C^\times$ of all roots of unity as target. For finite $A$, every character value is a root of unity, so the two definitions coincide. For infinite $A$ they need not coincide. See @mt3-abelian-duality for finite abelian self-duality.
