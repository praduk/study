For a subset $S\subseteq G$, its **centralizer** is

$$
C_G(S)=\{g\in G:gs=sg\text{ for every }s\in S\}.
$$

It is a subgroup, since the identity, products, and inverses preserve these commutation equations. For a single element use $C_G(s)=C_G(\{s\})$. In particular $Z(G)=C_G(G)$. Centralizing means fixing each element under conjugation; normalizing only means preserving a subgroup setwise.
