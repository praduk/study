**Definitions and results used:** @[Upper central series]mt3-upper-central; @[Nilpotence]mt3-nilpotent-definition; @[Nontrivial p-group center]mt3-pgroups.

If $Z_i(P)\ne P$, the quotient $P/Z_i(P)$ is a nontrivial finite $p$-group and has nontrivial center. By the definition of the upper central series, $Z_{i+1}(P)/Z_i(P)$ is that center, so $Z_{i+1}(P)>Z_i(P)$. Its order grows by at least a factor of $p$. Starting from $Z_0(P)=1$, at most $a$ such strict increases are possible, so some $Z_i(P)=P$. For $P=1$, the class is $0$.
