**Definitions and results used:** @[Conjugation action and the fixed-point congruence]mt3-actions; @[Centers, Cauchy theorem, and quotient correspondence]mt3-foundations; @[Elementary abelian groups]mt3-linear.

Conjugation by $P$ on $N\trianglelefteq P$ has fixed points $N\cap Z(P)$; all non-singleton orbits have size divisible by $p$. Hence $|N\cap Z(P)|\equiv |N|\equiv0\pmod p$, and this intersection contains1, so it has at least $p$ elements. Take $N=P$ for the center result. Successive nontrivial $p$-group quotients have nontrivial centers, so the upper central series reaches $P$.

For the strict normalizer property, induct on $|P|$. If $Z(P)\nleq H$, then $HZ(P)>H$ lies in $N_P(H)$. Otherwise quotient by $Z(P)$ and lift the strict normalizer supplied by induction. Maximality then gives $N_P(M)=P$. The nontrivial quotient $P/M$ has no proper nontrivial subgroup, so Cauchy forces order $p$.

For normal subgroups of each order, choose a central order-$p$ subgroup of the given nontrivial normal subgroup, quotient by it, induct, and lift.

If $G/Z(G)$ is cyclic, write every element as $g^iz$ for one fixed $g$ and central $z$; all elements commute. Thus a group of order $p^2$ cannot have center of order $p$ and must be abelian. In the nonabelian order-$p^3$ case, the center has order $p$ and the order-$p^2$ quotient is abelian but not cyclic, hence $C_p^2$.
