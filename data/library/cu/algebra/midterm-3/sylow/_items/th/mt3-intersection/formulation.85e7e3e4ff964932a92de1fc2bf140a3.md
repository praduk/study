If $n_p\not\equiv1\pmod{p^2}$, there are distinct $P,Q\in\Syl_p(G)$ such that, for $H=P\cap Q$,

$$
[P:H]=[Q:H]=p.
$$

Consequently $H\trianglelefteq P$, $H\trianglelefteq Q$, and

$$
\langle P,Q\rangle\le N_G(H),\qquad |N_G(H)|>|P|.
$$

**Exam sequence:** check $n_p$ modulo $p^2$ → choose $P,Q$ → compute $|H|=|P|/p$ → index-$p$ normality **inside the $p$-groups** → constrain $|N_G(H)|$ using Lagrange → produce a normal subgroup or a small-index action.

**Limits:** $H$ could be trivial if $|P|=p$. The conclusion does not automatically say $H\trianglelefteq G$. You must establish $N_G(H)=G$ or exploit its proper index. An index-$p$ subgroup of an arbitrary group need not be normal.
