**Definitions and results used:** @[Nontrivial p-group center]mt3-pgroups; @[Cyclic central quotient]mt3-cyclic-central-quotient; @[Elementary abelian groups]mt3-elementary-abelian; @[Lagrange theorem]mt3-lagrange.

The center has order $p$ or $p^2$. If it had order $p$, the quotient by the center would have order $p$, hence be cyclic, forcing $G$ abelian and contradicting that center order. Therefore $Z(G)=G$.

If $G$ has an element of order $p^2$, it is cyclic. Otherwise every nonidentity element has order $p$, so $G$ is an elementary abelian group with $p^2$ elements, a two-dimensional vector space over $\F_p$. Thus $G\cong C_p\times C_p$.
