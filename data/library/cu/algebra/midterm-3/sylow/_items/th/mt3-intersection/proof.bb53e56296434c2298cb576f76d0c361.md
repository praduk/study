**Definitions and results used:** @[Sylow (N)+ and its contrapositive]mt3-nplus; @[Index-p subgroups of finite p-groups are normal]mt3-pgroups; @[Normalizer definition and Lagrange theorem]mt3-foundations.

If all distinct Sylow intersections had index divisible by $p^2$, @mt3-nplus would give $n_p\equiv1\pmod{p^2}$. Thus some index is not divisible by $p^2$. It is a nontrivial power of $p$, so it equals $p$. Since $|P|=|Q|$, both indices equal $p$.

An index-$p$ subgroup of a finite $p$-group is normal: its coset action has image a transitive $p$-subgroup of $S_p$, necessarily of order $p$ (the $p$-part of $p!$ is $p$); its kernel therefore has the same index and equals the subgroup. Apply this to $H$ in $P,Q$.

Both $P,Q$ normalize $H$, so their generated subgroup lies in $N_G(H)$. As $P\ne Q$ and they have equal order, $Q\nleq P$ and this normalizer strictly contains $P$.
