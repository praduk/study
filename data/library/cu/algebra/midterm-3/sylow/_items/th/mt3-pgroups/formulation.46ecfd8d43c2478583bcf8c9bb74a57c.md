For a nontrivial finite $p$-group $P$:

- $Z(P)\ne1$; more generally, $1\ne N\trianglelefteq P$ implies $N\cap Z(P)\ne1$.
- $P$ is nilpotent; repeatedly quotient by its nontrivial center.
- Every proper $H<P$ satisfies $H<N_P(H)$.
- Every maximal subgroup is normal and has index $p$; every index-$p$ subgroup is normal.
- For each $p^b\mid |P|$, $P$ has a normal subgroup of order $p^b$. More generally this works inside a given normal subgroup of that order or larger.
- Every group of order $p^2$ is abelian. If $P$ is nonabelian of order $p^3$, then $|Z(P)|=p$ and $P/Z(P)\cong C_p^2$.

**Proof triggers:** normal $p$-subgroup → class equation on it; induction → quotient by a central subgroup of order $p$; maximal subgroup → strict normalizer; cyclic central quotient → the whole group is abelian.
