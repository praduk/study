Let $p$ be a @[prime element]mt4-prime-element in a domain, and write $p=ab$. Primality gives $p\mid a$ or $p\mid b$. If $a=pc$, @[cancellation]mt4-cancellation in $p=pcb$ gives $cb=1$, so $b$ is a unit. The other case gives that $a$ is a unit. This proves @[irreducibility]mt4-irreducible.

Conversely, let $p$ be irreducible in a @[PID]mt4-pid. It is a nonunit, so $(p)$ is proper by the @[unit ideal criterion]mt4-unit-ideal-test. An ideal containing $(p)$ has the form $(m)$, whence $p=mt$. Irreducibility forces one factor to be a unit. If $m$ is a unit, $(m)=R$. If $t$ is a unit, $m=pt^{-1}$, giving $(m)=(p)$. Thus $(p)$ is @[maximal]mt4-maximal-ideal. Its quotient is a @[field]mt4-maximal-quotient-field and therefore an @[integral domain]gre:mathematics-subject-test:abstract-algebra:th:field-domain. The @[prime ideal quotient criterion]mt4-prime-quotient-domain makes $(p)$ prime, equivalently $p$ is a prime element.
