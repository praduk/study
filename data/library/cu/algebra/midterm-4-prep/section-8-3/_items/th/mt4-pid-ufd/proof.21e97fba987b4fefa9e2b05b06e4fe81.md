In a @[PID]mt4-pid any ascending chain $I_1\subseteq I_2\subseteq\cdots$ stabilizes. Its union is an ideal (two elements lie in a common member), hence is generated by some $d$. Once $d\in I_n$, the whole union equals $I_n$.

If some nonzero nonunit had no finite factorization into irreducibles, it would factor into two nonunits, at least one of which again has no such factorization. Repeatedly choosing such a factor produces a strictly ascending chain of principal ideals: for $a=bc\ne0$ with $c$ a nonunit, $(a)\subsetneq(b)$ by cancellation. This contradicts stabilization, proving existence.

For uniqueness, any irreducible in one factorization is prime by @[Proposition 11]mt4-pid-prime-irreducible. It must divide a factor of the other product, and the two factors are then @[associates]mt4-associates by irreducibility. Cancel this pair, absorbing the unit, and repeat. Neither side can have leftover nonunit factors whose product is a unit. This gives @[unique factorization]mt4-ufd. The Euclidean assertion follows from @[Proposition 1]mt4-euclidean-principal.
