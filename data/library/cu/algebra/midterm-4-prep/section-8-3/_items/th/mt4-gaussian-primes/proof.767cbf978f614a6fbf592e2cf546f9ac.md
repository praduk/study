The integer norm from @[the quadratic norm formula]mt4-quadratic-integers-norm is multiplicative and positive on nonzero elements, and norm $1$ detects units by @[the norm criterion]mt4-quadratic-norm-units. An element of prime norm is therefore irreducible, in particular $1+i$. Any factorization of a rational prime $p$ into two nonunits would force each to have norm $p$ because $N(p)=p^2$. For $q\equiv3\pmod4$ that is impossible: an integer square is $0$ or $1$ modulo $4$.

For $p=4m+1$, the nonzero residues modulo $p$ can be paired with their inverses except for $\pm1$, so $(p-1)!\equiv-1\pmod p$. Pairing $k$ with $p-k$ instead gives $(p-1)!\equiv((2m)!)^2\pmod p$. Thus $p\mid n^2+1=(n+i)(n-i)$ for $n=(2m)!$, yet $p$ divides neither Gaussian factor, whose imaginary coefficient is $\pm1$. By @[Gaussian division]mt4-gaussian-division and @[Proposition 1]mt4-euclidean-principal, the Gaussian ring is a PID, so @[Proposition 11]mt4-pid-prime-irreducible implies $p$ is reducible. A factor $\pi=a+bi$ of norm $p$ gives $p=\pi\bar\pi$. These two are nonassociate: the four Gaussian units $\pm1,\pm i$ would force $a=0$, $b=0$ or $a=\pm b$, contradicting $p=a^2+b^2$ for an odd prime.

Finally any Gaussian irreducible $z$ divides the ordinary integer $N(z)>1$. It is prime in the Gaussian PID, so it divides some rational prime in the integer factorization of $N(z)$, and is associate to one of the irreducibles just listed.
