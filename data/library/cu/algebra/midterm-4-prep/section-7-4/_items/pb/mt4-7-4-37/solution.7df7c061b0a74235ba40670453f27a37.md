Suppose $R$ is @[local]mt4-local-ring with unique maximal ideal $M$. For a nonunit $a$, the @[principal ideal]mt4-principal-ideal $(a)$ is proper by @[the unit ideal test]mt4-unit-ideal-test. Every proper ideal lies in a maximal ideal by @[Proposition 11]mt4-maximal-over-ideal. That maximal ideal must be $M$, so $a\in M$. Thus every element outside $M$ is a @[unit]mt4-unit. Conversely $M$ contains no unit, since it is proper.

Now suppose the nonunits form an ideal $M$. Since $1$ is a unit, $M$ is proper. Every proper ideal consists entirely of nonunits by the same test, so each lies in $M$. Thus $M$ is maximal, and any other maximal ideal would have to be contained in it, hence equal to it by @[the definition of maximality]mt4-maximal-ideal. This proves that $R$ is local.
