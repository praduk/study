**Strategy.** Relate nonunits to proper ideals. In the forward direction, place the principal ideal of a nonunit inside the unique maximal ideal. In the converse, use the ideal of all nonunits to contain every proper ideal.

**From a unique maximal ideal to units outside it.** Suppose $R$ is @[local]mt4-local-ring with unique maximal ideal $M$. If $a$ is a nonunit, its @[principal ideal]mt4-principal-ideal $(a)$ is proper by @[the unit ideal test]mt4-unit-ideal-test. By @[Proposition 11]mt4-maximal-over-ideal, $(a)$ lies in a maximal ideal, which must be $M$. Thus every nonunit belongs to $M$, so each element outside $M$ is a @[unit]mt4-unit. Also, $M$ itself contains no unit because it is proper.

**From the ideal of nonunits to a unique maximal ideal.** Suppose the nonunits form an ideal $M$. Since $1$ is a unit, $M$ is proper. An ideal containing a unit contains $1$ and hence equals $R$. Thus every proper ideal consists of nonunits and lies in $M$. In particular, no proper ideal strictly contains $M$, so $M$ is maximal. Any maximal ideal $P$ lies in $M$ and must equal $M$ by @[the definition of maximality]mt4-maximal-ideal. Therefore $R$ is local.
