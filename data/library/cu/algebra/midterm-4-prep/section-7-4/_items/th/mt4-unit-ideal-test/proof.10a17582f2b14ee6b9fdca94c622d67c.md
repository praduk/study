If $I=R$, it contains $1$, which is its own inverse. Conversely, if $u\in I$ is a @[unit]mt4-unit, then $1=u^{-1}u\in I$ by @[ideal absorption]mt4-ideal. Every $r\in R$ now belongs to $I$ because $r=r1$.

In a commutative ring with identity, @[the principal ideal]mt4-principal-ideal $(a)$ contains $1$ exactly when $ra=1$ for some $r\in R$. Commutativity also gives $ar=1$, so this says precisely that $a$ is invertible.
