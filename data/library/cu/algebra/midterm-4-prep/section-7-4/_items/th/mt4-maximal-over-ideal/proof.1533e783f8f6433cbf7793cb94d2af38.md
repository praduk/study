Let $\mathcal S$ consist of all proper two-sided ideals containing $I$, partially ordered by inclusion. It contains $I$. For a nonempty @[chain]math:set:orders-well-orders:df:extremal-elements $\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal S$, take $U=\bigcup_{J\in\mathcal C}J$. If $a,b\in U$, the chain orders their respective ideals by inclusion. The larger one contains both $a$ and $b$, so $a-b\in U$. If $a\in U$ and $r\in R$, the ideal containing $a$ also contains $ra$ and $ar$. By @[the ideal test]mt4-ideal, $U$ is an ideal containing $I$.

No member contains $1$, since @[an ideal containing a unit is the whole ring]mt4-unit-ideal-test. Thus $1\notin U$, making $U$ a proper ideal and an upper bound in $\mathcal S$. The empty chain has $I$ as an upper bound. By @[Zorn’s lemma]mt4-zorn-lemma, $\mathcal S$ has a maximal member $M$. Every proper ideal strictly containing $M$ would also belong to $\mathcal S$, contradicting this maximality. Thus $M$ is a @[maximal ideal]mt4-maximal-ideal of $R$ containing $I$.
