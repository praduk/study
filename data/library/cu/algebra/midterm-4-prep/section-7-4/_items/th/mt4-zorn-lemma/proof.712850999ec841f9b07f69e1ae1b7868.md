By @[the well-ordering theorem]math:set:axiom-choice:th:well-ordering-choice, the assumed @[axiom of choice]math:set:axiom-choice:ax:choice lets us well-order $S$. Use this order only to list its elements. By @[the order-type theorem]math:set:ordinals:th:well-order-type, write the list as $(s_\alpha)_{\alpha<\theta}$ for an ordinal $\theta$.

Construct a subset $C$ by @[transfinite recursion]math:set:ordinals:th:transfinite-recursion. At stage $\alpha$, include $s_\alpha$ exactly when $t\le s_\alpha$ in the **original partial order** for every $t$ included at an earlier stage. This also specifies the decision at limit stages. Each later selected element dominates all earlier ones, so $C$ is a @[chain]math:set:orders-well-orders:df:extremal-elements.

By hypothesis $C$ has an upper bound $u\in S$. Suppose some $v\in S$ satisfied $u<v$. When the list reached $v$, every element already selected was at most $u$, hence at most $v$. The rule therefore put $v$ in $C$, which would force $v\le u$. This contradiction makes $u$ maximal.
