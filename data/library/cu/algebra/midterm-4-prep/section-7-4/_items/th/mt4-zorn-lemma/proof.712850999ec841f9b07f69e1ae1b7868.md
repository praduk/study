Use the well-ordering principle, the equivalent form of choice in Appendix I, Theorem 2. Well-order the underlying set $S$. Proceeding by transfinite recursion through this auxiliary order, include an element in $C$ exactly when it is at least as large in the original partial order as every element already included. All members of $C$ are pairwise comparable, so $C$ has an upper bound $u\in S$. If $v>u$, then every member of $C$ selected before $v$ is at most $u<v$. The rule would have included $v$ in $C$, contradicting that $u$ bounds $C$. Thus $u$ is maximal.
