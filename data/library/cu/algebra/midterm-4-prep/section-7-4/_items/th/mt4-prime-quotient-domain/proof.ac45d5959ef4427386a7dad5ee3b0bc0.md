In the @[quotient ring]mt4-quotient-ring, $a+P=0$ exactly when $a\in P$. If $R$ is the zero ring, its only ideal is improper and its quotient is again zero, so both conditions fail. Otherwise the quotient identity $1+P$ is nonzero exactly when $1\notin P$, which is equivalent to $P\ne R$ by @[the unit ideal test]mt4-unit-ideal-test.

Now $(a+P)(b+P)=ab+P$, so a zero product in $R/P$ means $ab\in P$. At least one factor is zero precisely when $a\in P$ or $b\in P$. Thus the condition that this commutative quotient be an @[integral domain]mt4-integral-domain is equivalent to $P$ being a @[prime ideal]mt4-prime-ideal.
