If $M=R$, the ideal is improper and the quotient is the zero ring, whose identity equals zero. Thus neither condition holds. If $M$ is proper, then $1\notin M$ by @[the unit ideal test]mt4-unit-ideal-test, so $1+M$ is a nonzero identity in the @[quotient ring]mt4-quotient-ring.

By @[the ideal correspondence theorem]mt4-ideal-correspondence, the ideals of $R/M$ correspond to the ideals of $R$ containing $M$. The endpoints $M$ and $R$ correspond to $(0)$ and $R/M$. Therefore $M$ is @[maximal]mt4-maximal-ideal exactly when these are the only two quotient ideals. By @[the field criterion for ideals]mt4-field-ideal-test this is equivalent to $R/M$ being a field.
