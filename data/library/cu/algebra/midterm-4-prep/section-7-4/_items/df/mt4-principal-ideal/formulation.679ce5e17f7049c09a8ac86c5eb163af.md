For a subset $S$ of a ring $R$, the ideal $(S)$ generated by $S$ is the smallest two-sided ideal containing $S$, equivalently the intersection of all such ideals. An ideal is **principal** if it equals $(a)$ for one $a\in R$. In a commutative ring with identity,

$$
(a)=\{ra:r\in R\}.
$$

Indeed these multiples form an ideal containing $a$, and every ideal containing $a$ contains all its multiples. More generally, $(a_1,\ldots,a_k)$ consists of all sums $r_1a_1+\cdots+r_ka_k$ with $r_i\in R$.
