By the @[zero-divisor definition]mt4-zero-divisor, zero is excluded. Thus for $R=\Z$ and $p=0$, $b=1$ satisfies $bp=0$ although $p$ is not a zero divisor. The criterion requires $p\ne0$ over a nonzero ring.

Fix $p=\sum_{j=0}^n a_jx^j\ne0$. Any nonzero scalar $b$ with $bp=0$ is itself a nonzero constant polynomial, proving one implication. Conversely, suppose $p$ is a zero divisor. Its @[annihilator]mt4-annihilator therefore contains a nonzero polynomial. The nonzero annihilators have nonnegative integer degrees, so @[well-ordering]math:set:orders-well-orders:df:well-order gives one, $g$, of least degree $m$. Write $b_m\ne0$ for its leading coefficient.

We prove $a_k g=0$ by descending @[induction]math:set:naturals-recursion:th:natural-induction, starting at $k=n$ and increasing $n-k$ by one at each step. After all higher terms have been removed, $p_k g=0$ for $p_k=\sum_{j=0}^k a_j x^j$. At $k=n$ this is just $pg=0$. By the @[coefficient formula]mt4-polynomial-ring the coefficient of $x^{k+m}$ is $a_kb_m=0$. Thus $a_kg$ is zero or has degree less than $m$. It also annihilates $p$, since $(a_kg)p=a_k(gp)=0$. Minimality forces $a_k g=0$. Subtracting $a_kx^kg$ leaves $p_{k-1}g=0$ for the next step. In particular $a_k b_m=0$ for every $k$, so $b_m p=0$. This is the required nonzero scalar.
