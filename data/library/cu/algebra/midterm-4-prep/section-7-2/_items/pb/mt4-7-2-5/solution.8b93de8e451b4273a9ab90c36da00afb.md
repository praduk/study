**(a)** The @[Laurent series operations]mt4-laurent-series satisfy the @[commutative ring axioms]mt4-ring. For series with lower bounds $N,M$, their sum has lower bound $\min(N,M)$ and their product has lower bound $N+M$. Addition and additive inverses are coefficientwise, and the all-zero series is the additive identity. Multiplication is commutative because interchanging the two factors interchanges the indices in each coefficient sum. Associativity and distributivity follow coefficientwise by rearranging finite sums of products in $F$ (for three factors, the indices are bounded below and their sum is fixed). The constant series $1$ is the identity.

Write a nonzero $f$ as $x^N u$ where $N$ is its least exponent and $u\in F[[x]]$ has nonzero constant coefficient. Since $F$ is a @[field]mt4-field, that coefficient is invertible. The @[power series unit criterion]mt4-power-series-unit gives $u^{-1}\in F[[x]]$, so $x^{-N}u^{-1}$ is an inverse in $F((x))$.

**(b)** Let $v(f)=N$ and $v(g)=M$. In degree $N+M$ the product coefficient is exactly the product of the two first nonzero coefficients, which is nonzero because @[a field is an integral domain]gre:mathematics-subject-test:abstract-algebra:th:field-domain. All earlier coefficients are zero. Thus $v(fg)=N+M$. If $f+g\ne0$, all coefficients below $\min(N,M)$ still vanish, giving $v(f+g)\ge\min(N,M)$. Since $v(x^k)=k$ for every $k\in\Z$, $v$ is surjective. These are the @[discrete valuation axioms]mt4-discrete-valuation. Its nonzero elements of nonnegative valuation are exactly the nonzero series with no negative powers. Adjoining zero gives the @[power series ring]mt4-power-series $F[[x]]$.
