For $P=(0)$ the quotient is isomorphic to $R$. If $P\ne(0)$, @[Proposition 7]mt4-pid-prime-maximal shows that $P$ is maximal, and @[the maximal ideal quotient criterion]mt4-maximal-quotient-field shows that $R/P$ is a field. A nonzero ideal in a field contains an invertible element and hence $1$, so the @[only ideals of the field]mt4-field-ideal-test are $(0)$ and $(1)$. Either case satisfies @[the definition of a PID]mt4-pid. One can also see the two PID requirements separately. By the @[ideal correspondence]mt4-ideal-correspondence each ideal upstairs containing $P$ maps to an ideal of $R/P$, and they account for every ideal downstairs. The image of $(a)$ is generated by $a+P$. Thus every quotient ideal is principal. The quotient is a domain by @[the prime ideal quotient criterion]mt4-prime-quotient-domain.
