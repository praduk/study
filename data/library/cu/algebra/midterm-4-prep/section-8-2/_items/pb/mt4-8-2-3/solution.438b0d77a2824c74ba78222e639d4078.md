**By ideal correspondence.** The @[prime ideal quotient criterion]mt4-prime-quotient-domain makes $R/P$ an integral domain. For any ideal $J$ of $R/P$, the @[ideal correspondence]mt4-ideal-correspondence gives an ideal $I\supseteq P$ of $R$ with $J=I/P$. Since $R$ is a @[PID]mt4-pid, write $I=(a)$. The elements of $J$ are exactly $ra+P=(r+P)(a+P)$, using @[quotient multiplication]mt4-quotient-operations. Thus $J$ is generated by $a+P$, proving the claim for every prime $P$.

**By splitting into two cases.** For $P=(0)$, the canonical map $r\mapsto r+(0)$ is an isomorphism with $R$. Otherwise $P$ is maximal by @[Proposition 7]mt4-pid-prime-maximal, so $R/P$ is a field by @[the maximal ideal quotient criterion]mt4-maximal-quotient-field. A field is an @[integral domain]gre:mathematics-subject-test:abstract-algebra:th:field-domain and its @[only ideals]mt4-field-ideal-test are $(0)$ and $(1)$. Thus it too is a PID.
