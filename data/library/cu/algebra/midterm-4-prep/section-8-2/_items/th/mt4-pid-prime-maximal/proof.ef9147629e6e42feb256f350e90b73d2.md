Write the nonzero prime ideal as $(p)$ by @[the PID hypothesis]mt4-pid. Every ideal containing $(p)$ has the form $(m)$. Then $p\in(m)$ means $p=mr$, by the description of @[principal ideals]mt4-principal-ideal. By @[primality]mt4-prime-ideal, either $m\in(p)$, giving equality, or $r=ps$ for some $s$. In the latter case $p=mps$, so @[cancellation]mt4-cancellation gives $ms=1$. Thus $m$ is a unit and $(m)=R$ by the @[unit ideal criterion]mt4-unit-ideal-test. Thus $(p)$ is a @[maximal ideal]mt4-maximal-ideal.
