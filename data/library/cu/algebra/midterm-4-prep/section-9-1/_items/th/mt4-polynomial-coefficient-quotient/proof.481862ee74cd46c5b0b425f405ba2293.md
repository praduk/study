Apply the @[quotient map]mt4-quotient-projection $R\to R/I$ to each coefficient. The resulting map $\rho:R[t]\to(R/I)[t]$ preserves sums and products because the @[polynomial operations]mt4-polynomial-ring use only sums and products of coefficients. It is onto: lift the finitely many coefficients of a given polynomial to $R$.

A polynomial belongs to the @[kernel]mt4-ring-homomorphism of $\rho$ exactly when all its coefficients lie in $I$. In that case it is a finite sum of terms $a_it^i$ with $a_i\in I$, hence belongs to the @[ideal generated by those constants]mt4-principal-ideal. Conversely, multiplying an element of $I$ by any polynomial gives coefficients in $I$, and finite sums preserve that property. Thus $\ker\rho=I[t]=(I)$. The @[first isomorphism theorem]mt4-first-isomorphism now gives $R[t]/I[t]\isoto(R/I)[t]$.

If $I$ is prime, then $R/I$ is an integral domain by the @[prime ideal quotient criterion]mt4-prime-quotient-domain. Its polynomial ring is a domain by @[Proposition 1]mt4-polynomial-domains. Applying the same criterion to $R[t]/I[t]$ shows that $I[t]$ is prime.
