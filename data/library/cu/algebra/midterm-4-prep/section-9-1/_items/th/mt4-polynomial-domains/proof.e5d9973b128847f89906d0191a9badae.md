Let $f$ and $g$ have degrees $m$ and $n$ and leading coefficients $a_m$ and $b_n$. In the @[coefficient formula for a product]mt4-polynomial-ring, the only term of degree $m+n$ comes from $a_mx^m$ and $b_nx^n$. Their coefficient $a_mb_n$ is nonzero because $D$ is an @[integral domain]mt4-integral-domain. No higher power occurs. This proves both the degree formula and the absence of nonzero zero divisors in $D[x]$.

If $f$ is a @[unit]mt4-unit with inverse $g$, then $fg=1$, so $m+n=0$. Both degrees must be zero, and the constant coefficients are inverses in $D$. Conversely, a constant unit retains its inverse in $D[x]$. This recovers @[Proposition 4 of §7.2]mt4-polynomial-degree-product.

For finitely many variables, apply the one-variable result each time a variable is adjoined. For infinitely many, @[each polynomial uses finitely many variables]mt4-polynomials-several-variables. Thus two proposed factors of zero, or a unit and its inverse, lie together in a finite-variable polynomial ring. The conclusions there apply to the original pair.
