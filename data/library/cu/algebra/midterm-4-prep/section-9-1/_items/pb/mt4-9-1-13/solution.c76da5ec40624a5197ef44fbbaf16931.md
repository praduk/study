Substitute $x=y^2$ to obtain a surjection $F[y][x]\to F[y]$. For each $i\ge1$, $x^i-(y^2)^i$ is a multiple of $x-y^2$ by the difference-of-powers identity. Term by term, $h(x,y)-h(y^2,y)$ is therefore a multiple of $x-y^2$, so the kernel is $(x-y^2)=(y^2-x)$. The first quotient is isomorphic to $F[y]$ by @[the first isomorphism theorem]mt4-first-isomorphism and is a domain by @[the polynomial domain theorem]mt4-polynomial-domains.

In the second @[quotient ring]mt4-quotient-ring, the classes of $y-x$ and $y+x$ have product zero by @[coset multiplication]mt4-quotient-operations, since $(y-x)(y+x)=y^2-x^2$. Each is nonzero: view the defining polynomial $y^2-x^2$ as a polynomial of degree two in $y$ over the domain $F[x]$. Every nonzero multiple has $y$-degree at least two by @[the degree formula]mt4-polynomial-domains, while each proposed factor has degree one. In characteristic two the two classes coincide, giving a nonzero element of square zero. In all characteristics the second quotient has a @[zero divisor]mt4-zero-divisor, a property preserved by a @[ring isomorphism]mt4-ring-isomorphism. The rings cannot be isomorphic.
