Regard $\Q[x,y]$ as $\Q[x][y]$ by @[the iterated polynomial ring definition]mt4-polynomials-several-variables. Evaluation at $x=0$ identifies $\Q[x]/(x)$ with $\Q$, since a polynomial has zero constant term precisely when it is divisible by $x$. By @[coefficient reduction]mt4-polynomial-coefficient-quotient, $\Q[x,y]/(x)\isoto\Q[y]$. This is a domain, and $y$ has no inverse there by @[the polynomial unit criterion]mt4-polynomial-domains. The @[prime]mt4-prime-quotient-domain and @[maximal]mt4-maximal-quotient-field quotient criteria show that $(x)$ is prime and is strictly below a maximal ideal.

Evaluation at $(0,0)$ maps $\Q[x,y]$ onto $\Q$. Its @[kernel]mt4-kernel-image is $(x,y)$ because every monomial except the constant one is a multiple of $x$ or $y$. The @[first isomorphism theorem]mt4-first-isomorphism identifies the quotient with $\Q$, a field and hence a domain. Thus $(x,y)$ is both maximal and prime. The inclusion $(x)\subsetneq(x,y)$ is also visible from the nonzero image of $y$ modulo $(x)$.
