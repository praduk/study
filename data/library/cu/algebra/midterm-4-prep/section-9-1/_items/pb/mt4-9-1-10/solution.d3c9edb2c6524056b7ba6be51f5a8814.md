From each pair $\{x_{2i-1},x_{2i}\}$ choose one variable $s_i$ and denote its partner by $t_i$. In $A/I$, let $P$ be the ideal generated by all $\bar s_i$. Setting the $s_i$ to zero gives a surjection

$$
A/I\longrightarrow\Z[t_1,t_2,\ldots].
$$

It is well defined because each generator of $I$ vanishes. Its kernel is $P$: in the @[monomial expansion]mt4-polynomials-several-variables the terms that disappear are precisely those divisible by some $s_i$. The target is a domain by @[the polynomial domain theorem]mt4-polynomial-domains. The @[first isomorphism theorem]mt4-first-isomorphism and @[quotient criterion]mt4-prime-quotient-domain make $P$ prime. Every $\bar t_i$ has nonzero image, so none belongs to $P$.

If a prime $Q$ lies inside $P$, then $\bar s_i\bar t_i=0\in Q$ for every $i$. Since $\bar t_i\notin Q$, @[primality]mt4-prime-ideal forces $\bar s_i\in Q$ for every $i$. Thus $Q=P$, proving @[minimality]mt4-minimal-prime. For each positive integer $j$, choose the even variable in pair $j$ and the odd variable in every other pair. Two such choices give distinct ideals because the even variable of the first exceptional pair survives in the second quotient. This exhibits infinitely many minimal primes.
