The @[distributive laws]mt4-ring give $(0+0)a=0a+0a$. The left side is $0a$, so @[cancellation in the additive group]cu:algebra:midterm:theorems:th:mt-cancellation gives $0a=0$. Distributing $a(0+0)$ gives $a0=0$ in the same way.

Now $ab+(-a)b=(a+(-a))b=0$. Thus $(-a)b$ is the additive inverse of $ab$. Similarly $ab+a(-b)=0$. Applying the sign rule twice gives $(-a)(-b)=-(-(ab))=ab$.

If $e,f$ are both multiplicative identities, $ef=f$ because $e$ is a left identity and $ef=e$ because $f$ is a right identity. Thus $e=f$. Finally, $(-1)a=-(1a)=-a$.
