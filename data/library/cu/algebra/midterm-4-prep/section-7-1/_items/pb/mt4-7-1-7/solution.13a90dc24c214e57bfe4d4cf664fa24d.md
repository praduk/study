Both $0$ and $1$ belong to the @[center]mt4-ring-center. For $z,w\in Z(R)$ and $r\in R$, distributivity gives $(z-w)r=r(z-w)$, and associativity gives $(zw)r=z(rw)=(rz)w=r(zw)$. The @[subring test]mt4-subring applies, and multiplication within $Z(R)$ is commutative.

Now let $R$ be a @[division ring]mt4-division-ring. For $0\ne z\in Z(R)$ the inverse $z^{-1}$ exists in $R$. Multiplying $zr=rz$ on the left and right by $z^{-1}$ gives $rz^{-1}=z^{-1}r$ for every $r$. Thus $z^{-1}\in Z(R)$. The center contains $1\ne0$ and satisfies the @[field definition]mt4-field.
