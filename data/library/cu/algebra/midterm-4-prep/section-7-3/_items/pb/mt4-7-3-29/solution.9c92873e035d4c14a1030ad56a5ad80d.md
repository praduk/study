Write $N$ for the @[nilradical]mt4-nilradical. It contains zero. For $a,b\in N$ choose $m,n\ge1$ with $a^m=b^n=0$. In the @[binomial expansion]mt4-binomial-theorem of $(a-b)^{m+n-1}$, every term has a factor $a^k$ with $k\ge m$ or a factor $(-b)^{m+n-1-k}$ with $m+n-1-k\ge n$. Hence the power is zero and $a-b\in N$. The same counting proof applies without a multiplicative identity by reading each term as repeated addition of a product of $m+n-1$ factors.

For $r\in R$, $(ra)^m=r^m a^m=0$ by commutativity, as in @[Exercise 7.1.14(b)]mt4-7-1-14. Thus $N$ absorbs multiplication by $R$, and the @[ideal test]mt4-ideal proves the claim.
