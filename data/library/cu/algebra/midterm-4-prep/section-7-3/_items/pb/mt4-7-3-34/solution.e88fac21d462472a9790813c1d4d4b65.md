**Strategy.** Start from the @[definitions of ideal sum and product]mt4-ideal-sum-product and use the @[two-sided ideal test]mt4-ideal. To compare $IJ$ with $I\cap J$, first follow an individual product $ij$. When $I+J=R$, use a decomposition of $1$ to build products from an arbitrary element of the intersection.

**(a) Verify that $I+J$ is an ideal.** It contains $0=0+0$. For $i,i'\in I$ and $j,j'\in J$,

$$
(i+j)-(i'+j')=(i-i')+(j-j')\in I+J.
$$

For $r\in R$, the @[ring laws]mt4-ring give $r(i+j)=ri+rj$ and $(i+j)r=ir+jr$. In each sum, the first term lies in $I$ and the second in $J$. This proves absorption on both sides.

**(a) Prove minimality.** Each $i\in I$ equals $i+0$, and each $j\in J$ equals $0+j$. Thus $I+J$ contains both ideals. Conversely, any ideal containing both contains every sum $i+j$ by additive closure. It therefore contains $I+J$.

**(b) Verify that $IJ$ is an ideal.** Its elements are finite sums of products $ij$ with $i\in I$ and $j\in J$. Zero is $0\cdot0$. Adding two such sums gives another one, and negation stays in the set since $-(ij)=(-i)j$ by the @[ring sign rule]mt4-ring-arithmetic. Thus $IJ$ is closed under subtraction. For $r\in R$, the products $r(ij)=(ri)j$ and $(ij)r=i(jr)$ still have the required form. Distributing over finite sums proves absorption on both sides.

**(b) Show $IJ\subseteq I\cap J$.** Each $ij$ lies in $I$ because $I$ absorbs multiplication on the right. It also lies in $J$ because $J$ absorbs multiplication on the left. Both ideals are closed under finite sums, so every element of $IJ$ lies in their intersection.

**(c) Exhibit strict containment.** Take $I=J=2\Z$, the @[principal ideal]mt4-principal-ideal generated by $2$ in $\Z$. Their intersection is $2\Z$, but their product is $4\Z$. Indeed, every product of two even integers is divisible by $4$, and $4k=(2k)2$ belongs to $IJ$ for each integer $k$. In particular $2\in I\cap J$ and $2\notin IJ$.

**(d) Prove equality in the comaximal case.** Suppose $R$ is commutative and $I+J=R$. Since the ideals are @[comaximal]mt4-comaximal, write $1=i+j$ with $i\in I$ and $j\in J$. For any $z\in I\cap J$, commutativity gives

$$
z=(i+j)z=iz+zj.
$$

Here $iz\in IJ$ because $z\in J$, and $zj\in IJ$ because $z\in I$. Thus $I\cap J\subseteq IJ$. Combining this with (b) proves equality.
