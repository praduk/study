For two-sided ideals $I,J$ of $R$, their sum is $I+J=\{i+j:i\in I,\ j\in J\}$. Their product $IJ$ consists of all finite sums $\sum_k i_kj_k$ with $i_k\in I,\ j_k\in J$. The order of the factors matters in general. A single product need not represent every element of $IJ$.

For an example where a sum is necessary, take $I=J=(2,x)$ in $\Z[x]$. Then $x^2+4=x\cdot x+2\cdot2\in IJ$. If $x^2+4=fg$ with $f,g\in I$, their @[degrees add]mt4-polynomial-degree-product and their integer leading coefficients multiply to $1$. A constant factor would have to be $\pm1$, but every element $2p+xq$ of this @[generated ideal]mt4-principal-ideal has even constant term. The only remaining possibility is two linear factors. An integer linear polynomial of degree one has a real root, while $x^2+4>0$ on $\R$. Thus this element of $IJ$ is no single product from $I$ and $J$.
