A ring isomorphism $R\to S$ is a bijective @[ring homomorphism]mt4-ring-homomorphism. Rings $R,S$ are isomorphic if at least one such map exists. For a ring isomorphism $\varphi:R\to S$, take $s=\varphi(a)$ and $t=\varphi(b)$. Then $\varphi(a+b)=s+t$ and $\varphi(ab)=st$. Applying the inverse bijection gives

$$
\varphi^{-1}(s+t)=a+b,\qquad \varphi^{-1}(st)=ab.
$$

Since $a=\varphi^{-1}(s)$ and $b=\varphi^{-1}(t)$, the inverse preserves both operations.
