Let $\pi:R\to R/I$ be the @[quotient projection]mt4-quotient-projection. If $A$ is a subring of $R$ containing $I$, then $\pi(A)=A/I$ is a subring by the @[image subring theorem]mt4-kernel-image applied to $\pi|_A$. If $B$ is a subring of $R/I$, its inverse image contains $I=\ker\pi$. It satisfies the @[subring test]mt4-subring because $\pi$ preserves differences and products.

These assignments are inverse. If $x\in\pi^{-1}(\pi(A))$, then $\pi(x)=\pi(a)$ for some $a\in A$. The @[coset equality criterion]mt4-quotient-ring gives $x-a\in I\subseteq A$, hence $x\in A$. The reverse inclusion is immediate. Also $\pi(\pi^{-1}(B))=B$ because $\pi$ is onto. Both image and inverse image preserve inclusions.

Finally suppose $A$ is a @[two-sided ideal]mt4-ideal. For $a\in A$ and any $r+I\in R/I$, the products of $\pi(a)$ with $r+I$ on the two sides are $\pi(ra)$ and $\pi(ar)$, both in $\pi(A)$. Conversely if $B$ is a two-sided ideal, $x\in\pi^{-1}(B)$, and $r\in R$, then $\pi(rx)=\pi(r)\pi(x)$ and $\pi(xr)=\pi(x)\pi(r)$ lie in $B$. This proves absorption on both sides for $\pi^{-1}(B)$ and establishes the correspondence for ideals.
