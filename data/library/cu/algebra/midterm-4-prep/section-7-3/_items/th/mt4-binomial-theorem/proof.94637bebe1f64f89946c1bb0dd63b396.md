The case $n=0$ gives $1_R$. For $n>0$, use the @[distributive laws]mt4-ring to expand the product of $n$ copies of $a+b$. Choosing $a$ from exactly $k$ factors contributes $a^k b^{n-k}$, independent of the positions since the ring is commutative. There are $\binom{n}{k}$ such choices by @[counting subsets of a fixed size]gre:mathematics-subject-test:discrete-mathematics:th:combination-count. Adding over $k$ gives the formula in every characteristic.
