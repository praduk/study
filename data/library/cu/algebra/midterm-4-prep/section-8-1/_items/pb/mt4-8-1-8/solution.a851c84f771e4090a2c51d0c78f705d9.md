**(a)** Write $d=|D|$. In the complex plane $N(z)=|z|^2$ by @[the field norm formula]mt4-quadratic-integers-norm. For $\beta\ne0$, it suffices to approximate $z=\alpha/\beta$ by $q\in\mathcal O$ with $|z-q|^2<1$. Then $r=\alpha-q\beta$ satisfies $N(r)<N(\beta)$ by @[multiplicativity of the norm]mt4-quadratic-norm-units, as required by @[the Euclidean condition]mt4-euclidean-domain.

For $d=1,2$ the lattice has basis $1,i\sqrt d$. Round each basis coefficient of $z$ to the nearest integer. The squared error is at most $(1+d)/4<1$.

For $d=3,7,11$ the lattice has basis $1,\omega=(1+i\sqrt d)/2$. First choose the nearest horizontal lattice row, whose height is a multiple of $\sqrt d/2$. On that row, choose the lattice point nearest in real coordinate. The vertical error is at most $\sqrt d/4$ and the horizontal error at most $1/2$. Their squared sum is at most $(4+d)/16\le15/16<1$.

**(b)** Use the same half-integer basis for $d=43,67,163$. If $z=a+b\omega$ with $b\ne0$, its norm is $(a+b/2)^2+db^2/4\ge d/4>9$. Thus all elements of norm at most $9$ are ordinary integers, and the only units are $\pm1$. A @[universal side divisor]mt4-universal-side-divisor $u$ applied to $x=2$ would divide one of $1,2,3$. It is a nonunit, so it divides $2$ or $3$. In any such factorization both factors have positive integral norms at most $9$, so both are integers. Consequently $u\in\{\pm2,\pm3\}$. Applied to $x=\omega$, it would divide one of $\omega,\omega+1,\omega-1$. Each has $\omega$-coefficient $1$, impossible for a multiple of $\pm2$ or $\pm3$. The ring has nonunits (for instance $2$), so @[the universal side divisor criterion]mt4-euclidean-side-divisor excludes every Euclidean norm.
