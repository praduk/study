**(a)** An element $x$ is a common multiple exactly when $x\in(a)\cap(b)$ by @[the definition of divisibility]mt4-divisibility-gcd. An @[lcm]mt4-lcm $e$ belongs to this intersection and divides each member. Thus $(e)=(a)\cap(b)$. This is the unique largest principal ideal inside the intersection. Conversely, if $(e)$ is largest there, each $x$ in the intersection has $(x)\subseteq(e)$, so $e$ is an lcm.

**(b)** The intersection is an @[ideal]mt4-ideal, since differences and ring multiples lie in both ideals. Hence it is principal by @[the Euclidean principal ideal theorem]mt4-euclidean-principal. It contains $ab\ne0$ because a Euclidean domain is an @[integral domain]mt4-integral-domain. Each generator is an lcm by (a), and @[Exercise 7.4.8]mt4-7-4-8 shows that any two generators differ by a unit.

**(c)** Write $a=da'$, $b=db'$. By @[the Euclidean algorithm]mt4-euclidean-gcd and the uniqueness up to units in @[the gcd generator criterion]mt4-gcd-generated-pair, we may write $d=ax+by$ for this particular gcd. By @[cancellation]mt4-cancellation of $d\ne0$, this gives $1=a'x+b'y$. The element $e=da'b'=ab/d$ is a multiple of both $a$ and $b$. For any common multiple $m$, both $ma'$ and $mb'$ are multiples of $e$ because $b\mid m$ and $a\mid m$ respectively. Then $m=(ma')x+(mb')y$ is a multiple of $e$, proving the claim.
