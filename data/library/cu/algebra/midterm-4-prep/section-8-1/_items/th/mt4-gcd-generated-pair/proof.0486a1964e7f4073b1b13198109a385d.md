By the description of @[generated ideals]mt4-principal-ideal, $a,b\in(d)$ means $d$ divides both, and $d\in(a,b)$ means $d=ax+by$ for some $x,y\in R$. Since at least one of $a,b$ is nonzero, $d\ne0$. Every common divisor divides both terms in this linear combination and therefore divides $d$. This is exactly the @[gcd condition]mt4-divisibility-gcd.

If $d,d'$ are gcds of the same pair, each divides the other. Write $d=sd'$ and $d'=td$. In an integral domain, @[cancellation]mt4-cancellation of $d\ne0$ in $d=(st)d$ gives $st=1$. Thus $s$ is a @[unit]mt4-unit and $d=sd'$.
