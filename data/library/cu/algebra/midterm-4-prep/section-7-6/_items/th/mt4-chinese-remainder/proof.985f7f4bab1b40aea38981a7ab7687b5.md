Each coordinate is a quotient projection, so the map preserves both operations by @[the coordinatewise operations]mt4-direct-product-rings. It vanishes exactly on the intersection. For two comaximal ideals $A,B$, use @[comaximality]mt4-comaximal to choose $a\in A,b\in B$ with $a+b=1$. The element $br+as$ maps to $(r+A,s+B)$. Every $c\in A\cap B$ satisfies $c=ca+cb\in AB$, and $AB\subseteq A\cap B$ by ideal absorption (see @[Exercise 7.3.34]mt4-7-3-34).

For $k>2$, let $J=I_2\cdots I_k$. For each $i\ge2$ take $b_i\in I_i$ with $b_i\equiv1\pmod{I_1}$. Their product lies in $J$ and is $1$ modulo $I_1$, so $I_1+J=R$. By induction on $k$ the last $k-1$ coordinates are simultaneously attainable with kernel $J$. Apply the two-ideal case to $I_1,J$ to realize all $k$ coordinates and obtain $I_1\cap J=I_1J$. The case $k=1$ is the quotient map itself. Finally @[the first isomorphism theorem]mt4-first-isomorphism gives the displayed isomorphism.
