Each coordinate is a @[quotient projection]mt4-quotient-projection. The @[direct product operations]mt4-direct-product-rings make the combined map a ring homomorphism. Its @[kernel]mt4-ring-homomorphism is $\bigcap_i I_i$ because all coordinates must vanish.

**Two ideals.** If $A$ and $B$ are @[comaximal]mt4-comaximal, choose $a\in A,b\in B$ with $a+b=1$. For prescribed residues $r+A$ and $s+B$, set $t=br+as$. Modulo $A$ we have $a=0,b=1$, and modulo $B$ we have $a=1,b=0$. Thus $t$ has both prescribed residues. Also $AB=A\cap B$ by @[Exercise 7.3.34]mt4-7-3-34. Indeed, for $c\in A\cap B$ we have $c=ca+cb\in AB$, and @[ideal absorption]mt4-ideal puts every summand of a product in $A\cap B$.

**Any finite family.** For $k=1$ the assertion is the quotient projection itself. Proceed by @[induction]math:set:naturals-recursion:th:natural-induction, assuming the result for the last $k-1$ ideals. Their intersection equals the @[product ideal]mt4-ideal-sum-product $J=I_2\cdots I_k$. For each $i\ge2$ choose $b_i\in I_i$ with $1-b_i\in I_1$ by pairwise comaximality. Then $b_2\cdots b_k\in J$ and $b_2\cdots b_k\equiv1\pmod{I_1}$. This shows $I_1+J=R$.

Given any desired tuple, first use the induction hypothesis to find $s$ having the last $k-1$ residues. Apply the two-ideal case to choose $t$ with the first prescribed residue modulo $I_1$ and with $t\equiv s\pmod J$. Since $J\subseteq I_i$ for every $i\ge2$, $t$ has the entire tuple of residues. The two-ideal case also gives $I_1\cap J=I_1J=I_1\cdots I_k$. The map is now surjective with this kernel, so @[the first isomorphism theorem]mt4-first-isomorphism gives the claimed isomorphism.
