Put $h=1-e$. Then $h^2=h$, $eh=he=0$, and $h$ is central. For either @[central idempotent]mt4-central-idempotent $c\in\{e,h\}$, $Rc$ contains $0$, is closed under differences, and absorbs multiplication on both sides: $s(rc)=(sr)c$ and $(rc)s=(rs)c$. Thus it is a two-sided @[ideal]mt4-ideal and a subring. Moreover $c(rc)=(rc)c=rc$, so $c$ is its identity, including when the factor is the zero ring.

Define $\phi(r)=(re,rh)$ in the @[direct product]mt4-direct-product-rings of the two ideals. Addition is coordinatewise. Multiplicativity follows from $(rc)(sc)=rsc^2=rsc$. The inverse sends $(u,v)$ to $u+v$: if $u\in Re$ and $v\in Rh$, then $ue=u$, $vh=v$, and $uh=ve=0$. Conversely, $re+rh=r$. This establishes bijectivity, making $\phi$ a @[ring isomorphism]mt4-ring-isomorphism.
