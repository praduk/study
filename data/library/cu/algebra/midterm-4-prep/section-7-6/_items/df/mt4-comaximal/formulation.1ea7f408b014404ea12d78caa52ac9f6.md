Ideals $A,B$ of a commutative ring $R$ with identity are **comaximal** if $A+B=R$. By the @[definition of an ideal sum]mt4-ideal-sum-product and the @[unit ideal test]mt4-unit-ideal-test, this is equivalent to $a+b=1$ for some $a\in A$ and $b\in B$. For integer ideals $m\Z,n\Z$, this is equivalent to $\gcd(m,n)=1$ by @[Bézout’s identity]math:num:divisibility:th:bezout-euclid when $m,n$ are not both zero. For $m=n=0$ the sum is the zero ideal and $\gcd(0,0)=0$.
