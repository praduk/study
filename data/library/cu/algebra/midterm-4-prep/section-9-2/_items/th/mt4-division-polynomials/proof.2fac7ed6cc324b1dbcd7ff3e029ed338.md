If $a=0$, take $q=r=0$. If $a\ne0$ and $\deg a<\deg b$, take $q=0$ and $r=a$. Otherwise put $k=\deg a-\deg b$. The @[leading coefficient]mt4-polynomial-ring of $b$ is nonzero and hence invertible in the @[field]mt4-field $F$. Divide the leading coefficient of $a$ by that of $b$ to obtain $c$. Subtracting $cx^kb$ from $a$ cancels its leading term. Repeat on the remainder. Its degree strictly decreases at each nonzero step, so after at most $\deg a-\deg b+1$ cancellations the remainder is zero or has degree below $\deg b$. Adding the terms $cx^k$ subtracted along the way gives the quotient $q$.

For uniqueness suppose $qb+r=q'b+r'$. Then $(q-q')b=r'-r$. If $q\ne q'$, the @[degree formula over a domain]mt4-polynomial-domains makes the left side nonzero of degree at least $\deg b$, while the right side is zero or has smaller degree. Hence $q=q'$ and then $r=r'$. Taking $N(0)=0$ and $N(h)=\deg h$ for $h\ne0$ now gives the required @[Euclidean norm]mt4-euclidean-norm and @[division property]mt4-euclidean-domain.
