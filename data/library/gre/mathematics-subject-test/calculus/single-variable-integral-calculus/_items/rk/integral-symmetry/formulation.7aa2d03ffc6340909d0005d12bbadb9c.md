For integrable $f$ on $[-a,a]$, $a>0$, oddness gives integral $0$ and evenness gives $2\int_0^a f$. More generally,

$$
\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=\int_a^b f(a+b-x)\,dx.
$$

If $f(x)+f(a+b-x)=K$, the integral is $K(b-a)/2$.

**Trap.** Symmetry cannot rescue a divergent improper integral; check integrability first.
