For continuous $f$ on $[a,b]$, the accumulation $G(x)=\int_a^x f(t)\,dt$ satisfies $G'(x)=f(x)$ on $(a,b)$. If $F'=f$ on that interval and $F$ is continuous on $[a,b]$, then

$$
\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a).
$$

**Pattern.** Differentiate an accumulation function without finding an antiderivative. A definite integral is a number and has no added constant.
