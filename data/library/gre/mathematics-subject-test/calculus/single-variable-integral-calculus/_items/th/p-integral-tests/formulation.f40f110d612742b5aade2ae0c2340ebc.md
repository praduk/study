For real $p$,

$$
\int_1^\infty x^{-p}\,dx\text{ converges iff }p>1,
$$

$$
\int_0^1 x^{-p}\,dx\text{ converges iff }p<1.
$$

For $0\le f\le g$ near the troublesome endpoint, convergence of $\int g$ implies convergence of $\int f$; divergence of $\int f$ implies divergence of $\int g$. Positive finite limiting ratios give the same convergence behavior.

**Trap.** The exponent inequality reverses between zero and infinity.
