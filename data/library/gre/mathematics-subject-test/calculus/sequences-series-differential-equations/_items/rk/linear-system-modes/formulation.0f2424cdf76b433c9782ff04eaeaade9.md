For $\mathbf x'=A\mathbf x$, an eigenpair $(\lambda,\mathbf v)$ gives a complex mode $e^{\lambda t}\mathbf v$; real solutions are obtained from real/imaginary parts as needed. A basis of eigenvectors gives the full solution by superposition; defective matrices may require polynomial factors in $t$.

All eigenvalues with negative real part imply asymptotic stability; any positive real part implies instability. Opposite-sign real eigenvalues in dimension two give a saddle. For a real $2\times2$ matrix, nonzero purely imaginary eigenvalues give a center. Zero real parts in general require checking Jordan structure.

**Trap.** Eigenvalues with zero real part do not guarantee bounded solutions.
