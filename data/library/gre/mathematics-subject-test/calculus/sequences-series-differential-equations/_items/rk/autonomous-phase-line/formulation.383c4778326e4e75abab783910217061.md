For $y'=f(y)$, equilibria satisfy $f(c)=0$. When $f$ is continuously differentiable near an isolated equilibrium, inspect the sign of $f$ on either side: arrows toward the equilibrium imply local asymptotic stability; arrows away imply instability. Arrows toward on only one side indicate one-sided attraction, not two-sided stability.

If $f'(c)<0$ the equilibrium is locally asymptotically stable; if $f'(c)>0$ it is unstable. A zero derivative is inconclusive.

**Pattern.** Draw intervals separated by zeros and test one sign in each.
