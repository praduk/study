On an interval where $p,q$ are continuous, the equation $y'+p(x)y=q(x)$ has integrating factor

$$
\mu(x)=\exp\left(\int p(x)\,dx\right).
$$

Then $(\mu y)^{\prime}=\mu q$, so $y=\mu^{-1}(\int\mu q\,dx+C)$.

**Pattern.** Put the coefficient of $y'$ at $1$ before identifying $p$. This first-order formula does not directly solve arbitrary higher-order linear equations.
