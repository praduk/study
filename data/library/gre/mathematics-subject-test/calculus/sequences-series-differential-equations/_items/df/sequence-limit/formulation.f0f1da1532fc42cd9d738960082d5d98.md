A real sequence $(a_n)$ converges to $L$ iff for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $N$ such that $n\ge N$ implies $|a_n-L|<\varepsilon$. Every convergent sequence is bounded, and every subsequence converges to the same limit.

**Pattern.** Divide rational expressions by the dominant power. Two subsequences with different limits disprove convergence; boundedness alone is insufficient.
