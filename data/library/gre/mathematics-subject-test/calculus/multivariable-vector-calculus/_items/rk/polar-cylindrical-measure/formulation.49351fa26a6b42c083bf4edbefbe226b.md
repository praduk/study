With $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$, $r\ge0$,

$$
dA=r\,dr\,d\theta,\qquad dV=r\,dr\,d\theta\,dz.
$$

The second formula uses cylindrical coordinates, keeping the Cartesian height $z$.

**Pattern.** Radial integrands over disks/cylinders often simplify. Multiply by the Jacobian even after replacing $x^2+y^2$ by $r^2$. Choose angles that cover the region once.
