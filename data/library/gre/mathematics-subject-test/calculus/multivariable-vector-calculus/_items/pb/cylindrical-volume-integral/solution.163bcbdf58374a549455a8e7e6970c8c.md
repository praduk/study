**Answer.** $2\pi$.

**Pattern cue.** Separate the height integral from the disk area.

**Fast route.** In cylindrical coordinates, $\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^1\int_0^2 zr\,dz\,dr\,d\theta=2\pi\cdot(1/2)\cdot2=2\pi$. Alternatively average height $1$ times cylinder volume $2\pi$ gives the same result.

**Trap check.** The radial Jacobian remains necessary even though the integrand depends only on height.
