A real function is continuous at a point $a$ of its domain iff $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a)$, with limits taken within the domain. At an interior point, a two-sided limit exists iff both one-sided limits exist and agree.

A removable discontinuity has a finite limit but a missing or mismatched value; a jump has distinct finite one-sided limits. Continuity does not imply differentiability.
