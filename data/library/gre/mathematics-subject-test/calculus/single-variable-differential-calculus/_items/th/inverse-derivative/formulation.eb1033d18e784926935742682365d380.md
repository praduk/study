If $f$ is continuously differentiable near $a$ and $f'(a)\ne0$, it has a differentiable local inverse near $b=f(a)$ and

$$
(f^{-1})^{\prime}(b)=\frac1{f'(a)}.
$$

**Pattern.** Find the original input $a$ first; then reciprocate the derivative there. A global inverse requires injectivity on the full domain.
