If $f$ is $C^2$ near a critical point $c$ with $f'(c)=0$, then $f''(c)>0$ gives a strict local minimum and $f''(c)<0$ gives a strict local maximum. $f''(c)=0$ is inconclusive.

An inflection point requires a change of concavity across the point; under the usual convention $f$ is continuous there. A zero of $f''$ alone is insufficient.

**Pattern.** When the test degenerates, inspect the sign of $f-f(c)$ or of $f'$/$f''$ on both sides.
