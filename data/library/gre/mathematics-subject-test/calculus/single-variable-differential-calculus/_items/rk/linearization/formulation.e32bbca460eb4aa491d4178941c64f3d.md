For differentiable $f$ at $a$,

$$
f(a+h)=f(a)+f'(a)h+o(h).
$$

The linearization is $L(x)=f(a)+f'(a)(x-a)$, and the differential is $dy=f'(a)\,dx$. If $f''$ is bounded by $M$ in magnitude between $a$ and $x$, Taylor's theorem gives $|f(x)-L(x)|\le M|x-a|^2/2$.

**Trap.** $dy$ approximates the actual increment; it is not generally equal to it.
