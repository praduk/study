With $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$, one has $r^2=x^2+y^2$. Polar coordinates are not unique: angles can differ by $2\pi$, and $(-r,\theta+\pi)$ represents the same point as $(r,\theta)$.

**Pattern.** Multiply equations such as $r=a\cos\theta$ by $r$ to reveal a Cartesian circle; check the origin and the permitted angular interval when comparing loci.
