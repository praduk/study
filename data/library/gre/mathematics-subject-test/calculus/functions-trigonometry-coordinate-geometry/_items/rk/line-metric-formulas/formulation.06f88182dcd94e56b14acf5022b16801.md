For points $(x_1,y_1),(x_2,y_2)$, distance is $\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}$ and, when $x_2\ne x_1$, slope is $(y_2-y_1)/(x_2-x_1)$. For a line $ax+by+c=0$ with $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$,

$$
d((x_0,y_0),\text{line})=\frac{|ax_0+by_0+c|}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}.
$$

**Pattern.** The coefficient vector $(a,b)$ is normal; a radius at a circle's tangent point is also normal. Vertical lines have no finite slope.
