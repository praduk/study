An injective function $f:D\to\R$ has an inverse $f^{-1}:f(D)\to D$, characterized by $f^{-1}(f(x))=x$ for $x\in D$ and $f(f^{-1}(y))=y$ for $y\in f(D)$. Its domain is the original range.

**Pattern.** To find $f^{-1}(b)$, solve $f(x)=b$ on the stated domain. The notation $f^{-1}$ does not mean $1/f$. Restrict a noninjective function before inverting.
