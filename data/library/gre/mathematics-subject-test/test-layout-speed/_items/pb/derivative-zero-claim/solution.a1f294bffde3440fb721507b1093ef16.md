**Answer: D, $f(x)=x^3$.**

**Pattern cue.** Test universal claims with a small counterexample.

**Fast route.** For $x^3$, the derivative at zero is zero, but the function takes negative values immediately to the left and positive values immediately to the right. Thus zero is neither a local minimum nor a local maximum. The other four functions have a local extremum at zero.

**Trap check.** The converse of the interior-extremum derivative condition is false.
