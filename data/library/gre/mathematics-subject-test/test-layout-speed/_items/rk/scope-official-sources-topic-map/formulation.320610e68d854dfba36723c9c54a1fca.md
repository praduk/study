# Scope

This branch prepares for the **GRE Mathematics Subject Test**, not the Quantitative Reasoning measure of the GRE General Test.

All practice questions, worked solutions, summaries, and timing recommendations in this Study branch are independently authored and unofficial. They are not ETS questions and should not be presented as endorsed by ETS.

The facts below were checked against official ETS material on September 6, 2026. Test specifications and policies can change, so recheck the linked ETS pages before registering and again shortly before test day.

# Official topic map

ETS describes three broad content areas. The percentages are estimates, and ETS says the actual mix varies somewhat between editions.

- **Calculus — approximately 50 percent**
  - Differential and integral calculus of one variable
  - Differential and integral calculus of several variables
  - Calculus-based applications and connections with coordinate geometry
  - Trigonometry
  - Differential equations
  - Connections with other branches of mathematics
- **Algebra — approximately 25 percent**
  - Elementary algebra: the basic algebraic techniques and manipulations learned in high school and used throughout mathematics
  - Linear algebra: matrix algebra, systems of linear equations, vector spaces, linear transformations, characteristic polynomials, eigenvalues, and eigenvectors
  - Abstract algebra and number theory: elementary group theory, rings, modules, fields, and number theory
- **Additional topics — approximately 25 percent**
  - Introductory real analysis: sequences and series of numbers and functions, continuity, differentiability, integrability, and elementary topology of $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{R}^n$
  - Discrete mathematics: logic, set theory, combinatorics, graph theory, and algorithms
  - Other topics: general topology, geometry, complex variables, probability and statistics, and numerical analysis

If a test contained exactly 66 questions in exactly these proportions, the weights would suggest about 33 calculus questions and 16 or 17 questions in each other area. That is only a planning estimate, not a promised question count.

ETS explicitly says its topic descriptions are **not exhaustive** and that knowledge of related concepts is necessary. It also warns that questions requiring no more than a good precalculus background can be among the hardest questions on the test. The test is intended to assess understanding and application of fundamental concepts as well as recall.
