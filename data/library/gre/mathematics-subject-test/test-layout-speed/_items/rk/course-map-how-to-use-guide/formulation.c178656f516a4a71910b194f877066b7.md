## What this branch contains

This is an **unofficial, independently authored Study guide**, not an ETS publication or a replacement for official ETS preparation material. Its topic coverage and problem bank are representative rather than exhaustive; an actual test can emphasize topics differently.

The branch has four layers:

1. **3 strategy entries.** Read @gre:mathematics-subject-test:test-layout-speed:rk:scope-official-sources-topic-map for scope and provenance, @gre:mathematics-subject-test:test-layout-speed:rk:test-layout-scoring-constraints for the test interface and constraints, and @gre:mathematics-subject-test:test-layout-speed:rk:three-pass-speed-plan-practice-protocol for a pacing method.
2. **19 concise topic guides.** There are 5 calculus guides, 5 algebra guides, and 9 additional-topic guides. Each is a compact checklist of core facts, recognition cues, and common traps.
3. **19 high-density memory decks.** Each topic also has a longer reference entry that expands the formulas, hypotheses, boundary cases, and fast recognition patterns. The trigonometry deck, in particular, distinguishes supplementary-angle identities from cofunction identities and records the quadrant signs omitted by unsigned half-angle mnemonics.
4. **200 original worked problems.** The bank is weighted exactly 100 calculus, 50 algebra, and 50 additional-topic problems: 50%, 25%, and 25% of this bank. Every prompt has five choices and a separate worked solution with a fast route and trap check. These are not copied or adapted ETS questions.

Mixed Timed Sets A through K cover all 200 problems exactly once. Sets A–C contain 16 questions each; Sets D–K contain 19 questions each. Their timers scale the current official full-test time to the set length and are practice guidance, not ETS timing rules.

## An honest workflow

1. Read the three strategy entries, then decide whether you want Mixed Timed Set A to be a cold diagnostic. If so, take it before opening any topic problem or solution.
2. After a set, classify each miss by cause: missing concept, failed recognition, algebra or sign error, or pacing decision. Correcting the cause matters more than merely reading the keyed answer.
3. Use the concise topic guide to locate the smallest gap, then use the matching high-density memory deck to rebuild the relevant formulas and hypotheses. Solve the linked topic problems without looking at their solutions, and write the decisive step before checking the worked route.
4. Use Sets B through K later for mixed retrieval and pacing. Once you have studied their linked problems, treat them as speed and method checks, not as fresh score predictions.
5. Re-solve missed problems after a delay and explain why each distractor fails. Immediate familiarity is not evidence of durable mastery.
6. Use official ETS material for final calibration to current wording, interface, and full-test endurance. Even this 200-problem bank cannot establish a scaled-score prediction or guarantee complete coverage because ETS says its published topic descriptions are not exhaustive.
