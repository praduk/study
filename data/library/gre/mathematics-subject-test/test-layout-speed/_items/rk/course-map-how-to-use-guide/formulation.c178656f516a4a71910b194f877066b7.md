This is an unofficial GRE Mathematics Subject Test library. **With six hours and prior familiarity, aim to recover reliable methods and remove hesitation. The full bank is too large to finish today.** Speed means correct answers per minute, not hurried algebra.

## What the labels mean

Every concept and problem has a priority and an estimated time in its header:

- **A — core refresh:** broadly reusable knowledge or a useful speed pattern. The fixed route selects a manageable subset; it does not mean complete every A item today.
- **B — next if rusty:** use a targeted item when a miss reveals a gap, or substitute it for an already fluent block.
- **C — defer today:** lower priority under this six-hour constraint, not unimportant mathematics or a claim that it cannot appear.

Concept times estimate **refresh of familiar material**, including checking the conditions; they exclude exercises. Problem headers separate **attempt time** from **solution-check time**. Estimates and priorities are editorial planning judgments, not measurements of your ability, mastery promises, or official topic-frequency estimates. If a concept is new, these estimates do not apply.

Each concept remains a separate recall target followed immediately by its practice problems. The solutions explain the cue, a short route, and a trap. The route pages are navigation, not additional formulas to memorize.

## Test-day speed rules

- **First identify what is asked.** Avoid solving for all roots, entries, or coordinates when only a sum, product, dimension, or sign is needed.
- **Inspect the choices early.** Domain, sign, parity, scale, bounds, and dimension may eliminate most choices. Substitute surviving choices into the original equation when that is shorter.
- **Check structure before calculation.** Try a leading Taylor term, integral symmetry, triangular matrix, invariant, complement event, modular cycle, or a small counterexample when its conditions fit.
- **Check the shortcut's condition.** Do not cancel a possible zero, ignore an improper-integral singularity, assume independence, lose an endpoint, or use a theorem in the wrong field.
- **Use exact forms until approximation helps distinguish choices.** Write enough scratch work to preserve signs, restrictions, and the requested quantity.
- **Leave stalled problems.** If no productive route appears after roughly 60–90 seconds, enter a provisional answer, mark it, and move on. That is an adjustable decision rule, not a rigid time limit for every question.
- **Answer every question.** ETS weights questions equally and subtracts nothing for incorrect answers. A hard question is not worth more than an easy one.

ETS describes approximately **66 questions in 170 minutes**, about **2 minutes 35 seconds per question on average**, with approximate **50% calculus / 25% algebra / 25% additional topics** coverage. The subject blocks roughly reflect those broad weights; no official fine-grained frequency is implied.
