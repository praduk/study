Use the sheet's convention: an inner product is linear in the **first** argument and conjugate-linear in the second. For all vectors $u,v,w$ and scalars $a,b$,

$$
\langle au+bv,w\rangle
=a\langle u,w\rangle+b\langle v,w\rangle,
$$

$$
\langle u,v\rangle=\overline{\langle v,u\rangle},
\qquad
\langle v,v\rangle\ge0,
$$

and $\langle v,v\rangle=0$ exactly when $v=0$. On $\C^n$ the standard example is $\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_i x_i\overline{y_i}$.

Over $\R$, conjugation disappears. Some sources instead choose linearity in the second argument.

**Memory cue (added).** Conjugate the second slot; self-products become nonnegative.
