For $K\subseteq\mathbb R^d$,

$$
K\text{ is compact}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K\text{ is closed and bounded}.
$$

This closed-and-bounded criterion is specific to finite-dimensional Euclidean space, not every metric space.

**Memory cue (added).** In Euclidean space, compact means “closed lid, bounded box.”

**Trap (sheet, p. 30).** Closed and bounded characterizes compactness in $\R^n$, not in every metric space.
