A map $f:(X,d_X)\to(Y,d_Y)$ is uniformly continuous if for every $\epsilon>0$ there exists $\delta>0$ such that for all $x,y\in X$,

$$
d_X(x,y)<\delta\ \Longrightarrow\ d_Y(f(x),f(y))<\epsilon.
$$

**Sheet cue.** Continuity lets $\delta$ depend on the point. Uniform continuity does not.

**Memory cue (added).** One $\delta$ works everywhere.
