Functions $f_n:E\to\mathbb R$ converge uniformly to $f$ if for every $\epsilon>0$ there exists $N$ such that $n\ge N$ implies $|f_n(x)-f(x)|<\epsilon$ for every $x\in E$. Equivalently, for nonempty $E$,

$$
\sup_{x\in E}|f_n(x)-f(x)|\longrightarrow0.
$$

**Memory cue (added).** One $N$ controls every point: the worst error tends to zero.

*Source: StudySheet.pdf, pp. 22, 23.*

**Trap (sheet, p. 30).** Pointwise and uniform convergence have different quantifier order; one index must work for the entire domain in uniform convergence.
