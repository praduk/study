A sequence $(a_n)$ in a metric space $(X,d)$ is Cauchy if for every $\epsilon>0$ there exists $N$ such that for all $m,n\ge N$, $d(a_m,a_n)<\epsilon$. In $\mathbb R$, this is $|a_m-a_n|<\epsilon$.

**Memory cue (added).** Cauchy compares two late terms; convergence compares a late term with the limit.
