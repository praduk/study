A p-value is the probability, under the specified null model, of a test statistic at least as extreme as the observed one using the test's extremeness rule. It is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true, nor the probability that a result happened “by chance.” Small p-values are evidence against that model; effect size and the test assumptions remain separate questions.
