**Caps include everything:** recalling the concept, its two attempts, and a short solution comparison. For a five-minute block, aim for about one minute of recall, one minute per attempt, and two minutes to compare. Use the ranges on each item as estimates, not as extra time. Do not add theorem-proof study to this session.

**Adaptive rule:** try both drills when the concept is familiar. If a gap consumes the cap, attempt one, check the decisive step, mark the other for later, and move on. Finishing every drill is not required. If the rule and a fresh problem are immediate and correct, reclaim the remaining minutes for a weak block in this subject. Keep the subject's total fixed. A slow correct answer needs a shorter method; a fast wrong answer needs a repaired condition or setup.

## 2. Pointwise versus Uniform Convergence — 6 min

**Speed cue:** First find the pointwise limit; then check its continuity and the supremum error over the actual domain.

## 3. Negating Quantified Statements — 5 min

Concept: @gre:mathematics-subject-test:additional-topics:logic-set-theory:rk:quantifier-negation

Practice immediately after this concept:

- @gre:mathematics-subject-test:additional-topics:logic-set-theory:pb:negate-nested-quantifiers
- @gre:mathematics-subject-test:additional-topics:logic-set-theory:pb:negate-indexed-inclusion

**Speed cue:** Write quantified claims in their original nesting order; flip each quantifier and negate the final predicate. For set inclusions, first write element membership.

**Check:** Negation flips quantifiers but does not reorder them; one common witness differs from a witness allowed to depend on the index.

Cumulative subject time: **15 min**.

## 4. Ordered versus Unordered Counting — 5 min

**Speed cue:** Decide order and replacement before writing factorials; for a subset symmetry, look for a reversible pairing.

**Check:** Dividing by a factorial is valid only when every object was counted equally often.
