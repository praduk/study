For $x_{n+1}=g(x_n)$ and $g(r)=r$, a continuously differentiable $g$ with $|g'(r)|<1$ is locally attractive. A stronger global sufficient condition is that $g$ map a closed interval into itself and satisfy $|g'(x)|\le q<1$ throughout: then the fixed point is unique and every start in the interval converges. If $|g'(r)|>1$, nearby errors grow to first order.
