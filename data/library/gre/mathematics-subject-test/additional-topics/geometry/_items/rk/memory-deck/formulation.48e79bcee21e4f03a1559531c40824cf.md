## Triangles

$$
A=\frac12bh=\frac12ab\sin C,
$$

$$
A=\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)},\qquad s=\frac{a+b+c}{2}.
$$

For a right triangle, $a^2+b^2=c^2$. Memorize the primitive triples $3$-$4$-$5$, $5$-$12$-$13$, $7$-$24$-$25$, and $8$-$15$-$17$. Similar triangles preserve angles and scale lengths, areas, and volumes by $k$, $k^2$, and $k^3$.

The inradius and circumradius satisfy

$$
A=rs=\frac{abc}{4R}.
$$

## Circles

$$
C=2\pi r,\qquad A=\pi r^2,\qquad
\text{arc length}=r\theta,\qquad
\text{sector area}=\frac12r^2\theta
$$

for radians. A radius to a tangent point is perpendicular to the tangent. An inscribed angle equals half its intercepted central angle. Intersecting chords satisfy $(PA)(PB)=(PC)(PD)$; tangent-secant gives tangent length squared equal to external times whole secant.

## Coordinate and vector geometry

Use dot products for angles and orthogonality:

$$
u\cdot v=\|u\|\|v\|\cos\theta.
$$

In the plane, triangle area from vectors $u,v$ is $|\det(u,v)|/2$. In three dimensions, $\|u\times v\|$ is parallelogram area and $|u\cdot(v\times w)|$ is parallelepiped volume.

## Solids

$$
V_{\rm cylinder}=\pi r^2h,\quad
V_{\rm cone}=\frac13\pi r^2h,\quad
V_{\rm sphere}=\frac43\pi r^3,\quad
S_{\rm sphere}=4\pi r^2.
$$

## Fast checks

Figures need not be drawn to scale. Confirm radians versus degrees, distinguish perimeter from area, and use similarity before coordinates when it eliminates computation.
