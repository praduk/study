**Pattern cue.** Check direction vectors and intersection separately.

**Fast route.** $A$ has the same direction as $L$ but its $y$ coordinate is always two, so the lines are distinct and parallel. The direction $(0,1,0)$ of $B$ is orthogonal to $(1,0,0)$, and they meet at the origin, making them perpendicular. $C$ has the same orthogonal direction, but its $z$ coordinate is always one while $L$ has $z=0$; nonparallel disjoint lines in space are skew.

**Trap.** Orthogonal direction vectors do not imply that two spatial lines intersect.
