A topological space is compact iff every open cover has a finite subcover. Continuous images of compact spaces and closed subsets of compact spaces are compact. Finite unions of compact subsets are compact; arbitrary unions need not be. In the usual set theory with choice, arbitrary products of compact spaces are compact (Tychonoff's theorem).
