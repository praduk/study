## Core facts

A topology on $X$ contains $\varnothing$ and $X$, is closed under arbitrary unions, and is closed under finite intersections. A basis determines open sets as unions of basic sets. Subspace-open sets have the form $U\cap Y$ with $U$ open in $X$. Product topologies are generated by products with only finitely many nontrivial open-coordinate restrictions; quotient topologies are defined by testing inverse images under the quotient map.

The closure $\overline A$ is the smallest closed set containing $A$ and consists of points whose every neighborhood meets $A$. The interior is the largest open subset of $A$, and the boundary is $\overline A\setminus\operatorname{Int}(A)$. A set is dense when its closure is the whole space.

A map is continuous exactly when inverse images of open sets are open. Continuity preserves compactness and connectedness under images. A homeomorphism is a bijection whose map and inverse are continuous. A continuous bijection need not be a homeomorphism, but a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is one.

Compactness means every open cover has a finite subcover. In metric spaces, compactness is equivalent to sequential compactness and to completeness plus total boundedness. Arbitrary products of compact spaces are compact, while arbitrary unions of compact subsets need not be compact. Connected spaces have no separation into two nonempty disjoint open sets; path connectedness implies connectedness, but not conversely.

Know the separation hierarchy at an elementary level: $T_1$ means points are closed, Hausdorff means distinct points have disjoint neighborhoods, and metric spaces are Hausdorff. Second countability implies separability, but converses require hypotheses.

## Recognition cues

- For continuity, inverse images are usually easier than direct images.
- Compact-to-Hausdorff turns continuous bijections into homeomorphisms.
- To disprove Hausdorffness, show every two nonempty open sets intersect.
- To disprove compactness, construct an open cover whose finite subfamilies miss a point.

## Edge cases and traps

- Compact does not mean closed unless the ambient space is Hausdorff.
- Closed subsets of compact spaces are compact; compact subsets of Hausdorff spaces are closed.
- Connected does not imply path connected in arbitrary spaces.
- Sequence tests do not characterize closure or continuity in every topological space.
- The continuous image of an open set need not be open.
