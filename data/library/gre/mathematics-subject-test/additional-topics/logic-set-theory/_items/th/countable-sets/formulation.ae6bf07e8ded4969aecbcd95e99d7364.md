A set is countable if it is finite or admits an injection into $\N$. The sets $\Z,\Q$, finite products of countable sets, and finite strings over a finite alphabet are countable. Under the usual choice assumptions, a countable union of countable sets is countable. Infinite sequences over a two-symbol alphabet form an uncountable set; do not confuse them with finite strings.
