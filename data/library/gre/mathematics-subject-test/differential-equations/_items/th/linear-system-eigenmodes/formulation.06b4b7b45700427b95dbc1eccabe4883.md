For $x'(t)=Ax(t)$ with constant matrix $A$, an eigenpair $Av_i=\lambda_i v_i$ gives a solution

$$
x_i(t)=e^{\lambda_i t}v_i.
$$

If an $n\times n$ matrix has a basis of eigenvectors $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ over the working field, then

$$
x(t)=\sum_{i=1}^n C_i e^{\lambda_i t}v_i
$$

is the general solution over that field.

**Memory cue (added).** Each eigenvector keeps its direction and gets its own exponential.
