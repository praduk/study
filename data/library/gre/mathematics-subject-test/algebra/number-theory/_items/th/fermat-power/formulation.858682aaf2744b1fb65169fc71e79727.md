For a prime $p$ and integer $a$ with $p\nmid a$,

$$
a^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p.
$$

Thus powers of such $a$ may have their nonnegative exponents reduced modulo $p-1$. The equivalent statement $a^p\equiv a\pmod p$ holds for every integer $a$.

**Recognition cue.** A prime modulus suggests a period dividing $p-1$, perhaps shortened by a smaller observed cycle.

**Boundary check.** Do not infer $a^{p-1}=1$ when the base is divisible by $p$.
