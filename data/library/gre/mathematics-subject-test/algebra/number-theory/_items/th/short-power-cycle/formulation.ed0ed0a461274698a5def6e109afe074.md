Let $a$ be an integer and $n\ge2$ an integer modulus. If $a^d\equiv1\pmod n$ for positive integer $d$, then $a^{qd+r}\equiv a^r\pmod n$ for nonnegative integers $q,r$. Such an identity already implies $a$ is a unit. A discovered $d$ need not be the smallest period to be useful. Nonunits can have an initial segment before repeating.

**Recognition cue.** Check powers two or four, or a residue $-1$, before computing a large totient.

**Boundary check.** Do not assume a cycle begins at exponent zero for a nonunit.
