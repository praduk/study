Every finite field has $q=p^n$ elements for a prime $p$ and integer $n\ge1$, because it is a finite-dimensional vector space over its prime field. Its nonzero elements form a cyclic multiplicative group of order $q-1$. Thus every nonzero $a$ satisfies $a^{q-1}=1$.

**Recognition cue.** Field cardinality is a prime power; nonzero multiplicative order divides one less than it.

**Boundary check.** A ring of prime-power size is not necessarily a field.
