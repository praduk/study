For a commutative ring $R$ with identity, an ideal $I$ is an additive subgroup with $rI\subseteq I$ for every $r\in R$. This absorption condition makes addition and multiplication of cosets in $R/I$ well-defined. Every ideal is the kernel of the quotient map. In $\Z$, every ideal is $n\Z$ for a unique $n\ge0$.

**Recognition cue.** A quotient-ring question requires an ideal, not just a subring.

**Boundary check.** A subring containing one need not absorb multiplication by all ambient scalars.
