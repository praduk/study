A nonzero element $a$ of a commutative ring is a zero divisor iff $ab=0$ for some nonzero $b$. In $\Z/n\Z$, with $n\ge2$ and $[a]\ne[0]$, this holds iff $\gcd(a,n)>1$. If $d=\gcd(a,n)>1$, then $[n/d]$ is a nonzero annihilating element.

**Recognition cue.** A nontrivial common factor produces a zero-divisor witness.

**Boundary check.** Zero itself is excluded by the chosen zero-divisor convention.
