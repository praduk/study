Over a field, division by $x-a$ leaves remainder $p(a)$. In particular, $(x-a)\mid p(x)$ iff $p(a)=0$. For division by a higher-degree polynomial, the remainder has smaller degree than the divisor. Evaluating at distinct known roots of the divisor can determine its coefficients.

**Recognition cue.** A linear divisor suggests one substitution; two distinct linear factors suggest two substitutions.

**Boundary check.** A degree-two divisor generally has a linear remainder, not a constant.
