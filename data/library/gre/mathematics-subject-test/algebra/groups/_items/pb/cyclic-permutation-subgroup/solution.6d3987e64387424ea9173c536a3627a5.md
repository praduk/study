**Fastest valid route.** List the four powers of $g=(1\ 2\ 3\ 4)$:

$$g^0=e,\qquad g^1=(1\ 2\ 3\ 4),\qquad g^2=(1\ 3)(2\ 4),\qquad g^3=(1\ 4\ 3\ 2).$$

Only choice (B) appears. The answer is **(B)**.

A quick structural check is that a square of a $4$-cycle must be a product of two disjoint transpositions pairing opposite points.
