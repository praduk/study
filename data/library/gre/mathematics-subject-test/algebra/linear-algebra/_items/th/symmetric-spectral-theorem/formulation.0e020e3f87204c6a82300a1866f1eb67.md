Every real symmetric matrix has real eigenvalues and an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors. Thus $A=QDQ^T$ for orthogonal $Q$ and real diagonal $D$. Eigenspaces for distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal. For $\norm{x}=1$, $x^TAx$ lies between the smallest and largest eigenvalues, with both endpoints attained.

**Recognition cue.** A symmetric quadratic form can be bounded by its eigenvalues.

**Boundary check.** A general real matrix need not have real eigenvalues or an orthonormal eigenbasis.
