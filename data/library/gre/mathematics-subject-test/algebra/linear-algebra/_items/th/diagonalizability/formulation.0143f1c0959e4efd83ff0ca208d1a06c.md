An $n\times n$ matrix over $F$ is diagonalizable over $F$ iff it has a basis of eigenvectors in $F^n$. Equivalently, $\chi_A$ splits over $F$ and each eigenvalue's geometric multiplicity $\dim\ker(A-\lambda I)$ equals its algebraic multiplicity. For an eigenvalue, geometric multiplicity is at least one and at most algebraic multiplicity. Having $n$ distinct eigenvalues in $F$ is sufficient.

**Recognition cue.** Repeated eigenvalues require an eigenspace count, not an automatic rejection.

**Boundary check.** The base field matters: real rotations can diagonalize over $\C$ but not $\R$.
