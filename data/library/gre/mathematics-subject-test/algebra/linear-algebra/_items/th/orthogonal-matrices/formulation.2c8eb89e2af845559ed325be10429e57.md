A real square matrix $Q$ is orthogonal iff $Q^TQ=I$, equivalently $Q^{-1}=Q^T$. It preserves norms and inner products, $\det Q=\pm1$, and every complex eigenvalue has modulus one.

**Recognition cue.** A rotation or reflection preserves lengths without explicit coordinate expansion.

**Boundary check.** Determinant one alone does not imply orthogonality, and a real orthogonal matrix can have nonreal eigenvalues.
