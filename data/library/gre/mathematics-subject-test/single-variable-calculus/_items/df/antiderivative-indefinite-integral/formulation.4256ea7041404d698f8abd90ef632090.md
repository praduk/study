An antiderivative of $f$ on an interval $I$ is a differentiable function $F$ satisfying $F'(x)=f(x)$ for every $x\in I$. If one such $F$ exists, all antiderivatives on that interval are

$$
\int f(x)\,dx=F(x)+C,
$$

where $C$ is an arbitrary constant. On disconnected domains, each interval can have its own constant.

**Memory cue (added).** Differentiate to check; the constant vanishes. An indefinite integral names a family, not a single number.
