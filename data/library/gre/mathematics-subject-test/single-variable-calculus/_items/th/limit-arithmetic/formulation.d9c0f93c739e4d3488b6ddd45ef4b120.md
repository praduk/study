For the same limiting approach, suppose $f(x)\to L$ and $g(x)\to M$, with $L,M$ finite. Then

$$
f+g\to L+M,\qquad fg\to LM.
$$

If $M\ne0$, then $f/g\to L/M$. These rules do not directly evaluate indeterminate combinations such as $0/0$.

**Memory cue (added).** Pass a finite limit through arithmetic; division needs a nonzero limiting denominator.
