For a one-sided limit at a finite endpoint or at infinity, suppose $f,g$ are differentiable nearby, $g$ and $g'$ are nonzero there, and the quotient has form $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$ (both magnitudes diverge in the latter case). If

$$
\lim\frac{f'}{g'}=L
$$

exists as a finite or extended-real limit, then $\lim f/g=L$. A two-sided limit requires the corresponding conclusion from both sides.

The sheet’s warning remains: first verify the indeterminate form. Differentiate numerator and denominator separately, not the quotient as a whole.
