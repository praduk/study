Let $u=g(x)$, so $du=g'(x)\,dx$. For a continuous $f$ and $C^1$ function $g$ on the relevant intervals,

$$
\int f(g(x))g'(x)\,dx=\int f(u)\,du.
$$

On the right, substitute back after finding an antiderivative. For a definite integral, transform the endpoints as well.

**Memory cue (added).** Replace the inside and its differential together.
