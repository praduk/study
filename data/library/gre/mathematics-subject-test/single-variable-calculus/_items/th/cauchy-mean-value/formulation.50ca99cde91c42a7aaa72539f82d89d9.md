Let $a<b$. If $f,g$ are continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable on $(a,b)$, some $c\in(a,b)$ satisfies

$$
(f(b)-f(a))g'(c)=(g(b)-g(a))f'(c).
$$

This cross-multiplied form requires no division by $g'(c)$ or $g(b)-g(a)$.

**Memory cue (added).** Two functions, cross-multiply their total changes with their instantaneous rates.
