For real $\alpha$ and $|x|<1$,

$$
(1+x)^\alpha=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{\alpha}{n}x^n,
$$

where $\binom{\alpha}{0}=1$ and, for $n\ge1$,

$$
\binom{\alpha}{n}
=\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)\cdots(\alpha-n+1)}{n!}.
$$

If $\alpha$ is a nonnegative integer, the series terminates and becomes a polynomial identity for all real $x$. For other $\alpha$, endpoints require separate analysis.

**Memory cue (added).** Fall from $\alpha$ for $n$ factors, divide by $n!$.
