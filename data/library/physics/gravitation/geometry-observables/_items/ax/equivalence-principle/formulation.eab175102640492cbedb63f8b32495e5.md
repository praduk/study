The weak equivalence principle models sufficiently small, freely falling, uncharged test bodies as following the same trajectory for the same initial position and velocity, independent of composition. The Einstein equivalence principle further models local nongravitational experiments in a freely falling frame by special relativity, in the limit that the laboratory is small compared with gravitational variation scales.

Finite-size tidal effects, spin-curvature coupling, non-gravitational forces, and self-gravity lie outside this elementary test-body approximation. A local inertial frame can remove connection coefficients at one event, but cannot generally remove curvature. Coordinate covariance by itself does not select Einstein's field equation or prohibit extra curvature couplings in matter actions. These are physical principles and model choices, not deductions from changing coordinates.
