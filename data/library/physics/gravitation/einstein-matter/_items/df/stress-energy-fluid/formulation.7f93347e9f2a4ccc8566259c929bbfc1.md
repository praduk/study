A symmetric stress-energy tensor records energy density, momentum density, and stresses. An observer $U$ measures $\epsilon_U=T_{\mu\nu}U^\mu U^\nu/c^2$. A perfect fluid is the isotropic rest-frame model

$$
T^{\mu\nu}=\frac{\epsilon+p}{c^2}U^\mu U^\nu+p g^{\mu\nu},\qquad U\cdot U=-c^2.
$$

Here $\epsilon$ is rest-frame energy density and $p$ pressure, both in $\mathrm{J/m^3}=\mathrm{Pa}$. Rest-frame orthonormal components are $\operatorname{diag}(\epsilon,p,p,p)$ and the trace is $-\epsilon+3p$. An equation of state and fluid evolution are additional inputs. Dust means negligible pressure; it is not a universal model near shocks or multistream regions.
