This course assumes @physics:relativity:rk:course-guide and graduate differential geometry. It introduces the physical interpretation of the Lorentzian metric and Einstein's equations, then studies test-body motion and selected spacetime models. Work the numbered topics in order. Every problem has a complete solution, and every theorem proves the stated mathematical consequence. Large background calculations used without derivation, such as the Schwarzschild vacuum solution and the Friedmann reduction, are explicitly remarks.

Use signature $(-+++)$ and $x^0=ct$ in length-based charts. Charts displaying $t$ directly write $-c^2dt^2$ explicitly. Greek indices range from $0$ to $3$. The connection is always Levi–Civita unless stated otherwise. Our convention agrees with @math:diffgeo:curvature:df:curvature.

$$
R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z.
$$

Components are $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\partial_\rho=R(\partial_\mu,\partial_\nu)\partial_\sigma$, with $R_{\sigma\nu}=R^\rho{}_{\sigma\rho\nu}$. Einstein's equation is $G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}/c^4$. Here $G$ without indices is Newton's gravitational constant, $G_{\mu\nu}$ is the Einstein tensor, and $\Lambda$ has units inverse length squared. Fluid $\epsilon$ is energy density, while $\rho=\epsilon/c^2$ is mass-equivalent density.

Original explanations, proofs, and calculations were checked against Sean Carroll's author-hosted notes: [gravitation and action](https://www.preposterousuniverse.com/wp-content/uploads/grnotes-four.pdf), [weak fields and gravitational radiation](https://www.preposterousuniverse.com/wp-content/uploads/grnotes-six.pdf), [Schwarzschild geometry](https://www.preposterousuniverse.com/wp-content/uploads/grnotes-seven.pdf), and [cosmology](https://www.preposterousuniverse.com/wp-content/uploads/grnotes-eight.pdf). These 1997 notes support classical equations, not current observational parameters. This course distinguishes local from global energy and does not infer topology from curvature sign. It does not cover the full Einstein initial-value theorem, singularity theorems, or quantum gravity.
