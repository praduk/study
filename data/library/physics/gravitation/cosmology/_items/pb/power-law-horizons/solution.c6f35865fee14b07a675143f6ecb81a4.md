The past integral is $(c/A)\int_0^t(t')^{-q}dt'$, finite precisely for $0<q<1$. Then $d_{\rm particle}=ct/(1-q)$. It diverges for $q\geq1$, logarithmically at one.

The future integral is $(c/A)\int_t^\infty(t')^{-q}dt'$, finite precisely for $q>1$, giving $d_{\rm event}=ct/(q-1)$. It diverges for $0<q\leq1$. Meanwhile $H=q/t$ yields Hubble radius $ct/q$ for every $q>0$, including $q=1$ when neither horizon exists. For radiation $q=1/2$ it happens to equal the particle-horizon distance, but this is not general. The answers assume the power law persists to both temporal boundaries.
