The static spherically symmetric asymptotically flat vacuum solution with $\Lambda=0$ and mass parameter $M>0$ is, in its exterior static chart,

$$
ds^2=-f(r)c^2dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{f(r)}+r^2(d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2),\qquad f(r)=1-\frac{r_s}{r},\quad r_s=\frac{2GM}{c^2}.
$$

Here $r>r_s$ and $r$ is areal radius: symmetry spheres have area $4\pi r^2$. The vacuum solution and its extension across $r_s$ are source-backed results used without deriving the full field-equation solution. In the black-hole extension the horizon is a regular null surface. The curvature invariant $R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}R^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=12r_s^2/r^6$ is finite there and diverges at $r=0$.

For a spherical star this metric describes vacuum outside the stellar surface. It does not assert every object of mass $M$ has a black-hole horizon. Rotating bodies require a different exterior model.
