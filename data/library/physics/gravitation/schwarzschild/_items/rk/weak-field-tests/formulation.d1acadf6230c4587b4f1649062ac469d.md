For a weak-field nearly Keplerian bound orbit with semimajor axis $a$ and eccentricity $0<e_{\rm orb}<1$, leading Schwarzschild periapsis advance per orbit is $6\pi GM/[a(1-e_{\rm orb}^2)c^2]$. A null ray passing an isolated weak mass at asymptotic impact parameter $b\gg GM/c^2$ has leading deflection $4GM/(bc^2)$. These are asymptotic approximations, not exact formulas at all radii. The next problem derives the orbital secular term; the light-deflection asymptotics are a source-backed result used without full derivation.

The null effective potential is proportional to $f/r^2$; its derivative vanishes at $r=3GM/c^2$, the unstable circular photon orbit. Redshift, light bending, orbital precession, and propagation-time delay test different observables. Actual comparisons must include other bodies, spin, plasma propagation, and instrumental effects when relevant.
