A circular solution needs $V_\ell'=0$ and $e^2c^2=V_\ell$. Differentiation gives $V_\ell'=c^2r_s/r^2-2\ell^2/r^3+3r_s\ell^2/r^4$. Solving gives $\ell^2=c^2r_sr^2/(2r-3r_s)$, positive precisely for $r>3r_s/2$. There $f>0$ and $V_\ell>0$, so choosing future-directed $e=\sqrt{V_\ell}/c$ constructs the circular solution.

At fixed $\ell$, substitution into the second derivative gives

$$
V_\ell''=\frac{2c^2r_s(r-3r_s)}{r^3(2r-3r_s)}.
$$

The denominator is positive in the existence range. Thus the sign changes at $r=3r_s=6GM/c^2$. The linearized radial equation $\delta\ddot r=-V_\ell''\delta r/2$ has oscillatory disturbances at a strict minimum and exponential ones at a strict maximum. At equality the linear frequency vanishes; the statement does not assert a strict minimum there.
