The lapses are $N_A=\sqrt3/2$ and $N_B=2\sqrt2/3$. Thus $\Delta\tau_B/\Delta\tau_A=N_B/N_A=4\sqrt2/(3\sqrt3)>1$. The outward light signal has $\omega_B/\omega_A=N_A/N_B=3\sqrt3/(4\sqrt2)<1$, a redshift. These reciprocal ratios provide a check.

Both observers are static rather than geodesic and need support because the lapse gradients are nonzero. An orbiting clock has spatial motion, so its proper-time rate must be obtained from its full tangent and the metric. Using only the static lapse omits that motion.
