For $\mathbf e=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,0)$, contraction gives $h_{ij}e^ie^j=h_+\cos2\theta$. Thus $\delta L/L=(h_0/2)\cos\omega t\cos2\theta$. The $x$ and $y$ radii change by $+h_+/2$ and $-h_+/2$, so fractional differential change of equal orthogonal arms is $h_+$.

For cross polarization the contraction is $h_\times\sin2\theta$, with principal directions rotated $45$ degrees relative to plus. First-order area change vanishes because the transverse trace is zero. These statements use linear perturbation theory and a detector much smaller than wavelength.
