Special relativity models nongravitational physics in inertial frames by the same laws, with a universal vacuum light speed $c$. An inertial frame uses freely moving reference bodies and a convention of Einstein synchronization: a reflected light signal sent at clock reading $t_1$ and received at $t_2$ assigns the remote reflection time $(t_1+t_2)/2$.

The flat-spacetime model takes inertial coordinates to be related by Poincaré transformations, and ideal clocks to measure proper time. The clock hypothesis extends this rule to accelerated ideal clocks: acceleration affects their elapsed time through the worldline, with no additional acceleration term. Real clocks can have acceleration-dependent instrumental errors. These are physical modeling assumptions, not theorems about arbitrary clocks.
