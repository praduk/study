Rapidity $\chi\in\R$ parametrizes a boost by $v/c=\tanh\chi$, so $\gamma=\cosh\chi$ and $\gamma v/c=\sinh\chi$. Its time-space block is

$$
\begin{pmatrix}\cosh\chi&-\sinh\chi\\-\sinh\chi&\cosh\chi\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Multiplication and the hyperbolic addition formulas show that collinear boosts add rapidities. This converts the bounded speed interval $(-c,c)$ to the additive real line. Boosts in different directions generally do not commute; their composition can include a spatial rotation. The collinear addition rule must not be generalized by vector addition of rapidities.

The future unit timelike vectors form the upper sheet of the hyperboloid $g(u,u)=-1$, with its positive induced metric. In a timelike two-plane, $u(\eta)=(\cosh\eta,\sinh\eta)$ satisfies $g(du/d\eta,du/d\eta)=1$. Rapidity is therefore intrinsic arc length along this hyperbolic line. The linear maps preserving $g$ form a @math:diffgeo:geometric-structures:df:lie-group; the one-parameter boost subgroup adds rapidities. This does not make noncollinear boosts commute.
