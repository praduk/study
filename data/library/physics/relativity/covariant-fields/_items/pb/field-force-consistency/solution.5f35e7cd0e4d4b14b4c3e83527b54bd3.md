The charge has $\mathbf u'=-v\hat{\mathbf x}$, $E'_y=\gamma E$, and $B'_z=-\gamma vE/c^2$. Since $\hat{\mathbf x}\times\hat{\mathbf z}=-\hat{\mathbf y}$, $(\mathbf u'\times\mathbf B')_y=-\gamma v^2E/c^2$. Therefore $f'_y=q\gamma E(1-v^2/c^2)=qE/\gamma$.

Initially in $S$, $K=(0,0,qE,0)$ because the charge is at rest. An $x$ boost leaves its $y$ component unchanged, so $K'^y=qE$. In $S'$ the charge's Lorentz factor is $\gamma$, and $K'^y=\gamma f'_y$, recovering $qE/\gamma$. Electric and magnetic forces combine to preserve the covariant dynamics.
