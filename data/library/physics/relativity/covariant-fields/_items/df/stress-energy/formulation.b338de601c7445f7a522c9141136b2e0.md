A symmetric stress-energy tensor $T^{\mu\nu}$ packages local energy and momentum: in an inertial frame $T^{00}$ is energy density, $cT^{0i}$ is energy flux, and $T^{ij}$ is flux of $i$-momentum in direction $j$. In an isolated smooth system local balance is modeled by $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0$; integrated conserved quantities also require vanishing boundary flux.

For electromagnetism in the present SI convention,

$$
T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm EM}=\frac1{\mu_0}\left(F^{\mu\alpha}F^{\nu}{}_{\alpha}-\frac14\eta^{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}\right).
$$

Its components are $T^{00}=u$, $T^{0i}=S_i/c$, and $T^{ij}=-\sigma_{ij}$ with the vacuum energy, Poynting vector, and Maxwell stress of electromagnetism. For a particle, energy measured by observer $U$ is $E_U=-p\cdot U$. For a continuum its measured energy density is $T_{\mu\nu}U^\mu U^\nu/c^2$.
