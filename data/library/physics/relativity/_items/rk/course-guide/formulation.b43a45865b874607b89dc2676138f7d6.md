Read this course after the first field-and-source topic of @physics:electromagnetism:rk:course-guide; its final topic returns to those Maxwell equations. Linear algebra, multivariable calculus, and tensors are assumed. The physical starting points are inertial-frame equivalence, a universal vacuum light speed, clock behavior, and energy-momentum dynamics. Lorentzian geometry organizes these assumptions but does not prove their empirical validity.

Coordinates are $x^\mu=(ct,x,y,z)$, with Greek indices from $0$ to $3$ and spatial indices from $1$ to $3$. The metric is $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$; $v\cdot w$ means $\eta_{\mu\nu}v^\mu w^\nu$. Repeated upper/lower index pairs are summed. We use passive boosts: frame $S'$ moves at velocity $+v$ along $x$ relative to $S$. Four-momentum is $(E/c,\mathbf p)$, and $c$ is explicit throughout. Spatial boldface dots and norms are Euclidean. All particles discussed as massive have positive invariant mass and future-directed timelike worldlines.

Each theorem is a mathematical consequence of its displayed hypotheses and has a complete proof. Physical assumptions are axioms or remarks. Problems separate an observer's coordinate measurements from measurements made by a single clock. Acceleration is allowed in flat spacetime; gravitational dynamics begin in @physics:gravitation:rk:course-guide.

Original exposition and worked derivations were checked against Sean Carroll's author-hosted [Special Relativity and Flat Spacetime notes](https://www.preposterousuniverse.com/wp-content/uploads/grnotes-one.pdf), particularly the interval, Lorentz tensors, four-velocity, stress-energy, and electromagnetic field tensor. The source frequently sets $c=1$ and uses Gaussian electromagnetic units; this course restores $c$ and uses SI consistently.

**Geometric route.** Use @physics:relativity:observer-geometry:df:observer-fields and @physics:relativity:observer-geometry:th:observer-reconstruction to interpret electric and magnetic fields as observer-dependent parts of one two-form. The causal and metric prerequisites are @math:diffgeo:geometric-structures:df:lorentzian-causality and @math:diffgeo:geometric-structures:df:pseudo-metric.
