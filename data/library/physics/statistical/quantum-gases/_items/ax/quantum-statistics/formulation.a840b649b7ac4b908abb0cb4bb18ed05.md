For ordinary identical particles in three spatial dimensions, the nonrelativistic quantum model restricts many-particle states to the symmetric sector for bosons or the antisymmetric sector for fermions. For a noninteracting Hamiltonian diagonal in one-particle modes $r$, the allowed occupation numbers are $n_r=0,1,\ldots$ for bosons and $n_r=0,1$ for fermions, with energy $\sum_r\epsilon_rn_r$. A mode includes spin and every other one-particle label. The statistics assignment is a physical postulate here; invariance of position probabilities alone does not prove that these are the only possible representations.
