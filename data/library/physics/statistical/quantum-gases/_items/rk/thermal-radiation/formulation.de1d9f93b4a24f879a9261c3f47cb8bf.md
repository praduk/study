The free electromagnetic field in a large cavity has two transverse polarizations with $\epsilon=\hbar c k$. Equilibrium absorption and emission do not conserve photon number, so the thermal model uses $\mu=0$. Counting modes gives the thermal spectral energy density


$$
u_\omega(T)=\frac{\hbar\omega^3}{\pi^2c^3}\frac1{e^{\hbar\omega/(k_BT)}-1}.
$$


The exponential suppresses large frequencies. Assigning $k_BT$ to every continuum mode instead gives $u_\omega\propto\omega^2T$ and a divergent integral. The thermal spectrum excludes the vacuum zero-point contribution; this convention does not solve gravitational vacuum-energy questions.
