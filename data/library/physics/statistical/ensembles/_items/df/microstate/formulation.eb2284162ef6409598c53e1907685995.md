A microscopic model specifies a measurable state space $\Gamma$, a Hamiltonian energy $H$, and physical observables $A:\Gamma\to\mathbb R$. A macrostate specifies controlled quantities and a probability measure $\rho$ on $\Gamma$; predictions are ensemble averages $\langle A\rangle=\int A\,d\rho$ when integrable. In a quantum discrete model, states can instead be labeled by energy eigenvectors and probabilities. A trajectory, a preparation ensemble, and a coarse-grained macrostate are different objects. The same mean energy does not determine the full energy distribution.
