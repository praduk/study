For energies $E_i$ independent of temperature, the canonical model at $T>0$ assigns


$$
p_i=\frac{e^{-\beta E_i}}{Z},\qquad Z=\sum_i e^{-\beta E_i},\qquad \beta=\frac1{k_BT}.
$$


Assume $0<Z<\infty$. These weights model weak coupling to an equilibrium heat bath; the subsystem may fluctuate in energy. Formally, bath state counting gives $\log\Omega_B(E_{\rm tot}-E_i)\approx\text{constant}-\beta E_i$ when the bath's entropy is nearly affine over the exchanged-energy range. Neglected curvature is a finite-reservoir correction, not an exact identity for every bath.

For a finite state set these weights form the one-parameter exponential family in @physics:statistical:geometry:df:exponential-family, with natural parameter $-\beta$ and sufficient observable $E$. The normalizing log partition generates means, fluctuations, and a metric on distinguishable equilibrium distributions. This is mathematical structure of the chosen weights; it does not remove the weak-bath modeling assumption that selected them.
