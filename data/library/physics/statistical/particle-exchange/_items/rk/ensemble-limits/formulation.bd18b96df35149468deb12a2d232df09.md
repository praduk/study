A thermodynamic limit usually means $N,V\to\infty$ with density $N/V$ fixed and boundary contributions subextensive. Equivalence asks whether specified macroscopic or local predictions agree between ensembles after matching their intensive variables. It is not equality of their finite probability measures: canonical energy fluctuates and microcanonical energy is constrained. Concavity, suitable short-range interactions, and the choice of observable matter; coexistence and long-range interactions can obstruct naive statements. The Poisson gas calculation proves one concentration example. A general interacting equivalence theorem is outside this course.
