Set $b=\beta a/2>0$. The normalized marginal is proportional to $e^{-b x^2}$. Integration of $(xe^{-b x^2})'=e^{-b x^2}-2b x^2e^{-b x^2}$ over $\mathbb R$ has zero boundary term, so the second-moment integral is $1/(2b)$ times the normalization integral. Therefore $\langle ax^2/2\rangle=a/(4b)=1/(2\beta)$. Discrete quantum levels and bounded or coupled coordinates do not satisfy these particular hypotheses.
