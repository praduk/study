A point source of strength $q$ at $a\in\R^3$ is represented by $q\delta_a$, meaning $\langle q\delta_a,f\rangle=qf(a)$ for a smooth @[compactly supported]math:diffgeo:manifolds:df:partition-unity test function $f$. Distributional derivatives are defined by integration by parts, for example $\langle\partial_i T,f\rangle=-\langle T,\partial_i f\rangle$.

A delta source is not an ordinary function with an infinite value at one point. An equation with a delta is tested after smearing. Products of general distributions require additional definitions and cannot be manipulated as ordinary products.

The general definition, topology on test functions, and distributional derivative are in @math:analysis:distributions-sobolev:df:distribution. Weak derivatives and finite-energy function spaces are introduced in @math:analysis:distributions-sobolev:df:sobolev. These definitions distinguish a point-source equation from an equality of ordinary functions at the singular point.
