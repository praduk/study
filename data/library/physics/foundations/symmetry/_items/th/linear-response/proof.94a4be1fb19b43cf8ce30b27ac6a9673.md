Multiplying the equation by $e^{-At}$ and integrating gives the first formula; differentiation verifies it. Jordan normal form gives $\|e^{Ar}\|\le C e^{-\alpha r}$ for $r\ge0$ and some $C,\alpha>0$, so the improper integral converges uniformly under bounded input and is bounded. The difference of two bounded solutions is $e^{A(t-t_0)}v$. If it were nonzero, boundedness in the remote past would fail: $v=e^{AR}x(t_0-R)$ tends to zero as $R\to\infty$ for bounded $x$. Thus $v=0$.
