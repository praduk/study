A quantity is written $q=\{q\}_u u$, where $u$ is its unit and $\{q\}_u$ its numerical value. A dimension records how the unit scales under changes of independent base units. In mechanics write $[q]=M^aL^bT^d$, using mass, length, and time dimensions.

Only quantities of the same kind and dimension may be added. Equal dimensions alone do not imply identical physical meaning: energy and torque both have dimension $ML^2T^{-2}$. A dimensionless ratio such as $v/c$ can be an argument of an exponential or a logarithm; a dimensional quantity requires an explicitly specified reference scale.
