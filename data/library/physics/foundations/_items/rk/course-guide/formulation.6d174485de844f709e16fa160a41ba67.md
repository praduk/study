The prerequisite is the existing Mathematics curriculum, @math:rk:course-map. The new task is to attach mathematical objects to preparations, measurements, scales, and idealizations. A mathematically consistent model can still fail to describe an experiment.

Start here for quantity dimensions, model assumptions, conservation laws, boundary data, uncertainty, and controlled approximations. Then read @physics:classical:rk:course-guide. This course supplies a language for the other Physics courses; it is not a course in experimental apparatus or a replacement for laboratory experience.

**Conventions.** Use SI unless a lesson explicitly declares natural units. $c$ is the speed of light in vacuum, $\hbar=h/(2\pi)$ is the reduced Planck constant, and $k_B$ converts temperature to energy. In relativity use metric signature $(-,+,+,+)$ and $x^0=ct$. Thermodynamic heat into the system and work done by the system are positive. Quantum inner products are conjugate-linear in the first argument. Each model states any additional convention it needs.

**Status of entries.** Axioms record physical postulates or model assumptions, not truths deducible from mathematics. Theorems are conditional mathematical consequences. Remarks explain interpretation, scope, or approximation. Problems ask for a prediction or diagnostic and contain separate solutions.

**Inspected sources.** The [BIPM SI Brochure](https://www.bipm.org/en/publications/si-brochure) supplies the unit conventions. [Burgess's effective-field-theory review](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0701053) supplies context for scale separation. The algebraic examples and arguments below are internal derivations, with all assumptions stated.
