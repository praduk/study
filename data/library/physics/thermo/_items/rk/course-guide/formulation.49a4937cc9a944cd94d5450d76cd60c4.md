Assume the Mathematics library. Connect state spaces, differential forms, convexity, and Legendre transforms with temperature, pressure, heat, work, and phase equilibrium. Read in numerical order. Every theorem has a full conditional derivation; empirical laws and constitutive models are identified separately.

Conventions: SI units; $N$ counts particles, $k_B$ is Boltzmann's constant, and $\mu$ is energy per particle. Heat entering and work done by the system are positive: $dU=\delta Q-\delta W$ for a closed stationary system. Ordinary positive-temperature equilibrium is assumed. A simple compressible equilibrium substance obeys $dU=T\,dS-p\,dV+\mu\,dN$. Irreversible processes need not trace equilibrium states.

Inspected author sources: Rafael Jaramillo, MIT [first law](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-020-thermodynamics-of-materials-spring-2021/mit3_020s21_l03.pdf), [second law](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-020-thermodynamics-of-materials-spring-2021/mit3_020s21_l05.pdf), [potentials](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-020-thermodynamics-of-materials-spring-2021/mit3_020s21_l06.pdf), and [coexistence](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-020-thermodynamics-of-materials-spring-2021/mit3_020s21_l11.pdf). Those notes use work on the system; this course converts that sign. Derivations and exercises are independently authored.

This finite core does not cover all reacting mixtures, interfaces, magnetic systems, or transport. Microscopic interpretation follows in @physics:statistical:rk:course-guide; thermodynamic laws are physical assumptions, not probability theorems alone.
