The enclosure receives no heat and the gas pushes against zero external pressure: $Q=W=0$. The first law gives $\Delta U=0$, so $cNk_B\Delta T=0$ and the final temperature is unchanged. The equilibrium entropy difference is $Nk_B\log(V_2/V_1)>0$. A reversible isothermal expansion instead produces $W=\int_{V_1}^{V_2}Nk_BT\,dV/V=Nk_BT\log(V_2/V_1)$ and needs $Q=W$ to maintain constant $U$. The entropy change is the same state difference, but its origin is reversible heat input in the second process and entropy production in the first.
