For the perfect-equilibrium-crystal convention with no residual ground-state degeneracy, take the entropy reference so that $S(T)\to0$ as $T\downarrow0$ at fixed composition and fixed admissible external constraints. The associated Nernst condition says entropy differences between such equilibrium states tend to zero.

This is additional physical input, independent of the first and second laws. It is not a claim that every quantum Hamiltonian has a unique ground state. Degenerate ground states, frozen disorder, glasses, and interchanging zero-temperature and thermodynamic limits require separate treatment. No equivalence with finite-time unattainability is assumed here.
