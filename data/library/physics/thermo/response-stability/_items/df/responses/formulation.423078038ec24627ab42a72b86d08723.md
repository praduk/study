On a smooth single-phase branch at fixed particle count $N$, define


$$
C_V=\left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial T}\right)_{V,N}
$$

$$
C_p=\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial T}\right)_{p,N}
$$

$$
\alpha=\frac1V\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial T}\right)_{p,N}
$$

$$
\kappa_T=-\frac1V\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial p}\right)_{T,N}.
$$


$C_V,C_p$ have units J/K, $\alpha$ has units $\mathrm{K}^{-1}$, and $\kappa_T$ has units $\mathrm{Pa}^{-1}$. They describe measurable small changes, not universal material constants. A stable ordinary bulk phase normally has positive $C_V$ and $\kappa_T$; singularities, constraints, and nonadditive systems require separate analysis.
