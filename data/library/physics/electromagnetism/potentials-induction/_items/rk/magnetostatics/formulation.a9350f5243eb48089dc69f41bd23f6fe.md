For stationary sources and fields, Maxwell's equations reduce to $\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mu_0\mathbf j$ and $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$, requiring $\nabla\cdot\mathbf j=0$. Current must return somewhere; an isolated finite segment with a steady current is incomplete source data.

For smooth localized divergence-free currents in otherwise empty space, the decaying Coulomb-gauge solution is represented by $\mathbf A(\mathbf x)=\mu_0(4\pi)^{-1}\int\mathbf j(\mathbf y)/|\mathbf x-\mathbf y|\,d^3y$ and $\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A$. Curling the kernel gives the Biot–Savart integrand $\mu_0\mathbf j(\mathbf y)\times(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)/(4\pi|\mathbf x-\mathbf y|^3)$. This Green-function representation needs its decay and regularity hypotheses; external magnetic fields can be added when boundary data permit.
