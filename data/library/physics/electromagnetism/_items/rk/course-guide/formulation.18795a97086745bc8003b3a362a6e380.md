This course treats classical electromagnetism as a physical model whose differential equations can be studied rigorously. Electric and magnetic fields are inferred from forces and induction; a solution is meaningful only after sources, initial data, boundary conditions, and a material model have been specified. Read the numbered topics in order. Work each problem before opening its solution.

Assume vector calculus, distributions, linear PDE, and differential forms. The integral identities are instances of @math:diffgeo:stokes-cohomology:th:stokes. Spacetime notation and moving observers are developed in @physics:relativity:rk:course-guide. Here space is oriented Euclidean three-space, $t$ is time in seconds, and bold letters denote spatial vectors. SI constants satisfy $c^2=1/(\epsilon_0\mu_0)$. Sources $\rho,\mathbf j$ are total charge and current unless explicitly called free sources. Point sources and surface sheets require distributional equations or jump conditions; smooth proofs do not automatically apply at their singularities.

Physical laws and constitutive assumptions are marked as axioms or remarks. Theorems prove exact consequences under stated regularity assumptions; the radiation formulas are explicitly approximations. The course does not establish microscopic matter models or point-charge self-force dynamics.

The exposition, proofs, and worked problems are original derivations. Inspected author sources for physical conventions and further reading are Richard Fitzpatrick's University of Texas notes: [potential formulation](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/em/lectures/node47.html), [retarded potentials](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/em/lectures/node50.html), [electrostatic uniqueness](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/em/lectures/node62.html), [energy conservation](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/em/lectures/node89.html), [momentum conservation](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/em/lectures/node91.html), [dielectric interfaces](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/em/lectures/node70.html), [reflection](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/em/lectures/node104.html), and [electric dipole radiation](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/em/lectures/node95.html). Some notation and hypotheses here deliberately differ: Lorenz gauge retains residual gauge freedom, and interface equations include free surface sources.
