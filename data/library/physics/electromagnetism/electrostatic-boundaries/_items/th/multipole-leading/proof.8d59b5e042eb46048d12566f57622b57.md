Taylor-expand $f(\mathbf y)=|\mathbf x-\mathbf y|^{-1}$ at zero. Its value is $1/r$ and its gradient there is $\mathbf x/r^3$. Every Hessian component is bounded by $C/r^3$ for $|\mathbf y|\leq a<r/2$, since $|\mathbf x-\mathbf y|\geq r/2$. The second-order remainder is therefore bounded by $C|\mathbf y|^2/r^3$. Integrate the expansion against $\rho$ and bound the error by its integral against $|\rho|$. Finally $\int(\mathbf y-\mathbf b)\rho=\mathbf p-Q\mathbf b$. A neutral distribution has origin-independent dipole moment; a charged one generally does not.
