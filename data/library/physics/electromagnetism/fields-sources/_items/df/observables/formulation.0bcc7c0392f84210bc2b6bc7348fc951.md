A charge $q$ is measured in coulombs. A continuum charge density $\rho(\mathbf x,t)$ has units $\mathrm{C/m^3}$, and current density $\mathbf j$ has units $\mathrm{A/m^2}$: $\int_S\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf n\,dS$ is charge per second crossing an oriented fixed surface. For a single transported continuum, $\mathbf j=\rho\mathbf v$; several species contribute additively.

The electric field $\mathbf E$ has units $\mathrm{N/C}=\mathrm{V/m}$ and magnetic field $\mathbf B$ units tesla. Operationally they are the velocity-independent and velocity-dependent coefficients in the force on a sufficiently weak test charge. A test body is assumed small enough to sample a field and weak enough that its backreaction can be neglected. This is a model limit, not an assertion that an actual measuring instrument leaves the field unchanged.
