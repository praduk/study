A symmetry of the source does not by itself remove homogeneous solutions generated by boundary or initial data. Symmetry reductions also require symmetric data or a uniqueness argument. An infinite wire describes a central region of a long wire, and a plane wave describes a region of a much broader beam; neither has finite total energy over all space.

A point charge has $\rho=q\delta_0$ and Coulomb field $q\mathbf x/(4\pi\epsilon_0|\mathbf x|^3)$. Its divergence vanishes away from zero but has distributional integral $q/\epsilon_0$ across a surrounding sphere. Its near-source field energy diverges as $\int_0^a r^{-2}dr$. Thus inserting its own field into a smooth force-density formula is not justified.
