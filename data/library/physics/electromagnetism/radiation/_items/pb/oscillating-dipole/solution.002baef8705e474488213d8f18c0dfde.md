The acceleration amplitude is $-\omega^2p_0\hat{\mathbf z}$ and its squared sinusoid has mean $\omega^4p_0^2/2$. The angular factor from transverse projection is $\sin^2\theta$. Therefore

$$
\left\langle\frac{dP}{d\Omega}\right\rangle=\frac{\omega^4p_0^2}{32\pi^2\epsilon_0c^3}\sin^2\theta,\qquad
\langle P\rangle=\frac{\omega^4p_0^2}{12\pi\epsilon_0c^3}.
$$

There are zeros on the dipole axis and maxima in its equatorial plane. The source must be much smaller than its wavelength, its relevant motion nonrelativistic, and the observation point in the radiation zone. The result is the leading classical electric-dipole contribution, not a claim that every oscillating microscopic dipole can be treated classically.
