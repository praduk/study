For a localized nonrelativistic source with dipole $\mathbf p(t)=\int\mathbf x\rho(\mathbf x,t)d^3x$, the leading electric-dipole radiation field at retarded time $t_r=t-r/c$ is

$$
\mathbf E_{\rm rad}=\frac{\mathbf n\times(\mathbf n\times\ddot{\mathbf p}(t_r))}{4\pi\epsilon_0c^2r},\qquad
\mathbf B_{\rm rad}=\frac1c\mathbf n\times\mathbf E_{\rm rad}.
$$

Here $\mathbf n=\mathbf x/r$, and dots are time derivatives. These expressions retain the leading $1/r$ term under the small-source and radiation-zone assumptions; they are not exact fields at finite distance. Their sign follows by taking minus the time derivative of the transverse retarded vector potential. Magnetic-dipole and higher electric multipoles may matter when the electric-dipole contribution vanishes. The Green-function asymptotics are a source-backed result used without a full derivation.
