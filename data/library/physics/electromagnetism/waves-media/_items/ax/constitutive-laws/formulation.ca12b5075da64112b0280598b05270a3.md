In a homogeneous, isotropic, linear, local, nondispersive material at rest, one may model $\mathbf D=\epsilon\mathbf E$ and $\mathbf B=\mu\mathbf H$ with constants $\epsilon,\mu>0$. This gives wave speed $v=1/\sqrt{\epsilon\mu}$ and impedance $Z=\sqrt{\mu/\epsilon}$. The lossless stored-energy expression is $(\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf D+\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf H)/2$ in this restricted model.

Ohmic conduction adds $\mathbf j_f=\sigma\mathbf E$ with $\sigma\geq0$ and dissipation $\sigma|\mathbf E|^2$. Real materials can be anisotropic, frequency-dependent, nonlinear, or hysteretic. A general linear response is a time convolution, constrained by causality; a measured phase velocity in a dispersive band is not automatically a signal speed. The simple energy expression must not be applied indiscriminately to dispersive or dissipative matter.
