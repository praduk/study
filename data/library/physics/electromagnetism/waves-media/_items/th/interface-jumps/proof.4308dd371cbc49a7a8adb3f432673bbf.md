Integrate the two divergence equations over a thin pillbox crossing the interface. As its thickness tends to zero, the side flux vanishes, and its top-minus-bottom flux per area is the normal jump. Free volume charge contributes only its surface-sheet integral in this limit, giving the first two equations.

Integrate Faraday and Ampère–Maxwell around a thin rectangle crossing the interface. The two short sides vanish in the limit. Finite time derivatives of the fields contribute vanishing flux through its shrinking area. Faraday therefore gives zero tangential electric jump; the free sheet current contributes a finite term to Ampère's law. With the right-hand orientation, that term is exactly $\mathbf n\times(\mathbf H_2-\mathbf H_1)=\mathbf K_f$. Doing this for every tangential rectangle direction gives the vector equations.
