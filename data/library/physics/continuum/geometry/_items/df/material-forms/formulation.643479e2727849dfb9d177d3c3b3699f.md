Let $(M,g)$ be an oriented Riemannian spatial manifold with fixed volume form $\mu=\operatorname{vol}_g$. A smooth flow map $\Phi_t$ carries material particles with velocity $v_t$. A time-dependent @[differential form]math:diffgeo:forms:df:forms-pullback $\alpha_t$ is advected when $\Phi_t^*\alpha_t=\alpha_0$. By @math:diffgeo:geometric-structures:th:transport-form this is equivalent to $\partial_t\alpha_t+\mathcal L_{v_t}\alpha_t=0$ wherever the flow is defined.

The mass form is $m_t=\rho_t\mu$, where $\rho_t>0$ is mass per metric volume. Material mass conservation means that this top-degree form is advected. Define the velocity one-form $u=v^\flat=g(v,\cdot)$, its circulation around an oriented closed curve $C$ by $\oint_Cu$, and the vorticity two-form by $\Omega=du$. In Euclidean three-space $\Omega=\iota_{\nabla\times v}\mu$; the vector curl is obtained from the two-form using metric and orientation.

Advection is a pullback statement, so the appropriate local derivative depends on tensor type. For a scalar $f$, $\mathcal L_vf=v(f)$; for a one-form, $(\mathcal L_vu)_i=v^j\partial_ju_i+u_j\partial_iv^j$. The second term is essential and follows by differentiating the pullback's Jacobian. Reusing the scalar material derivative component by component would omit it.
