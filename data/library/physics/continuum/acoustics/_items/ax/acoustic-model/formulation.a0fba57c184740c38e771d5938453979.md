Consider an inviscid fluid at rest with uniform density $\rho_0>0$ and pressure $p_0$. For perturbations small enough to neglect their products, assume the constitutive response $p'=c^2\rho'$ with constant $c>0$. Linear mass and momentum balances are

$$
\partial_t\rho'+\rho_0\operatorname{div}v=0,\qquad \rho_0\partial_tv=-\nabla p'.
$$

Here primes mean perturbations, not derivatives. For rapid sound with negligible heat exchange, $c^2$ is the pressure derivative with respect to density at constant entropy per mass. The constitutive derivative and its physical regime are input; the equations do not derive that derivative from mechanics alone.
