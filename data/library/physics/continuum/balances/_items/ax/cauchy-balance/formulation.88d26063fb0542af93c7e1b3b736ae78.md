A classical nonpolar continuum models the contact force per current area on a surface of outward unit normal $n$ by $t(n)=\sigma n$, where $\sigma$ is the Cauchy stress. With body force per mass $b$ in $\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, postulate

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\int_{B_t}\rho v\,dx=\int_{B_t}\rho b\,dx+\int_{\partial B_t}\sigma n\,dA.
$$

Stress and traction have units $\mathrm{Pa}=\mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}$. Positive normal stress is tensile. In this model there are no independent couple stresses or intrinsic spin. These assumptions matter for the subsequent symmetry of $\sigma$; generalized continua can carry additional angular-momentum fluxes.
