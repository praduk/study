Under smooth mass conservation and the Cauchy force-balance model on every material subregion,

$$
\rho D_tv=\operatorname{div}\sigma+\rho b.
$$

Equivalently $\partial_t(\rho v_i)+\partial_j(\rho v_iv_j)=\partial_j\sigma_{ij}+\rho b_i$.

In arbitrary coordinates on Euclidean space, the invariant acceleration is $\partial_tv+\nabla_vv$ for the Levi-Civita connection. If stress is written as a contravariant two-tensor, its divergence has components $\nabla_j\sigma^{ij}$; Cartesian coordinates reduce this to the expression above. Stress must be transformed as a tensor before taking a covariant divergence. Extension to a genuinely curved material space additionally specifies its metric and physical constitutive model, rather than changing only derivative symbols.
