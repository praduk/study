Change variables in the mass integral to get $\int_{B_0}\rho(\chi,t)J\,dX$. Its derivative is $\int_{B_0}J(D_t\rho+\rho\operatorname{div}v)\circ\chi\,dX$ by the material-derivative and Jacobian identities. Vanishing on every subregion forces the continuous integrand to vanish, since otherwise a small ball of one sign contradicts the integral. This is $D_t\rho+\rho\operatorname{div}v=0$, which expands to the displayed equation. Conversely it makes all mass derivatives zero. The same identity is precisely $\partial_t(\rho\circ\chi\,J)=0$. If $D_t\rho=0$ and $\rho>0$, divide by $\rho$ to get the last claim.
