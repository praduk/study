The derivative of $\int_{B_t}\rho(x\times v)dx$ is $\int_{B_t}\rho x\times D_tv\,dx$, because $v\times v=0$. Insert local linear momentum. For component $k$, the divergence theorem applied to $\epsilon_{k\ell m}x_\ell\sigma_{mj}$ gives the surface torque as the volume integral of $[x\times\operatorname{div}\sigma]_k+\epsilon_{kjm}\sigma_{mj}$. Thus angular balance is equivalent, for every region, to $\epsilon_{kjm}\sigma_{mj}=0$ for $k=1,2,3$. These three equations are the vanishing of the three independent antisymmetric stress components. They are equivalent to symmetry, and the calculation reverses for the converse.
