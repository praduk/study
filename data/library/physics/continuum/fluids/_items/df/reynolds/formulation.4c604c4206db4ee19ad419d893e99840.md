Choose characteristic speed $U>0$, length $L>0$, and constant fluid properties $\rho,\eta>0$. The Reynolds number is $\mathrm{Re}=\rho UL/\eta=UL/\nu$. With $x=Lx'$, $t=(L/U)t'$, $v=Uv'$, and $p=\rho U^2p'$, the dimensionless momentum equation has viscosity coefficient $1/\mathrm{Re}$.

This ratio compares an inertial scale $\rho U^2/L$ with a viscous scale $\eta U/L^2$. Small Reynolds number motivates neglecting inertia only when the chosen velocity and time scales support that balance. Large Reynolds number alone does not prove turbulence or permit deletion of wall boundary layers.
