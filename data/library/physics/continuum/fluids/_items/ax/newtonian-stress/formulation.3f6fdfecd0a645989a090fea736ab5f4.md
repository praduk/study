An isotropic Newtonian fluid with local rate of strain $D=(\nabla v+(\nabla v)^T)/2$ is modeled by

$$
\sigma=-p\mathbf1+2\eta D+\zeta(\operatorname{div}v)\mathbf1,
$$

where $\eta$ is shear viscosity and $\zeta$ is the second viscosity coefficient. Both have units $\mathrm{Pa\,s}$; the bulk viscosity is $\zeta+2\eta/3$. The coefficients can depend on the thermodynamic state. For constant-density incompressible flow $\operatorname{div}v=0$ and $p$ acts as the constraint pressure.

Linearity in strain rate and local isotropy are material assumptions, not identities for every fluid. Setting viscosity to zero defines the inviscid Euler model; it does not justify ignoring boundary layers in every small-viscosity flow.
