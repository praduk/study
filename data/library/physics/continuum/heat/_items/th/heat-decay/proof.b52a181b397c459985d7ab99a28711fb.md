Multiply the PDE by $\theta$ and integrate. Integration by parts gives $\alpha\int_{\partial B}\theta\partial_n\theta\,dA-\alpha\int_B|\nabla\theta|^2dx$; either boundary condition makes the surface term vanish. Under insulation, integrating the PDE without a multiplier gives $d\int_B\theta/dt=\alpha\int_{\partial B}\partial_n\theta=0$. The difference of two solutions obeys the homogeneous equation and boundary condition with initially zero squared norm. Nonnegativity and monotonicity force that norm to remain zero, hence the smooth difference vanishes.
