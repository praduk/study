For a stationary solid with constant density $\rho>0$ and heat capacity per mass $c_h>0$ appropriate to the imposed mechanical constraints, assume thermal energy changes per volume as $\rho c_h\partial_tT$. Fourier's constitutive law is $q=-k\nabla T$ with constant $k>0$. Local energy balance is

$$
\rho c_h\partial_tT=-\operatorname{div}q+r.
$$

Temperature $T$ is in K, conductivity $k$ in $\mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$, heat flux $q$ in $\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$, heat capacity $c_h$ in $\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$, and source $r$ in $\mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}$. The symbol $c_h$ does not assert equality of the thermodynamic constant-pressure and constant-volume heat capacities. This model neglects motion, thermal expansion work, latent heat, temperature-dependent coefficients, and finite relaxation of heat flux.
