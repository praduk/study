A time-independent closed-system Hamiltonian is modeled by a self-adjoint operator $H$, with evolution $U(t)=e^{-itH/\hbar}$ defined by its spectral calculus. The physical postulate is $|\psi(t)\rangle=U(t)|\psi(0)\rangle$. This preserves norm. For initial vectors in $D(H)$, the evolution is strongly differentiable and satisfies $i\hbar\,\dot\psi=H\psi$; arbitrary Hilbert-space states need not have a strong time derivative. The functional-calculus facts are mathematical inputs here. Time-dependent unbounded Hamiltonians need additional common-domain and regularity hypotheses, so an ordinary exponential is not asserted for them.

For finite-dimensional $\mathcal H$, @physics:quantum:geometry:th:projective-dynamics identifies the projected evolution with Hamiltonian flow of the energy expectation on projective state space. The removed vertical component of $\dot\psi$ is the dynamical phase. Parameter-dependent eigenspaces carry the separate @physics:quantum:geometry:df:berry-connection; identifying its holonomy with adiabatic dynamics requires gap and slow-variation control beyond unitary evolution alone.
