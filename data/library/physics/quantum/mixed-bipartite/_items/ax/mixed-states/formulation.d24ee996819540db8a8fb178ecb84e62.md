A finite-dimensional quantum preparation is represented by a positive semidefinite matrix $\rho$ with $\operatorname{tr}\rho=1$. A projective outcome $P$ has probability $\operatorname{tr}(\rho P)$ and an observable has expectation $\operatorname{tr}(\rho A)$. Pure states have $\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$. Combining distinguishable subsystems uses $\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B$, with local observables $A\otimes I$ and $I\otimes B$. These are physical composition and probability postulates. A classical ensemble of pure preparations gives $\rho=\sum_jp_j|\psi_j\rangle\langle\psi_j|$, and that decomposition is generally not unique.
