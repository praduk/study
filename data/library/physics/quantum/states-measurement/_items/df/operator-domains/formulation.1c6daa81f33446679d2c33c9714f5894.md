An unbounded operator means a linear map $A:D(A)\subset\mathcal H\to\mathcal H$ with its domain included in the specification. For a dense domain, symmetry means $\langle\phi|A\psi\rangle=\langle A\phi|\psi\rangle$ for $\phi,\psi\in D(A)$; self-adjointness means $A=A^*$ including equality of domains. Boundary terms vanishing on test functions prove symmetry on those functions, not self-adjointness. For example, the differential expression $-i\hbar\,d/dx$ on an interval needs boundary conditions to specify an operator. Products require $\psi\in D(AB)$, meaning $\psi\in D(B)$ and $B\psi\in D(A)$.
