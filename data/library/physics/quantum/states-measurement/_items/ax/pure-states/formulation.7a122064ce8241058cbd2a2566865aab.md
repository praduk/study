A pure preparation is represented by a ray in a complex Hilbert space $\mathcal H$: normalized vectors $|\psi\rangle$ and $e^{i\theta}|\psi\rangle$ represent the same state. Relative phases between components can affect measurement probabilities. In a position representation for a spinless particle, $\mathcal H=L^2(\mathbb R^d)$ and $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle=\int\overline{\phi(x)}\psi(x)\,dx$. The ray assignment is a physical postulate, not a consequence of Hilbert-space axioms. Normalizable states need not lie in the domain of every unbounded observable.
