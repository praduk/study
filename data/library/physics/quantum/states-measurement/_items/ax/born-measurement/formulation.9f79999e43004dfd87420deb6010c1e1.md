A sharp real-valued observable is modeled by a self-adjoint operator $A$ with spectral projection measure $\Pi_A$. For a normalized pure state and Borel set $B\subset\mathbb R$, the model predicts


$$
\Pr(A\in B)=\langle\psi|\Pi_A(B)\psi\rangle.
$$


For a discrete outcome $a$ with positive probability and projector $P_a$, an ideal selective projective measurement leaves $P_a\psi/\|P_a\psi\|$. This specifies an ideal instrument, not every laboratory measurement. Continuous spectra are handled by the projection measure; a plane wave is not a normalized Hilbert-space eigenvector.

In finite dimension, a rank-one projector $|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|$ gives probability $|\langle\phi,\psi\rangle|^2$, depending only on the two rays. For a horizontal tangent $\xi$ at unit $\psi$, normalize $\phi(\varepsilon)=(\psi+\varepsilon\xi)/\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^2\|\xi\|^2}$. Then $1-|\langle\psi,\phi(\varepsilon)\rangle|^2=\varepsilon^2\|\xi\|^2+O(\varepsilon^4)$. Thus the infinitesimal overlap loss is the squared Fubini–Study length in @physics:quantum:geometry:df:projective-state-space, derived directly from this measurement model.
