The radial probability density is $4r^2e^{-2r/a}/a^3$. Using $\int_0^\infty r^n e^{-br}dr=n!/b^{n+1}$, its integral is one and its first moment is $4(3!)/(a^3(2/a)^4)=3a/2$. For $r>0$, $\Delta e^{-r/a}=(a^{-2}-2/(ar))e^{-r/a}$. Hence


$$
H\psi=\left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_ra^2}+\frac{\hbar^2/(m_ra)-\kappa}{r}\right]\psi=-\frac{m_r\kappa^2}{2\hbar^2}\psi.
$$


There is no point delta term: the flux of the bounded radial derivative through a sphere of radius $\varepsilon$ tends to zero with its area. The function and its weak Laplacian lie in $L^2$, so it belongs to the usual Coulomb operator domain and verifies the eigenvalue equation there.
