With an infinitely heavy point nucleus, a nonrelativistic $N$-electron model includes one-electron kinetic and nuclear-attraction terms plus


$$
\sum_{i<j}\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0|\mathbf r_i-\mathbf r_j|}.
$$


The complete wavefunction is antisymmetric in combined position-and-spin labels. A Slater determinant enforces this and vanishes when two occupied spin orbitals coincide. A single determinant is an approximation to a correlated interacting eigenstate; an electron configuration does not specify every level energy. Screening, exchange, and correlations modify hydrogenic ordering, while spin-orbit terms further split levels. Spectroscopic transitions require coupling to light as well as energy differences.
