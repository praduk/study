For a spinless particle of mass $m>0$ in a prescribed real scalar potential $V$, the nonrelativistic model uses the expression $H=-(\hbar^2/2m)d^2/dx^2+V(x)$ with a specified self-adjoint realization. Kinetic energy is quadratic in momentum; relativistic corrections, spin, radiation, and back-reaction of a dynamical source are absent. A finite potential step imposes continuity of both $\psi$ and $\psi'$ for the ordinary constant-mass model. Singular interactions instead require separately specified matching conditions. Ideal walls and delta interactions are useful limiting models, not literal microscopic potentials.
