A spin-one-half space is $\mathbb C^2$ with $S_i=(\hbar/2)\sigma_i$, where


$$
\sigma_x=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
\sigma_y=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
\sigma_z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
$$


For a unit real vector $\mathbf n$, measuring $S_{\mathbf n}=(\hbar/2)\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$ has outcomes $\pm\hbar/2$ and projectors $(I\pm\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)/2$. These statements follow from $(\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)^2=I$, checked by $\sigma_i\sigma_j=\delta_{ij}I+i\epsilon_{ijk}\sigma_k$. The physical magnetic moment has an additional charge- and species-dependent coefficient; it is not universally equal to spin.

Pure qubit rays form $\CP^1$, identified with the unit Bloch vector $\mathbf n$ through the rank-one projector $(I+\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)/2$. @physics:quantum:geometry:pb:spin-berry-sphere calculates its Fubini–Study metric and Berry curvature explicitly. The Bloch-sphere representation applies to the ray, while the spinor itself retains a choice of phase lift.
