Define $Kf(x)=C\int_B e^{ik|x-y|}|x-y|^{-1}V(y)f(y)\,dy$ for $x\in B$. The supremum over $x\in B$ of $\int_B|x-y|^{-1}dy$ is finite: if the ball has radius $R$, it is bounded by $\int_{|u|\le2R}|u|^{-1}du=8\pi R^2$. Thus $K$ is bounded on $L^\infty(B)$. If $|\lambda|\|K\|<1$, the geometric operator series gives $(I+\lambda K)^{-1}$, proving existence, uniqueness, and $\psi_\lambda=e^{ikz}+O(\lambda)$ in that norm. For $x=r\mathbf n$ and bounded $y$, $|x-y|=r-\mathbf n\cdot y+O(r^{-1})$ and $|x-y|^{-1}=r^{-1}+O(r^{-2})$, uniformly in angle. Substitution into the outside integral gives $f_\lambda=-\lambda C\int_Be^{-ik\mathbf n\cdot y}V(y)\psi_\lambda(y)\,dy$. Replacing $\psi_\lambda$ by its norm expansion yields the asserted uniform $O(\lambda^2)$ error. This theorem treats the stated integral model and does not prove general scattering completeness.
