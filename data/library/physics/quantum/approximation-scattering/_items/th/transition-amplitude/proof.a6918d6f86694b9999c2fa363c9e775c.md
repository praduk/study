Set $\psi_I(s)=e^{iH_0s/\hbar}\psi(s)$. Differentiation cancels the $H_0$ terms and gives $\dot\psi_I=-(i\lambda/\hbar)V_I(s)\psi_I$, where $V_I=e^{iH_0s/\hbar}Ve^{-iH_0s/\hbar}$. Its integral equation is $\psi_I(t)=|n\rangle-(i\lambda/\hbar)\int_0^tV_I(s)\psi_I(s)\,ds$. A continuous finite matrix coefficient gives differentiable dependence on $\lambda$ by the uniformly convergent iterated integral series on a finite interval. Differentiating at zero replaces $\psi_I(s)$ by $|n\rangle$. Projecting onto $\langle m|$ proves the formula. It is a finite-time expansion coefficient, not an irreversible constant transition rate.
