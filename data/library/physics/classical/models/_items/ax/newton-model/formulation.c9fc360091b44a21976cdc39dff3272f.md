An inertial frame is a frame in which an isolated particle has constant velocity. The Newtonian model postulates absolute time and, for fixed positive masses,

$$
m_a\ddot x_a=F_a(x_1,\ldots,x_N,v_1,\ldots,v_N,t).
$$

Force is measured in $\mathrm N=\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}$. The force functions are physical input, not consequences of this differential equation. Galilean changes $x'=Rx+ut+b$, $t'=t+t_0$, with constant $R\in\SO(3)$, preserve accelerations up to $R$.

For an isolated pair-interaction model we additionally assume $F_{ab}=-F_{ba}$; angular-momentum conservation also requires these pair forces to lie along $x_a-x_b$. This does not describe arbitrary interactions after their fields or environment have been discarded. The model and its regime are empirical premises.
