For a body mass measure $dm$ about its center of mass, define the constant body inertia tensor

$$
I=\int (|a|^2\mathbf1-aa^T)\,dm.
$$

It is symmetric and positive semidefinite. Rotational kinetic energy is $T=\omega^TI\omega/2$ and body angular momentum is $L_b=I\omega$: these follow from $|\omega\times a|^2$ and $a\times(\omega\times a)=|a|^2\omega-(a\cdot\omega)a$. The eigenvectors are principal axes and their eigenvalues are principal moments. Moments have units $\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. A mass distribution on a line can have a zero principal moment; a general three-dimensional body does not.
