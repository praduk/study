For $q_\epsilon=q+\epsilon\eta$, differentiation under the integral gives $\delta S=\int(L_{q^i}\eta^i+L_{\dot q^i}\dot\eta^i)dt$. Integrate the second term by parts; its endpoint term is zero. Hence $\delta S=\int(L_{q^i}-dL_{\dot q^i}/dt)\eta^i dt$. If a continuous coefficient were positive or negative at an interior point, a nonnegative smooth bump in that coordinate supported in a sufficiently small neighborhood would give a nonzero integral. Therefore every coefficient vanishes; continuity also gives endpoint values. Conversely the displayed equations make this expression zero for every allowed variation.
