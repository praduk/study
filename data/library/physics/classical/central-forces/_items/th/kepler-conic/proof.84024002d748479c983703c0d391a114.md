Put $u=1/r$. Since $\dot\phi=\ell u^2/\mu$, differentiation gives $\dot r=-(\ell/\mu)u'$ and $\ddot r=-(\ell^2/\mu^2)u^2u''$. Substitute in the radial equation to obtain $u''+u=\mu\kappa/\ell^2$. Its general solution is $u=(1+e\cos(\phi-\phi_0))/p$, choosing $e\ge0$ by a phase shift. Energy becomes $E=\ell^2(u'^2+u^2)/(2\mu)-\kappa u=\mu\kappa^2(e^2-1)/(2\ell^2)$. For $e<1$, the polar equation gives an ellipse and its extreme radii are $p/(1\pm e)$. Half their sum is $a=p/(1-e^2)=-\kappa/(2E)$.
