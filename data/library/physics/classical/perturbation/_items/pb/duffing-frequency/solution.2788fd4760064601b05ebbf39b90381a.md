Divide by $A\Omega_0^2$ and expand to first order. The equation for $y_1$ is $y_1''+y_1=2\nu\cos\tau-\cos^3\tau=(2\nu-3/4)\cos\tau-(1/4)\cos3\tau$. Resonance is absent when $\nu=3/8$. Then $y_1=(\cos3\tau-\cos\tau)/32$ satisfies both the equation and the initial data. Thus the formal frequency is $\Omega_0[1+3\epsilon/8]$. Substitution into the original differential operator leaves a bounded residual of order $A\Omega_0^2\epsilon^2$ for small $\epsilon$, since the omitted terms are products of bounded trigonometric functions. The initial displacement and velocity are exact for this approximation. A small residual is not a proof of a uniformly small trajectory error over arbitrarily long times. Positive $\alpha$ raises this first frequency correction; $[\alpha]=\mathrm{m^{-2}\,s^{-2}}$.
