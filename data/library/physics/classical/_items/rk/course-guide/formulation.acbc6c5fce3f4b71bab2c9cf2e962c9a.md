This course assumes the Mathematics collection, including differential equations, differential geometry, and linear algebra, but introduces physical states, forces, energy, and measurement units. It proceeds from Newtonian particle models to variational mechanics, symplectic evolution, central forces, rigid bodies, normal modes, and a first controlled use of perturbation expansions. Mathematical familiarity does not establish a physical law: the axioms here specify empirical models, while theorem entries derive consequences conditional on those models.

Use SI: positions in metres, time in seconds, mass in kilograms, force in newtons, energy in joules, and action in joule-seconds. Angular coordinates are dimensionless radians. Generalized coordinates need not have the units of length; their conjugate momenta have units of action divided by the coordinate's units. Smoothness is assumed wherever derivatives are used. Newtonian descriptions concern nonrelativistic macroscopic motion with the specified forces; constraints and rigid bodies are idealizations.

Our canonical convention is $\omega=\sum_i dq^i\wedge dp_i$, $i_{X_H}\omega=dH$, and $\{f,g\}=\sum_i(f_{q^i}g_{p_i}-f_{p_i}g_{q^i})$, so $\dot f=\partial_t f+\{f,H\}$. This sign convention is fixed throughout. Useful mathematics: @math:diffeq:existence:th:picard-lindelof and @math:diffgeo:forms:df:interior-lie.

Inspected authorities for scope and model conventions are David Tong's author-hosted Classical Dynamics chapters [Newtonian mechanics](https://davidtong.org/teaching/classical-dynamics/dynhtml/S1), [Lagrangian mechanics](https://davidtong.org/teaching/classical-dynamics/dynhtml/S2), [rigid bodies](https://davidtong.org/teaching/classical-dynamics/dynhtml/S3), and [Hamiltonian mechanics](https://davidtong.org/teaching/classical-dynamics/dynhtml/S4). The derivations and exercises here are independently constructed, not a reproduction of the notes. Nonholonomic mechanics, KAM theory, detailed celestial perturbations, and rigorous long-time averaging are beyond this course. The perturbation section explicitly separates formal residual cancellation from an error theorem.

The geometric route begins with @physics:classical:geometry:df:kinetic-metric and @physics:classical:geometry:df:moment-map. Newton's law becomes covariant acceleration, canonical phase space is a cotangent bundle, and symmetry produces momentum maps. @physics:classical:geometry:pb:rotation-momentum checks the construction on angular momentum. The coordinate calculations remain available as local realizations of these structures.
