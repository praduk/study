Differentiate $H=p_iv^i-L(q,v,t)$. Since $L_{v^i}=p_i$, terms containing $dv^i$ cancel, leaving $dH=v^i dp_i-L_{q^i}dq^i-L_tdt$. Therefore $H_p=v$ and $H_q=-L_q$. Euler–Lagrange is precisely $\dot p=L_q$, giving the equations in both directions after the local inverse map. Along a Hamilton trajectory, the chain rule gives $dH/dt=H_q\cdot H_p-H_p\cdot H_q+H_t=H_t$. The time coefficient of the differential gives $H_t=-L_t$.
