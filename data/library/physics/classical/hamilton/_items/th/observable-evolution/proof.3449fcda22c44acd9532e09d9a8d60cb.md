The first identity follows by substituting Hamilton's equations into the chain rule. To prove the second without appealing to an unstated bracket identity, let $\Phi_t$ be the Hamilton flow. For a covector $\beta$, define $X_\beta$ by $i_{X_\beta}\omega=\beta$. Preservation of $\omega$ preserves the induced pairing $(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\alpha(X_\beta)$, which is exactly the displayed canonical bracket: $\{f,g\}\circ\Phi_t=\{f\circ\Phi_t,g\circ\Phi_t\}$. This follows by inserting the derivatives of the pullbacks in that pairing and using $\Phi_t^*\omega=\omega$. The first identity gives $f\circ\Phi_t=f$ and $g\circ\Phi_t=g$. Therefore $\{f,g\}\circ\Phi_t=\{f,g\}$, as required.
