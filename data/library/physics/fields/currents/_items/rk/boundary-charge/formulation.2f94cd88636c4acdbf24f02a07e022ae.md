From $\partial_t j^0+\nabla\cdot\mathbf j=0$, the charge in a fixed volume is $Q_V=\int_Vj^0\,d^3x$ and $\dot Q_V=-\int_{\partial V}\mathbf j\cdot n\,dA$. It is constant when the boundary flux vanishes. For all of space one also needs convergence and sufficient decay to pass to the infinite-volume limit.

Changing a current by a divergence can change boundary terms even if the bulk continuity equation is unchanged. This matters for charges at spatial infinity and for gauge transformations acting nontrivially on a boundary.
