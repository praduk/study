This is a first bridge from mechanics and quantum theory to fields, using the Mathematics library's analysis, geometry, and algebra. Read @physics:classical:rk:course-guide, @physics:relativity:rk:course-guide, @physics:quantum:rk:course-guide, and @physics:electromagnetism:rk:course-guide first. Statistical mechanics supplies a complementary interpretation of Gaussian integrals and scale dependence.

The route is local action principles, currents, free fields, regulated quantization, spinors and connections, perturbation theory, and effective descriptions. Work the finite-mode and finite-dimensional examples before taking continuum formulas literally. Unless stated otherwise this course uses $c=\hbar=1$, $x^0=t$, and $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$. Greek indices run from $0$ to $3$ and repeated upper/lower indices are contracted. $\Box=\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu=-\partial_t^2+\Delta$. Mass then has inverse-length dimension.

Classical variational identities and finite-dimensional algebra are proved here. Quantum fields at a point are distributions, not bounded operators. Formal perturbation series and cutoff constructions are labeled as such; these entries do not construct interacting four-dimensional QFT nonperturbatively. The course introduces gauge and particle physics without claiming to replace courses in the Standard Model, many-body field theory, or constructive QFT.

**Inspected sources for further study.** David Tong's [classical fields](https://davidtong.org/teaching/quantum-field-theory/qfthtml/S1), [free fields](https://davidtong.org/teaching/quantum-field-theory/qfthtml/S2), [interactions](https://davidtong.org/teaching/quantum-field-theory/qfthtml/S3), [Dirac equation](https://davidtong.org/teaching/quantum-field-theory/qfthtml/S4), and [electrodynamics](https://davidtong.org/teaching/quantum-field-theory/qfthtml/S6); Pavel Etingof's [MIT mathematical QFT notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-238-geometry-and-quantum-field-theory-spring-2023/mit18_238_s23_lec_full.pdf); and [Burgess on effective field theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0701053). The worked calculations below are independently derived, including their stated regulators and sign conventions.
