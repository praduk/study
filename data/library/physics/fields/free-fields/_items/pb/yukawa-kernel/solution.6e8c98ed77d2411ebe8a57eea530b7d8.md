For $r>0$, the radial identity $\Delta(u(r)/r)=u''(r)/r$ gives $\Delta G=m^2G$. Near zero, $G=(4\pi r)^{-1}+O(1)$ and $\partial_rG=-1/(4\pi r^2)+O(1)$. Integration by parts outside a radius-$\epsilon$ sphere against a test function therefore leaves a boundary term tending to its value at zero; the term involving $G$ times the test-function normal derivative tends to zero. Thus $-\Delta G+m^2G$ has unit delta strength. The exponential ensures decay; $m^{-1}$ is the screening length in the declared natural units. A force law additionally needs the coupling and source normalization.
