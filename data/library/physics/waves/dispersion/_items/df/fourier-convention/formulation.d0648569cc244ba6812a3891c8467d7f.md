For a sufficiently regular integrable signal use

$$
\widehat u(k)=\int_{\R}u(x)e^{-ikx}dx,\qquad u(x)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\R}\widehat u(k)e^{ikx}dk.
$$

The inverse formula is understood under appropriate Fourier-inversion hypotheses; Schwartz functions suffice. If $u$ is a displacement, $\widehat u$ has units $\mathrm{m^2}$. Real signals satisfy $\widehat u(-k)=\overline{\widehat u(k)}$. A linear translation-invariant propagation model evolves each spectral component independently; its dispersion relation specifies the relation between wave number and temporal frequency.
