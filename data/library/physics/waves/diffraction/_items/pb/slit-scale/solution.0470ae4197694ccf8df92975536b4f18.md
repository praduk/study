The first zeros are at $X=\pm\lambda z/b=\pm0.010\,\mathrm m$. The width between them is therefore $0.020\,\mathrm m=20\,\mathrm{mm}$. The dimensionless diffraction parameter is $b^2/(\lambda z)=4\times10^{-8}/(2\times10^{-6})=0.020$, small enough to motivate the far-field model for this transverse dimension. The first-zero angle is about $0.0025$ radians, consistent with a paraxial calculation. Doubling wavelength doubles the predicted lobe width and halves this diffraction parameter. The other aperture dimension also needs its own far-field check.
