For monochromatic propagation in a homogeneous transparent medium of wavelength $\lambda$ and $k=2\pi/\lambda$, model a prescribed aperture field $A(x)$ in the plane $z=0$ by

$$
U(X,z)=\frac{e^{ikz}}{i\lambda z}\int_{\R^2}A(x)\exp\!\left(\frac{ik|X-x|^2}{2z}\right)d^2x,\qquad z>0.
$$

This Fresnel integral is a scalar, forward, paraxial model with aperture and observation offsets small compared with $z$. It does not solve arbitrary electromagnetic edge boundary conditions. $A$ and $U$ have the same field units. Aperture dimensions comparable to wavelength or large observation angles can require a vector or nonparaxial treatment.

The ray limit and its flux transport are organized by @physics:waves:geometry:df:phase-space-rays and @physics:waves:geometry:th:amplitude-flux. Diffraction retains wave information that a single projected ray chart loses near focusing or aperture edges. The existence of a ray caustic therefore signals a limitation of that local phase representation, not a divergence that must occur in every finite-wavelength wave field.
