Boundary equality for all times requires equal temporal frequency, since exponentials with distinct frequencies cannot have the same nonzero single-frequency trace. Equality along every point of the plane similarly fixes the tangential component of wave vector. Reflection remains in medium 1 with the same magnitude of $k$, so its tangential component fixes the same angle magnitude; the normal component reverses sign. Transmission has tangential magnitudes $(n_j\omega/c_0)\sin\theta_j$. Their equality gives Snell's relation after cancellation. If the required sine exceeds one, the traveling-wave assumption fails rather than giving a real angle.
