For two fields of the same frequency and polarization reaching the same observation point, use an ideal time-averaged quadratic response

$$
I=C\langle|U_1+U_2|^2\rangle,\qquad C>0.
$$

The factor $C$ converts the squared field amplitude to intensity in $\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$ for optical energy-flux measurements. With orthogonal vector polarizations, use the squared vector norm; orthogonality removes their interference term. A detector averages over its finite area and response time, so experimentally resolved fringes also depend on those scales. This is a detector/field model; intensities cannot generally be added before coherent amplitudes are added.
