Set $y=X(x)e^{-i\omega t}$ with $\omega>0$. Then $X''+(\omega/c)^2X=0$. The condition at zero removes the cosine term. A nonzero sine coefficient is compatible with the condition at $L$ exactly when $\omega L/c=n\pi$ for a positive integer $n$. Orthogonality follows by integrating $2\sin a\sin b=\cos(a-b)-\cos(a+b)$; both cosine integrals vanish for distinct positive integer wave numbers. For equal indices, the squared norm is $L/2$. This classifies separated modes, not yet convergence of arbitrary mode expansions.
