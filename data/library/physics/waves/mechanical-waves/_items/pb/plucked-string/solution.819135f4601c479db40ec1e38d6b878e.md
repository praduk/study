The coefficients are $b_n=(2/L)\int_0^Lf(x)\sin(n\pi x/L)dx$. Integration by parts on each half cancels endpoint terms and yields $b_n=8A\sin(n\pi/2)/(n^2\pi^2)$. Thus the modal solution is $y(x,t)=\sum_{n\ge1}b_n\sin(n\pi x/L)\cos(n\pi ct/L)$. Since $\sum|b_n|<\infty$, this series converges uniformly in $(x,t)$ and reproduces the continuous initial displacement by its Fourier series. Its modes have zero initial velocity; in the finite-energy interpretation the velocity is zero initially as well, since the squared velocity coefficients are dominated by a summable multiple of $1/n^2$ and vanish termwise at $t=0$. Every partial sum solves the wave equation; uniform convergence allows that equation to pass to the limit in distributions by moving derivatives onto a compactly supported smooth test function. Also $\sum n^2b_n^2<\infty$, so the spatial derivative has finite squared integral by sine/cosine orthogonality. The initial slope jumps at the midpoint, so initial displacement is not even $C^1$ there. The series describes a weak finite-energy wave with traveling corners, not a globally $C^2$ field.
