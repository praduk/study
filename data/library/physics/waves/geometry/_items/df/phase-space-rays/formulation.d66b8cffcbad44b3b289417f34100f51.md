For a real scalar second-order differential operator $P=a^{ij}(x)\partial_i\partial_j+$ terms of lower order on a @[smooth manifold]math:diffgeo:manifolds:df:smooth-manifold, its principal symbol is the fiberwise quadratic function $p(x,\xi)=a^{ij}(x)\xi_i\xi_j$ on $T^*M$. We omit the alternative overall minus sign that arises from using $-i\partial_i$. A nonzero covector is characteristic if $p(x,\xi)=0$. Under a coordinate change, derivatives of the change of coordinates occur only in lower-order terms, so $p$ is intrinsic.

A smooth hypersurface $S=\text{constant}$ is characteristic where $dS\ne0$ and $p(x,dS)=0$. A bicharacteristic is an integral curve of $X_p$ in the characteristic set, using @math:diffgeo:geometric-structures:df:symplectic on $T^*M$. Its projection to $M$ is the corresponding ray. The characteristic set is preserved since $X_pp=0$. Multiplying $p$ by a smooth nowhere-zero factor rescales $X_p$ on $p=0$ and hence preserves its unparametrized rays.

For $P=c(x)^{-2}\partial_t^2-\Delta_x$, the symbol is $p=c(x)^{-2}\tau^2-|k|^2$, where $\tau\,dt+k\cdot dx$ is a spacetime covector. A local phase defines a Lagrangian graph $dS$ by @math:diffgeo:geometric-structures:th:cotangent-symplectic. A caustic of a ray family is a critical value of its projection from the ray manifold to position space; at such a point a single regular phase chart can fail. These definitions organize a high-frequency approximation, not an assertion that finite-frequency waves become singular there.
