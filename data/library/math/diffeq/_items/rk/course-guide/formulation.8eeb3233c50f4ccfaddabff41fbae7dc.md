This course treats ordinary differential equations with both computation and justification, then introduces boundary-value problems, Fourier series, and the heat and wave equations. The order is first-order methods; existence and uniqueness; linear scalar equations; linear systems; qualitative dynamics; Laplace transforms; series methods; and boundary-value/PDE methods. Every worked problem has a separate solution. Numerical methods are included with a distinction between approximation error and stability.

The organization was checked against Jiří Lebl's author-hosted [Notes on Diffy Qs](https://www.jirka.org/diffyqs/html/diffyqs.html). Proofs and worked solutions here are original internal derivations. The Fourier convergence theorem is explicitly source-stated without a proof; it is not packaged as a proof-recall task. Existence proofs use completeness of continuous functions in the uniform norm, explained in the relevant argument.

Functions and equations are real unless a complex eigenvalue or transform parameter is explicitly introduced. A solution is defined on an interval and must satisfy both the equation and all stated initial or boundary conditions. Dividing by an unknown function can remove equilibrium solutions. A local existence result alone does not guarantee a solution for all time. Graduate dynamical systems, distributional PDE, and nonlinear PDE existence theory are beyond this course.
