For an integrable real $2L$-periodic function, $L>0$, define

$$
a_n=\frac1L\int_{-L}^L f(x)\cos(n\pi x/L)\,dx\quad(n\ge0),\qquad b_n=\frac1L\int_{-L}^L f(x)\sin(n\pi x/L)\,dx\quad(n\ge1).
$$

Its Fourier series is $a_0/2+\sum_{n\ge1}[a_n\cos(n\pi x/L)+b_n\sin(n\pi x/L)]$. Orthogonality of sines and cosines explains these coefficients by projection. Defining the coefficients does not itself prove that the series converges to $f$. Even functions have zero sine coefficients; odd functions have zero cosine coefficients, by symmetry of the integrals.
