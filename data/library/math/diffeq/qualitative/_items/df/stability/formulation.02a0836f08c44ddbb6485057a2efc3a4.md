For an autonomous equation $x'=f(x)$ with local uniqueness, an equilibrium is a point $x_*$ with $f(x_*)=0$. It is stable if for every $\epsilon>0$ there is $\delta>0$ such that solutions starting within $\delta$ remain within $\epsilon$ for all $t\ge0$ (and exist for those times). It is asymptotically stable if it is stable and some neighborhood of initial states also satisfies $x(t)\to x_*$. It is unstable if it is not stable. Attraction alone does not include the stability quantifier. An equilibrium is hyperbolic when the eigenvalues of $Df(x_*)$ all have nonzero real parts.
