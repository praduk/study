In complex Jordan form, each exponential block is $e^{\lambda t}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}t^jN^j/j!$, with $N^r=0$. If all real parts are negative, choose $\gamma>0$ smaller than their absolute decay rates; every polynomial factor times $e^{(\operatorname{Re}\lambda+\gamma)t}$ is bounded on $[0,\infty)$. Changing basis gives the bound, which implies stability and convergence. Conversely an eigenvector with eigenvalue of nonnegative real part gives a complex solution $e^{\lambda t}v$ that fails to converge to zero. Its real and imaginary parts are real solutions; they cannot both converge to zero. Scaling their initial conditions arbitrarily small contradicts local attraction. This proof uses the finite-dimensional Jordan normal-form theorem as a linear-algebra prerequisite.
