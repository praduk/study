For $y'=\lambda y$, forward Euler gives $Y_{j+1}=(1+h\lambda)Y_j$. Decay of numerical perturbations requires $|1+h\lambda|<1$. For real $\lambda<0$ this is $0<h<-2/\lambda$, despite decay of the exact solution for every $t>0$. At the endpoint the numerical magnitude does not decay. Backward Euler instead gives $Y_{j+1}=Y_j/(1-h\lambda)$, whose amplification magnitude is less than one for every $h>0$ when $\operatorname{Re}\lambda<0$. These calculations explain a basic stiffness issue; neither condition alone supplies an error tolerance for a nonlinear problem.
