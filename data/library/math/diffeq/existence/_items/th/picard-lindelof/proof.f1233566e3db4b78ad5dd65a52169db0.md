Let $X$ be the continuous curves on this interval satisfying $\norm{y(t)-y_0}\le b$, with uniform distance. Uniform @[Cauchy sequences]math:pst:metric:df:complete-total-bounded have pointwise limits in complete $\R^n$, converge uniformly to them, and uniform limits are continuous; the bound is closed. Thus $X$ is complete. Define $(Ty)(t)=y_0+\int_{t_0}^t f(s,y(s))\,ds$. The bound $hM\le b$ makes $T$ map $X$ into itself, and $\norm{Tu-Tv}_\infty\le hL\norm{u-v}_\infty$. Starting with the constant curve, successive differences shrink geometrically; summing that bound makes the iterates Cauchy. Their limit $y$ satisfies $Ty=y$ by the same Lipschitz estimate. The fundamental theorem of calculus gives $y'=f(t,y)$. If $u,v$ are fixed points, their distance is at most $hL$ times itself, so they coincide. Every solution in the ball satisfies the integral equation.
