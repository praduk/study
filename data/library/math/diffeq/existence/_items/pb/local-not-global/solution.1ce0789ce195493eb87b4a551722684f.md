Both $y(t)=0$ and $y(t)=t^2$ solve the first problem for $t\ge0$; more generally one may stay at zero until any $a\ge0$ and then take $(t-a)^2$. The joined function has derivative zero at $a$, so it satisfies the equation there too. The state function is not locally Lipschitz at zero. For the second problem separation gives $z(t)=1/(1-t)$, whose maximal interval containing zero is $(-\infty,1)$. A differentiable extension through $1$ would be continuous and finite there, contradicting divergence. The smoothness of $z^2$ gives local uniqueness but does not prevent blow-up.
