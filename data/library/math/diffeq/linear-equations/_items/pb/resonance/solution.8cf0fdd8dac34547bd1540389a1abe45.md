Because $\cos t$ solves the homogeneous equation, a constant multiple of $\cos t$ cannot produce the forcing. Try $y_p=A t\sin t$: differentiation gives $y_p''+y_p=2A\cos t$, so $A=1/2$. This particular solution already has both zero initial data; uniqueness gives $y(t)=t\sin t/2$. Its oscillation envelope grows linearly because the forcing frequency equals the undamped natural frequency. Direct differentiation checks the forcing and initial values.
