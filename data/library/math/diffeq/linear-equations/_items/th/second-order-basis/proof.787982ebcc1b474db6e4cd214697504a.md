Rewrite the equation as $(y,v)'=(v,-qy-pv)$. On every compact subinterval the coefficients are bounded, so local existence and the Gronwall estimate extend solutions across that compact interval: the state remains bounded, and the integral equation gives a limit at any finite endpoint, where local existence continues it. Thus arbitrary pairs $(y(t_0),y'(t_0))$ give unique solutions on $I$. Evaluation at $t_0$ is consequently a linear bijection from the solution space to $\R^2$. The two solutions form a basis exactly when their evaluated vectors are independent, which is the nonzero determinant condition.
