Substitution verifies exponential solutions. For a repeated root the operator is $(D-r)^2$, and $(D-r)(te^{rt})=e^{rt}$, so applying it twice gives zero. For complex roots, the real and imaginary parts of the verified complex exponential solve the real equation. At any time the Wronskians are respectively $(r_2-r_1)e^{(r_1+r_2)t}$, $e^{2rt}$, and $\beta e^{2\alpha t}$, all nonzero. The fundamental-pair theorem makes them bases.
