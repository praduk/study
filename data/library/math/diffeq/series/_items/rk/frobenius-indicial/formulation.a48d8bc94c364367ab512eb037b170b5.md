At a regular singular point shifted to $0$, put $p_0=\lim_{x\to0}xP(x)$ and $q_0=\lim_{x\to0}x^2Q(x)$. Seeking a solution $y=x^r\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_nx^n$ on $x>0$ with $a_0\ne0$ forces the lowest-power coefficient to satisfy $r(r-1)+p_0r+q_0=0$. This is the indicial equation. It is a necessary algebraic condition for this ansatz, not by itself a convergence proof or a guarantee of two independent series. Repeated roots or roots differing by an integer can require logarithms, as the Euler–Cauchy repeated-root solution demonstrates. The full Frobenius existence theorem is beyond this entry.
