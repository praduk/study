An ordinary differential equation relates an unknown function of one independent variable and its derivatives. Its order is the highest derivative appearing. A solution of $y'=f(t,y)$ on an interval $I$ is a differentiable map $y:I\to\R^n$ with $(t,y(t))$ in the domain of $f$ and $y'(t)=f(t,y(t))$ for every $t\in I$. An initial-value problem also specifies $y(t_0)=y_0$ with $t_0\in I$. A solution is maximal if no solution on a strictly larger interval extends it. A finite endpoint of a maximal interval is not necessarily a singularity of the formula for $f$; the solution itself may diverge.
