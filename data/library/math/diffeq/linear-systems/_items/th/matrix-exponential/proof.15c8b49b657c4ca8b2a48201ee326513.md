In any submultiplicative matrix norm the series is bounded by $e^{|t|\norm A}$, uniformly on compact time intervals; the differentiated series has the same compact convergence bound. Termwise differentiation is therefore justified and gives $Ae^{tA}$. Absolute convergence permits the Cauchy product, whose coefficient of $A^k$ is $\sum_{j=0}^k s^jt^{k-j}/(j!(k-j)!)=(s+t)^k/k!$, giving the product identity. Thus $e^{tA}$ has inverse $e^{-tA}$. Differentiating $e^{-tA}x(t)$ for any solution gives zero, proving the asserted formula and uniqueness.
