For a separated term $u=X(x)T(t)$, the Dirichlet spatial eigenfunction has $X''=-(n\pi/L)^2X$, so the PDE requires $T'=-\kappa(n\pi/L)^2T$. Each displayed term satisfies the PDE and boundary data, and the finite sum avoids any interchange-of-infinite-series issue. At $t=0$ the sum equals $f$. For uniqueness subtract two solutions to get $v$ with zero data. Integration by parts gives $(\frac12\int_0^L v^2dx)'=-\kappa\int_0^L v_x^2dx\le0$ with zero initial energy. Nonnegative energy must remain zero; continuity implies $v=0$.
