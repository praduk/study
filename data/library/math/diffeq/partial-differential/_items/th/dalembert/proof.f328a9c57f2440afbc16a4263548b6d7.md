Set $\xi=x-ct$, $\eta=x+ct$. The equation becomes $U_{\xi\eta}=0$, so on the half-plane $\xi\le\eta$ every solution has the form $A(\xi)+B(\eta)$; this follows by integrating first in one variable and then the other on its interval. Initial data impose $A(x)+B(x)=f(x)$ and $-cA'(x)+cB'(x)=g(x)$. Therefore $A'=(f'-g/c)/2$ and $B'=(f'+g/c)/2$. Integrating and adding produces the formula; the additive constants cancel because their sum is fixed by the displacement condition. The regularity assumptions permit two differentiations and verify the PDE and both data directly. The derivation also proves uniqueness.
