Before the switch, $y=0$. After the switch, solve $y'+2y=1$ with the matching value $y(1)=0$, giving $y=(1-e^{-2(t-1)})/2$. Combined, $y(t)=H(t-1)(1-e^{-2(t-1)})/2$, with value zero at $1$. It is continuous there and satisfies the equation on each open side; its derivative jumps from $0$ to $1$. The transform calculation agrees: $Y(s)=e^{-s}/[s(s+2)]$. Direct substitution and continuity establish the solution without relying on a transform inversion theorem.
