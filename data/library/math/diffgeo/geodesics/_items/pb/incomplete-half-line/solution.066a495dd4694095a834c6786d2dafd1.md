For $g$, the distance is $|x-y|$ and $1/n$ is Cauchy with no limit in $M$, so the metric is incomplete. Its geodesic is $x(t)=x_0+v_0t$ on the maximal interval where it is positive. For $h$, the map $u=\log x$ is a global isometry to the Euclidean line because $du^2=dx^2/x^2$. Thus $h$ is complete and the geodesic is $x(t)=x_0\exp((v_0/x_0)t)$ for all real $t$. Differentiating at zero checks its prescribed velocity. Completeness is a property of the chosen metric, not of the @[smooth manifold]math:diffgeo:manifolds:df:smooth-manifold alone.
