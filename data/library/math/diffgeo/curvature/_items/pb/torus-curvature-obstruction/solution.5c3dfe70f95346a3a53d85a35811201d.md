The torus has a CW structure with one vertex, two edges, and one face, so $\chi=0$. Gauss–Bonnet forces $\int K\,dA=0$. If $K>0$ everywhere, compactness gives a positive minimum and the total area is positive, so this integral would be positive, a contradiction. On the other hand, the quotient $\R^2/\Z^2$ carries the Euclidean metric because integer translations preserve it. Each point has an isometric Euclidean neighborhood; the connection there has zero Christoffel symbols and zero curvature. Thus a flat torus exists.
