For a finite-dimensional real or complex @[vector space]math:algebra:linear-foundations:df:vector-space $V$ with symmetric @[bilinear form]math:algebra:linear-foundations:df:bilinear-forms $b$, define its Clifford algebra as the tensor algebra modulo the relations

$$
uv+vu=2b(u,v)1.
$$

Here the tensor algebra is $T(V)=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}V^{\otimes k}$ with concatenation product, and the quotient is by the @[two-sided ideal]math:algebra:rings-ideals:df:ring-ideal generated by those relations. This fixes the sign convention; some sources use $-2b$ instead. The tensor product is as in @math:algebra:tensor-projective:df:tensor over the chosen field.

A Clifford module is a vector space $S$ with a unital algebra homomorphism from this algebra to $\operatorname{End}(S)$. Its vectors are spinors for this representation. Thus matrices $\gamma^i$ representing an orthogonal basis obey $\gamma^i\gamma^j+\gamma^j\gamma^i=2b^{ij}I$. The Dirac Hamiltonian matrices in @physics:fields:spinors-gauge:df:dirac-hamiltonian realize a related Clifford relation; a spinor is not an ordinary tangent vector. A particular global spinor bundle is extra structure: its local representations must patch compatibly with frame changes. This entry defines the algebra and its modules; it does not assert that every metric admits every desired irreducible spinor bundle.
