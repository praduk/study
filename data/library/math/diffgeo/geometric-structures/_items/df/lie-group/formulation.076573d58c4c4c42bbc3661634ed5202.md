A Lie group is a @[group]math:algebra:groups-actions:df:groups-homomorphisms that is also a finite-dimensional @[smooth manifold]math:diffgeo:manifolds:df:smooth-manifold, with smooth multiplication and inversion. A smooth action on $M$ is a @[smooth map]math:diffgeo:manifolds:df:smooth-map $G\times M\to M$ satisfying the group-action identities in @math:algebra:groups-actions:df:action.

Its Lie algebra is $\mathfrak g=T_eG$, identified by left translation with left-invariant vector fields, with bracket given by @math:diffgeo:bundles-flows:th:lie-bracket. For matrix groups the bracket is $[X,Y]=XY-YX$. The exponential $\exp(tX)$ is the one-parameter subgroup with tangent $X$ at the identity; for matrices it is the matrix exponential. It is a local parametrization near the identity, not necessarily a one-to-one global parametrization.

Examples are $SO(n)=\{R:R^TR=I,\det R=1\}$ with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(n)=\{X:X^T=-X\}$, and $U(n)=\{U:U^\dagger U=I\}$ with $\mathfrak u(n)=\{X:X^\dagger=-X\}$. The dagger denotes conjugate transpose. Differentiating their defining identities gives the displayed tangent conditions; exponentiating a matrix with those conditions preserves the identities. For $SU(n)$ impose also $\det U=1$, giving $\operatorname{tr}X=0$ in addition to skew-Hermiticity.
