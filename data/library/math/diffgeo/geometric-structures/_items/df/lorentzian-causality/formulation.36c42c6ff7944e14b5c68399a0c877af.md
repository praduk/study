On a Lorentzian manifold with the @math:diffgeo:geometric-structures:df:pseudo-metric convention $(-,+,\ldots,+)$, a nonzero tangent vector $v$ is timelike when $g(v,v)<0$, null when $g(v,v)=0$, and spacelike when $g(v,v)>0$. Timelike or null vectors are causal. Each tangent space's timelike cone has two connected components.

A time orientation is a continuous choice of one timelike-cone component as future at each point, equivalently a continuous timelike vector field. Null future vectors lie in the corresponding closure. A causal curve has future-directed causal tangent where differentiable; a timelike curve has timelike tangent. A spacelike submanifold has positive-definite induced metric, so a spacelike hypersurface has a timelike normal locally.

For a future unit timelike vector $u$ with $g(u,u)=-1$, its rest space is $u^\perp$ and its projection is $v\mapsto v+g(u,v)u$. Extending $u$ to a Lorentz-orthonormal basis proves that the metric on $u^\perp$ is @[positive definite]math:algebra:linear-foundations:df:bilinear-forms. Spacetime orientation (a choice for top forms) and time orientation are distinct inputs. These definitions impose no global hyperbolicity or causality theorem.
