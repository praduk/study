Use the graded product rule and $d^2=0$ from @math:diffgeo:forms:th:d-squared. First, $dF_A=dA\w A-A\w dA$. Also $A\w F_A-F_A\w A=A\w dA-dA\w A$, since the two cubic terms cancel. Their sum is zero.

For the second identity, expand $d_A(d\beta+A\w\beta-(-1)^k\beta\w A)$, noting that $d_A\beta$ has degree $k+1$. The terms containing $d\beta$ cancel. Those containing $dA$ are $dA\w\beta-\beta\w dA$; those containing two $A$ factors are $A\w A\w\beta-\beta\w A\w A$. The two mixed terms $A\w\beta\w A$ cancel. Grouping the remaining terms gives the formula.
