Follow the numbered topics from @[smooth manifolds]math:diffgeo:manifolds:df:smooth-manifold to global Riemannian geometry. Unless explicitly stated otherwise, manifolds are finite-dimensional, Hausdorff, @[second countable]math:pst:separation:df:countability, smooth, and without boundary. Maps and tensor fields are smooth. Smooth means $C^\infty$. Repeated coordinate indices are summed over their full range. @[Riemannian metrics]math:diffgeo:connections:df:riemannian-metric are @[positive definite]math:algebra:linear-foundations:df:bilinear-forms; statements about distance and completeness are not assertions about indefinite metrics.

Our curvature convention is $R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$, with @[sectional curvature]math:diffgeo:curvature:df:sectional-ricci $g(R(u,v)v,u)$ divided by squared area. This gives positive curvature to round spheres. Throughout the Geodesics and Completeness and Curvature and Global Geometry topics, geodesics, exponential maps, covariant derivatives, and curvature use the @[Levi–Civita connection]math:diffgeo:connections:th:levi-civita of the Riemannian metric unless an entry explicitly discusses a general connection. Boundary orientations use outward normal first. Sources may choose different signs.

The instructional derivations and worked examples are original. Authorities inspected for the main constructions and larger milestones are [MIT differential-forms notes](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.952/2018SP/files/18.952_book.pdf), [Dupont's differential-geometry notes](https://www.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/dupontnotes.pdf), and [Paternain's geometry notes](https://www.dpmms.cam.ac.uk/~gpp24/dgnotes/dg.pdf). Source-stated theorems without a stored full proof are labeled accordingly.

This course covers the graduate foundation in manifolds, bundles, forms, connections, geodesics, and curvature. It does not claim to replace a specialized course in characteristic classes, comparison geometry, geometric PDE, or complex geometry.
