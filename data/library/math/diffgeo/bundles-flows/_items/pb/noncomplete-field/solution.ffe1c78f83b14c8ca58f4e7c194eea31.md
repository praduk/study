The equation is $x'=x^2$. Separating variables for a positive solution gives $-1/x=t+C$, and the initial condition at $t=0$ yields $x(t)=x_0/(1-x_0t)$ on $(-\infty,1/x_0)$. Direct differentiation verifies both the equation and initial condition. As $t\uparrow1/x_0$, the solution tends to $+\infty$ and has no finite limit in $\R$, so it cannot extend continuously, hence cannot extend as an integral curve. This is the maximal interval by local uniqueness. A globally smooth field on a noncompact manifold need not be complete.
