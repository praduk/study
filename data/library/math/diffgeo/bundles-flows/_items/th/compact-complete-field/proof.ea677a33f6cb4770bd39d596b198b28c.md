For each $p$, the local-flow theorem provides a neighborhood $U_p$ and a positive $\varepsilon_p$ such that every initial point in $U_p$ has flow for $|t|<\varepsilon_p$. Compactness gives finitely many $U_p$ covering $M$; let $\varepsilon>0$ be smaller than all their time bounds. If a maximal integral curve had a finite upper endpoint $b$, choose a time $t_0<b$ with $b-t_0<\varepsilon/2$. Starting at $\gamma(t_0)$ yields an integral curve lasting at least $\varepsilon$ forward. Uniqueness makes it agree with the original where both exist, extending beyond $b$, a contradiction. The lower endpoint is treated by reversing time.
