At a point of the fiber choose coordinates with $q$ at zero. Surjectivity supplies $n$ input coordinates for which the corresponding Jacobian minor is invertible. After permuting coordinates, the map $x\mapsto(F(x),x^{n+1},\ldots,x^m)$ has invertible derivative. The Euclidean inverse function theorem makes it a local coordinate change. In these new coordinates $F$ is projection onto the first $n$ coordinates, so the fiber is their zero slice. Its tangent vectors are exactly those annihilated by that projection, giving the kernel formula. These local descriptions use the subspace topology and cover the fiber.
