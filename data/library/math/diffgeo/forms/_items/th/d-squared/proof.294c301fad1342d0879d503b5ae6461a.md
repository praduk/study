Locally it is enough to check the generating terms $f\,dx^{i_1}\w\cdots\w dx^{i_k}$. Applying $d$ twice leaves second derivatives of $f$ wedged with coordinate differentials; the symmetric coefficients $\partial_i\partial_j f$ cancel against the antisymmetry of $dx^i\w dx^j$. Hence $d^2=0$. Pullback takes a generating term to $(f\circ F)\,dF^{i_1}\w\cdots\w dF^{i_k}$. Differentiating it, all terms involving $d(dF^i)$ vanish by the just-proved identity, while the chain rule makes its first term the pullback of $df\w dx^{i_1}\w\cdots$. Thus naturality holds on each term and hence globally.
