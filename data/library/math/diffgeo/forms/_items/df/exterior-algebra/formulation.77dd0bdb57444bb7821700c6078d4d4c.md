For a @[finite-dimensional vector space]math:algebra:linear-foundations:df:basis-dimension $V$, $\Lambda^kV^*$ is the space of alternating $k$-linear maps $V^k\to\R$. If $\alpha$ has degree $p$ and $\beta$ degree $q$, their wedge is the alternating product normalized so that, for one-forms, $(\alpha_1\w\cdots\w\alpha_k)(v_1,\ldots,v_k)=\det(\alpha_i(v_j))$.

With a @[dual basis]math:algebra:linear-foundations:df:dual-annihilator $e^1,\ldots,e^n$, the forms $e^{i_1}\w\cdots\w e^{i_k}$ for increasing indices form a basis. Thus $\dim\Lambda^kV^*=\binom nk$ and it is zero for $k>n$.
