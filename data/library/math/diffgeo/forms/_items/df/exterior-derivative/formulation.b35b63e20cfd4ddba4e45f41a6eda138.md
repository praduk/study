The exterior derivative is the degree-one operator $d:\Omega^k(M)\to\Omega^{k+1}(M)$ given in coordinates by

$$
d\left(\sum_Ia_I\,dx^{i_1}\w\cdots\w dx^{i_k}\right)=\sum_I da_I\w dx^{i_1}\w\cdots\w dx^{i_k}.
$$

For functions this is the usual differential. It satisfies the graded Leibniz rule $d(\alpha\w\beta)=d\alpha\w\beta+(-1)^p\alpha\w d\beta$ when $\alpha$ has degree $p$. Its compatibility with coordinate changes follows from the chain rule and the naturality identity below.
