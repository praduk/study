For a smooth manifold $M$, integration over smooth singular simplices induces a natural isomorphism $H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\isoto H^k(M;\R)$. Smooth singular cohomology agrees with ordinary singular cohomology; this comparison is part of the theorem's construction. The isomorphism takes wedge product to cup product.

This theorem is used without proof here. See the inspected [John Morgan, Modern Geometry course notes](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~jmorgan/MG_Course_Notes_1.pdf). Real coefficients matter: differential forms do not retain integral torsion.
