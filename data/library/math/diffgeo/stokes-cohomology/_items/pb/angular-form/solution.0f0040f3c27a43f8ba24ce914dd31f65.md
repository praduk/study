Writing $P=-y/(x^2+y^2)$ and $Q=x/(x^2+y^2)$, direct differentiation gives $\partial_xQ=(y^2-x^2)/(x^2+y^2)^2=\partial_yP$, so $d\alpha=(\partial_xQ-\partial_yP)dx\w dy=0$. On the unit circle $\gamma(t)=(\cos t,\sin t)$, $0\le t\le2\pi$, the pullback is $dt$, so its integral is $2\pi$. If $\alpha=df$, the chain rule would give $\int_\gamma\alpha=f(\gamma(2\pi))-f(\gamma(0))=0$, a contradiction. The missing origin prevents the star-shaped-domain theorem from applying.
