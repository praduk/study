There are only finitely many poles on a compact neighborhood of the closed disk, since poles are isolated and cannot accumulate within the meromorphic domain. Subtract the finite principal part at each interior pole. The resulting function $g$ extends holomorphically throughout a neighborhood of the closed disk, so its circle integral vanishes by Cauchy's theorem. For a principal term $(z-b)^{-k}$ with $k\ge2$, the single-valued primitive $(z-b)^{-k+1}/(-k+1)$ gives zero circle integral. For $k=1$, Cauchy's formula applied to the constant function $1$ at the interior point $b$ gives integral $2\pi i$. Summing these contributions proves the formula.
