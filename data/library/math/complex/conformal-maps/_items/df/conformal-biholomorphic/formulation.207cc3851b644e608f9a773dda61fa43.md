A holomorphic map with $f'(a)\ne0$ preserves oriented angles between regular curves through $a$: the derivative multiplies tangent vectors by the same nonzero complex number, producing a common rotation and positive scaling. Such a map is conformal at $a$.

A biholomorphism is a bijective holomorphic map between domains whose inverse is holomorphic. A Möbius transformation is

$$
M(z)=\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\qquad ad-bc\ne0,
$$

extended to the Riemann sphere $\C\cup\{\infty\}$ by its limiting values. It has a Möbius inverse, obtained by solving the fractional linear equation. At finite points away from its pole, $M'(z)=(ad-bc)/(cz+d)^2\ne0$.
