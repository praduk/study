Write $z=x+iy$ with $x,y\in\R$ and $i^2=-1$. Its conjugate is $\overline z=x-iy$ and its modulus is $|z|=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$. Thus $z\overline z=|z|^2$ and, for $z\ne0$, $z^{-1}=\overline z/|z|^2$.

The identities $|zw|=|z||w|$ and $|z+w|\le|z|+|w|$ follow from conjugation and the Euclidean triangle inequality. Every nonzero $z$ has polar form $re^{i\theta}$ with $r=|z|>0$; $\theta$ is determined modulo $2\pi$, not uniquely as a real number.
