Define

$$
e^z=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{n!},\qquad
\cos z=\frac{e^{iz}+e^{-iz}}2,\qquad
\sin z=\frac{e^{iz}-e^{-iz}}{2i}.
$$

The exponential series converges absolutely and uniformly on every bounded disk by comparison with $\sum R^n/n!$. The same holds for its derivative series, so termwise differentiation is valid and $(e^z)'=e^z$.

For real $x,y$, $e^{x+iy}=e^x(\cos y+i\sin y)$; this follows by multiplying absolutely convergent series. Hence $|e^{x+iy}|=e^x$, the exponential never vanishes, and $e^{z+2\pi i}=e^z$.
