Substitution gives

$$
\int_\gamma(z-a)^k\,dz
=ir^{k+1}\int_0^{2\pi}e^{i(k+1)t}\,dt.
$$

If $k=-1$, the value is $2\pi i$. Otherwise the exponential antiderivative gives zero because $k+1$ is a nonzero integer. A primitive for $1/z$ on the punctured plane would force its integral on the unit circle to vanish, contradicting the case $a=0$, $r=1$, $k=-1$.
