Subdivide $T$ into four similar triangles. Their oriented boundary integrals add to the original because the internal edges cancel. One subtriangle has integral magnitude at least one quarter of the original. Repeating gives nested closed triangles $T_n$ with diameter $d/2^n$, perimeter $L/2^n$, and $|I_n|\ge |I_0|/4^n$. Their intersection is one point $a$.

Complex differentiability at $a$ gives $f(z)=f(a)+f'(a)(z-a)+r(z)$ where, for any $\epsilon>0$, $|r(z)|\le\epsilon|z-a|$ sufficiently near $a$. Constants and linear functions have primitives, so their integrals around each triangle vanish. For large $n$, the ML estimate yields $|I_n|\le\epsilon(d/2^n)(L/2^n)=\epsilon dL/4^n$. Therefore $|I_0|\le\epsilon dL$. Since $\epsilon$ is arbitrary, $I_0=0$. Degenerate triangles have cancelling edges and already have zero integral.
