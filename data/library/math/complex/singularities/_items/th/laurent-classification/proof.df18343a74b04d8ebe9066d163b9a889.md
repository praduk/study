If no negative coefficients occur, the remaining power series extends holomorphically to $a$. Conversely, an extension has a Taylor series, and uniqueness of Laurent coefficients forces all negative ones to vanish. If the most negative nonzero power is $-m$, multiplication by $(z-a)^m$ produces a power series with nonzero constant term, exactly the definition of a pole of order $m$. Conversely, the holomorphic nonzero factor at a pole has a Taylor series whose division by $(z-a)^m$ yields this pattern. The only remaining Laurent pattern has infinitely many negative coefficients, and the exhaustive definitions make the singularity essential.
