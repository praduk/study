Suppose $|f|$ has a local maximum $M$ at $a$. Choose a small closed disk about $a$ on which $|f|\le M$. If $M=0$, $f$ vanishes on the disk and hence on the domain by the identity theorem. If $M>0$, multiply $f$ by a unit scalar so its value at $a$ is the positive real number $M$. Cauchy's formula at the center says this value is the average of the boundary values. Each real part is at most $M$, so continuity and equality of the average force every boundary real part to equal $M$. The modulus bound then forces each boundary value to equal $M$. Cauchy's formula gives the same constant throughout the disk, and the identity theorem extends it. For the final assertion, continuity attains a maximum on the compact disk; an interior maximum would force constancy.
