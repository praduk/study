At a zero $a$, take the local Taylor expansion. If a first nonzero coefficient occurs at degree $m$, factor $f(z)=(z-a)^m g(z)$ with $g$ holomorphic and $g(a)\ne0$. Continuity makes $g$ nonzero near $a$, so the zero is isolated. If all coefficients vanish, $f$ vanishes on a neighborhood of $a$.

Let $S$ be the set of points where $f$ vanishes on a neighborhood. It is open. It is also relatively closed: at a limit point of $S$, either the function has a first nonzero Taylor coefficient, which would force isolated zeros nearby, or its entire Taylor series vanishes and the point belongs to $S$. The isolated-zero alternative is impossible because points of $S$ accumulate there. If zeros have an interior accumulation point, their Taylor series there must vanish, so $S$ is nonempty. Connectedness gives $S=U$. Apply this result to the difference of two functions for the identity theorem.
