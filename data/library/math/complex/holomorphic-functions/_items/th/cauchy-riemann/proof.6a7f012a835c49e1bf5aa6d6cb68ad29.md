The real derivative has matrix $\begin{pmatrix}u_x&u_y\\v_x&v_y\end{pmatrix}$. Multiplication by a complex number $\alpha+i\beta$ has matrix $\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&-\beta\\\beta&\alpha\end{pmatrix}$. The matrices agree exactly under the stated equations. Real differentiability then says the remainder is $o(|h|)$, which gives complex differentiability. Conversely, a complex derivative gives a real-linear first approximation by multiplication, so the same matrix comparison proves necessity. The $C^1$ assumption supplies real differentiability at every point.
