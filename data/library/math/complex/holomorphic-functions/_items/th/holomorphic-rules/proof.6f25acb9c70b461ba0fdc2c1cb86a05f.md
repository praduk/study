At a point write $f(z+h)=f(z)+ah+o(|h|)$ and $g(z+h)=g(z)+bh+o(|h|)$. Adding and multiplying gives the sum and product rules, because @[holomorphic functions]math:complex:holomorphic-functions:df:holomorphic are continuous. For a nonzero denominator, the identity
$1/g(z+h)-1/g(z)=-(g(z+h)-g(z))/(g(z+h)g(z))$
gives the reciprocal derivative. For composition, substitute $k=f(z+h)-f(z)=ah+o(|h|)=O(|h|)$ into the first-order expansion of the outer function. Its remainder is then $o(|h|)$, giving the chain rule.
