A power series centered at $a$ is $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_n(z-a)^n$. Its radius of convergence is

$$
R=\frac1{\limsup_n|c_n|^{1/n}},
$$

with $1/0=\infty$ and $1/\infty=0$. The root test gives absolute convergence for $|z-a|<R$ and divergence for $|z-a|>R$; boundary points require separate analysis.

On every smaller closed disk the series and its formal derivative converge uniformly, by comparison with a geometric majorant. Hence the sum is holomorphic inside its convergence disk. A function is analytic if it has such a power-series representation near each point. Cauchy's formula will prove that every @[holomorphic function]math:complex:holomorphic-functions:df:holomorphic is analytic.
