For conjugation, the difference quotient is $\overline h/h$: it equals $1$ for real $h$ and $-1$ for purely imaginary $h$. Thus no complex derivative exists anywhere. For $g(z)=x^2+y^2$, $u_x=2x$, $u_y=2y$, and both derivatives of the imaginary part vanish. @[Cauchy–Riemann]math:complex:holomorphic-functions:th:cauchy-riemann therefore holds only at zero. At zero the quotient is $|h|^2/h=\overline h\to0$, so the derivative exists and is zero. Since no open set consists only of zero, neither function is holomorphic on any nonempty open set.
