Fix a closed disk contained in $U$. Uniform convergence on that disk makes $f$ continuous there. Uniform convergence on its boundary permits passage to the limit in Cauchy's formula, giving the same integral representation for $f$ at every interior point. Expanding the kernel geometrically on any smaller disk, as in the Taylor derivation, proves that this representation is holomorphic.

For derivatives, differentiating the kernel under the contour integral is justified uniformly on a smaller concentric disk because the distance to the contour is positive. The difference formula for order $k$ has kernel $k!/(\zeta-z)^{k+1}$, so its magnitude is bounded by a fixed constant times the uniform boundary error $\sup|f_n-f|$. This tends to zero uniformly on the smaller disk. A compact subset of $U$ is covered by finitely many such smaller disks, proving local uniform convergence of the derivatives.
