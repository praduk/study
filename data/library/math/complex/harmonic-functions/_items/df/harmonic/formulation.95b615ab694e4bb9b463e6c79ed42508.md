A real-valued $C^2$ function $u$ on an open subset of $\R^2$ is harmonic when

$$
\Delta u=u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0.
$$

A harmonic conjugate of $u$ is a real-valued function $v$ for which $u+iv$ is holomorphic. The real and imaginary parts of a holomorphic function are harmonic: Taylor expansions give the required smoothness, and differentiating Cauchy–Riemann gives $u_{xx}+u_{yy}=v_{yx}-v_{xy}=0$, with the analogous identity for $v$.

When a conjugate exists on a domain, it is unique up to an additive real constant: the difference of two corresponding holomorphic functions has zero real part, so Cauchy–Riemann makes all its first partial derivatives vanish.
