The zero-chains are finite integer combinations of points. Send each point to its path component in the free abelian group on components. The boundary of a singular one-simplex is its terminal point minus its initial point, so all boundaries map to zero. Conversely a zero-chain in the kernel has coefficient sum zero in each of its finitely many represented components. Choose a representative point in each such component and paths from it to each point appearing in the chain. Their boundaries express the chain as a sum of point-minus-representative differences. Thus the kernel is exactly the boundaries and the map is surjective. Quotienting proves the claim; for $X=\nulset$ both groups are zero.
