For $A\subseteq X$, the relative chain complex is $C_n(X,A)=C_n(X)/C_n(A)$ with the induced boundary, and its homology is $H_n(X,A)$. A relative cycle may have a nonzero boundary provided that boundary lies in $A$.

Reduced homology augments the singular complex by $C_0(X)\to\Z$, sending each point to $1$. For nonempty $X$, $\widetilde H_0(X)$ is the kernel of $H_0(X)\to\Z$, and positive-degree reduced and ordinary homology agree.
