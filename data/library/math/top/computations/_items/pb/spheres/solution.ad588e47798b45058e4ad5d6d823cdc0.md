For $n=0$, the sphere is two points, and the augmentation $H_0(S^0)=\Z^2\to\Z$ has kernel $\Z$. Positive-degree homology vanishes: each point is contractible and a @[singular simplex]math:top:homology:df:singular-chains, having connected domain, lies in one component.

For $n\ge1$, cover $S^n$ by the complements of its north and south poles. Both sets are contractible (stereographic projection identifies each with $\R^n$), and their intersection deformation retracts onto the equatorial $S^{n-1}$. The reduced @[Mayer–Vietoris]math:top:computations:th:mayer-vietoris sequence therefore gives $\widetilde H_k(S^n)\isoto\widetilde H_{k-1}(S^{n-1})$ for $k\ge1$. The sphere $S^n$ is @[path connected]math:pst:compactness:df:connectedness for $n\ge1$, so its reduced degree-zero homology is zero. Induction yields $\widetilde H_k(S^n)=\Z$ for $k=n$ and zero for all other nonnegative $k$. This calculation precedes the definition of degree and does not rely on cellular boundary degrees.
