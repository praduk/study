If a @[CW complex]math:top:cells:df:cw-complex $X$ is $(n-1)$-connected with $n\ge2$, then $\widetilde H_i(X;\Z)=0$ for $i<n$ and the Hurewicz map $\pi_n(X)\to H_n(X;\Z)$ is an isomorphism. For a @[path-connected]math:pst:compactness:df:connectedness space, the degree-one version is $H_1(X;\Z)\isoto\pi_1(X)_{\mathrm{ab}}$.

A map between connected CW complexes inducing isomorphisms on every $\pi_n$, $n\ge1$, is a @[homotopy equivalence]math:top:homotopy:df:homotopy. This is Whitehead's theorem. Having merely isomorphic groups, without a map inducing the isomorphisms, is insufficient.

This structural theorem is used without proof here. See the inspected [Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology*, §2.A; §§4.1–4.2, Whitehead and Hurewicz theorems](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATplain.pdf).
