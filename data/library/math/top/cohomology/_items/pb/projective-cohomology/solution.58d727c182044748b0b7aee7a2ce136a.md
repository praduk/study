From the earlier computation, $H_0=\Z$, $H_1=\Z/2$, $H_2=0$, and higher groups vanish. In degree zero the universal coefficient theorem gives $H^0=\Z$. In degree one, both $\operatorname{Ext}^1(\Z,\Z)$ and $\Hom(\Z/2,\Z)$ are zero, so $H^1=0$. In degree two, $\operatorname{Ext}^1(\Z/2,\Z)=\Z/2$ and the Hom term is zero, so $H^2=\Z/2$. All higher cohomology vanishes. Thus the dual of $H_2=0$ misses the nonzero degree-two cohomology entirely.
