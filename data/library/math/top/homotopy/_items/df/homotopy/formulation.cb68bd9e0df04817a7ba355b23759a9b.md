Maps $f,g:X\to Y$ are homotopic if some continuous $H:X\times I\to Y$ satisfies $H(x,0)=f(x)$ and $H(x,1)=g(x)$. It is relative to $A\subseteq X$ if $H(a,t)=f(a)$ for every $a\in A,t\in I$.

A homotopy equivalence $f:X\to Y$ has a map $g:Y\to X$ with $gf\simeq\id_X$ and $fg\simeq\id_Y$. A nonempty space is contractible if its identity map is homotopic to a constant map. Homeomorphism implies homotopy equivalence, but the converse fails: an interval and a point are homotopy equivalent.
