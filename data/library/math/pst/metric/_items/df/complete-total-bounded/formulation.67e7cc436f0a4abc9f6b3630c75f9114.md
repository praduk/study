A metric $d$ is a nonnegative, symmetric function satisfying $d(x,y)=0$ exactly when $x=y$ and the triangle inequality. A sequence is Cauchy if for each $\varepsilon>0$ all sufficiently late pairs have distance below $\varepsilon$. The space is complete when every Cauchy sequence converges.

It is totally bounded when for every $\varepsilon>0$ finitely many open balls of radius $\varepsilon$ cover it. Boundedness alone is weaker: an infinite discrete space with distance $1$ between distinct points is bounded and complete but not totally bounded.
