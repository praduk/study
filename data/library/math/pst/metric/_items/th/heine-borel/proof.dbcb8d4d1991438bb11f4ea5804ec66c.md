A compact subset is closed because $\R^n$ is Hausdorff and bounded because finitely many radius-$1$ balls cover it. Conversely a closed subset of complete $\R^n$ is complete: the ambient limit of a @[Cauchy sequence]math:pst:metric:df:complete-total-bounded lies in the closed set. A bounded subset is totally bounded by dividing a containing finite cube into finitely many cubes of sufficiently small diameter and choosing one point from each small cube that meets the subset. The metric compactness criterion finishes the proof. Completeness of $\R^n$ follows coordinatewise from completeness of $\R$.
