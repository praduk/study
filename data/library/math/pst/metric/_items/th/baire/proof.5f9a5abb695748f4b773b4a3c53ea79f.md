Let $G_1,G_2,\ldots$ be dense open sets and $U$ a nonempty open set. Choose a closed ball $F_1=\closure{B}(x_1,r_1)$ of positive radius at most $1/2$ inside $U\cap G_1$. Inductively choose a closed ball $F_{n+1}$ of positive radius at most $2^{-n-1}$ inside $B(x_n,r_n)\cap G_{n+1}$; density and openness make this possible, even at isolated points. The balls are nested. For $m\ge n$, $x_m\in F_n$, so $d(x_m,x_n)\le r_n\to0$. Completeness gives a limit $x$. Each $F_n$ is closed and contains a tail, hence contains $x$. Therefore $x\in U\cap\bigcap_nG_n$. Every nonempty open $U$ meets the intersection, proving density.
