The continuous map $f(t)=e^{2\pi it}$ is surjective onto $S^1$, and its only repeated values on $[0,1]$ occur at the two endpoints. The quotient property therefore supplies a continuous bijection $g:Y\to S^1$. The interval is compact, so its quotient $Y$ is compact. The circle is Hausdorff as a metric subspace. The theorem @math:pst:compactness:th:compact-bijection now makes $g$ a @[homeomorphism]math:pst:spaces:df:continuity. The proof does not assume that an arbitrary continuous bijection is a homeomorphism.
