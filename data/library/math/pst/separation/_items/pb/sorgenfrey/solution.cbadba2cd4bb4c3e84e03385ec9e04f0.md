At $x$, the intervals $[x,x+1/n)$ form a countable local basis: any basic interval containing $x$ contains one of them. Every nonempty basic interval contains a rational, so $\Q$ is dense. Suppose a countable basis $\mathcal B$ existed. For each real $x$, choose $B_x\in\mathcal B$ with $x\in B_x\subseteq[x,x+1)$. If $x<y$ and $B_x=B_y$, then $x\in B_y\subseteq[y,y+1)$, impossible. Thus $x\mapsto B_x$ is an injection of $\R$ into a countable family, a contradiction.
