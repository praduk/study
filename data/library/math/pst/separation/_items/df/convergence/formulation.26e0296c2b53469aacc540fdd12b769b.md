A sequence $x_n$ converges to $x$ if it is eventually in each neighborhood of $x$. More generally a directed set is a nonempty preordered set in which any two indices have a common upper bound. A net is a family $(x_d)_{d\in D}$ indexed by a directed set; convergence again means eventual membership in every neighborhood.

Nets capture closure in arbitrary spaces. Sequences do so in @[first-countable]math:pst:separation:df:countability spaces, but cannot safely replace nets in every space.
