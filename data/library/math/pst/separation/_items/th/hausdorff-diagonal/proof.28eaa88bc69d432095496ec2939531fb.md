If $x\ne y$ in a Hausdorff space, choose disjoint open neighborhoods $U,V$. Then $U\times V$ contains $(x,y)$ and avoids $\Delta$, so the complement of $\Delta$ is open. Conversely, if $\Delta$ is closed, any $(x,y)$ with $x\ne y$ lies in a basic rectangle $U\times V$ disjoint from $\Delta$. A point in $U\cap V$ would give a diagonal point in that rectangle. Hence $U,V$ are disjoint neighborhoods of $x,y$.
