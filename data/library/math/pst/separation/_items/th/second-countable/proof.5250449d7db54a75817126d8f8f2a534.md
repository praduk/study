Let $\{B_n\}$ be a countable basis. The members containing a given point form a countable local basis. Choose one point from each nonempty $B_n$; any nonempty open set contains such a member and thus a chosen point, proving density. For an open cover, consider basis members contained in some cover member, and choose one covering member for each such basis member. The chosen collection is countable and still covers every point. For a subspace $A$, the sets $A\cap B_n$ form a countable basis. Empty spaces satisfy all four conclusions directly.
