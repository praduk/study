A map $f:X\to Y$ is continuous if $f^{-1}(V)$ is open in $X$ for every open $V\subseteq Y$. A homeomorphism is a bijection for which both $f$ and $f^{-1}$ are continuous.

Continuity can be tested on a subbasis of $Y$: inverse images preserve unions and finite intersections. Equivalently, inverse images of closed sets are closed. A continuous bijection is not automatically a homeomorphism; from a discrete copy of $\R$ to its usual topology, the identity is a counterexample.
