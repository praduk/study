A topology $\mathcal T$ on a set $X$ contains $\nulset$ and $X$, is closed under arbitrary unions, and is closed under finite intersections. Members are open; $F\subseteq X$ is closed when $X\setminus F$ is open. The pair $(X,\mathcal T)$ is a topological space.

If $\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal S$, then $\mathcal S$ is finer than $\mathcal T$. The discrete topology is $\mathcal P(X)$; the indiscrete topology is $\{\nulset,X\}$. An infinite intersection of open sets need not be open: in $\R$, $\bigcap_{n\ge1}(-1/n,1/n)=\{0\}$.
