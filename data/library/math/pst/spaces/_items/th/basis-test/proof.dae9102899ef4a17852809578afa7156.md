Let $\mathcal T$ consist of all unions of basis members. The empty union is empty and the covering condition puts $X$ in $\mathcal T$. Arbitrary unions remain unions of basis members. If $U,V\in\mathcal T$ and $x\in U\cap V$, choose basis members $B_1,B_2$ containing $x$ inside $U,V$. The refinement condition supplies $B_3$ containing $x$ inside $U\cap V$. Thus $U\cap V$ is the union of all basis members it contains. Induction gives finite intersections. Every topology with this family as a basis must have exactly these unions, proving uniqueness.
