A nonempty union includes one cofinite set, so its complement is a subset of a finite set. A finite intersection of cofinite sets has complement a finite union of finite sets. Thus these sets form a topology. The closed subsets are $X$ and the finite subsets. The only closed set containing an infinite subset $A$ is $X$, so $\closure A=X$. Any two nonempty open sets intersect: their intersection has finite complement and an infinite underlying set. Distinct points therefore cannot have disjoint open neighborhoods, so the space is not Hausdorff.
