A space is compact if every open cover has a finite subcover. A family of sets has the finite-intersection property if each finite subfamily has nonempty intersection. Taking complements shows: a space is compact exactly when every family of closed sets with this property has nonempty total intersection.

Compactness alone does not assert Hausdorffness. The empty space is compact, and the empty family of subsets has intersection equal to the whole ambient space.
