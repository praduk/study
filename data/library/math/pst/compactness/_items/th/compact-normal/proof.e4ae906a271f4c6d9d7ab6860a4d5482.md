It is $T_1$. First separate a point $x$ from a disjoint closed set $F$: the proof that compact subsets are closed actually constructs disjoint open neighborhoods of $x$ and $F$ by taking finite intersections and unions of Hausdorff neighborhoods. Now let $A,B$ be disjoint closed sets. For each $a\in A$, choose disjoint open sets $U_a\supseteq\{a\}$ and $V_a\supseteq B$. Compactness of $A$ provides finitely many $U_{a_i}$ covering it. Then $\bigcup_iU_{a_i}$ and $\bigcap_iV_{a_i}$ are disjoint open neighborhoods of $A$ and $B$. Empty closed sets are handled directly.
