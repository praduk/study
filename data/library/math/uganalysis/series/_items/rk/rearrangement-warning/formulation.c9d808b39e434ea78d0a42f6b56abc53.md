The alternating harmonic series converges by the alternating series theorem while its absolute values form the divergent harmonic series. Its positive-term and negative-magnitude subseries both diverge: the positive terms dominate half a harmonic tail, and $\sum_{n=1}^N(2n)^{-1}=\tfrac12\sum_{n=1}^N n^{-1}$.

This permits a rearrangement toward any prescribed real target: take unused positive terms until the partial sum exceeds the target, then unused negative terms until it falls below, and repeat. Each crossing error is bounded by the last used term, which tends to zero. Both lists are exhausted because infinitely many crossing stages consume an initial segment of each. Therefore the new series converges to that target. @[Absolute convergence]math:uganalysis:series:df:series-convergence is the condition that prevents this phenomenon.
