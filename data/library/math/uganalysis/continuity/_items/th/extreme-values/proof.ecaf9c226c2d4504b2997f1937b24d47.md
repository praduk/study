If $f$ were unbounded, choose $x_n\in[a,b]$ with $|f(x_n)|>n$. A convergent subsequence has limit $x\in[a,b]$, and continuity forces its function values to converge to $f(x)$, a contradiction. Let $M=\sup f([a,b])$. Choose $x_n$ with $f(x_n)>M-1/n$ and take a convergent subsequence. Continuity shows the value at its limit is $M$. Apply the maximum argument to $-f$ for the minimum.
