For $f:E\to\R$ and an accumulation point $a$ of $E$, the limit $f(x)\to L$ as $x\to a$ means every $\epsilon>0$ admits $\delta>0$ such that $x\in E$ and $0<|x-a|<\delta$ imply $|f(x)-L|<\epsilon$. Continuity at $a\in E$ instead requires $|f(x)-f(a)|<\epsilon$ whenever $x\in E$ and $|x-a|<\delta$.

Uniform continuity on $E$ means $\delta$ depends only on $\epsilon$, and works for every pair $x,y\in E$. A Lipschitz bound $|f(x)-f(y)|\le L|x-y|$ implies uniform continuity.
