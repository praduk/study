Refining a partition can only increase its lower sum and decrease its upper sum. A common refinement therefore shows that every lower sum is at most every upper sum. If the integrals agree at $I$, choose partitions with lower sum greater than $I-\epsilon/2$ and upper sum less than $I+\epsilon/2$; their common refinement works. Conversely, if a partition has upper-minus-lower gap below $\epsilon$, the upper integral minus lower integral is bounded by that gap. As this holds for every $\epsilon>0$, the integrals agree.
