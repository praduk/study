The oscillation of $f+g$ on an interval is at most the sum of their oscillations. Choose partitions with small Darboux gaps for $f$ and $g$ and take a common refinement; this proves sum integrability. The inequalities

$$
L(f,P)+L(g,P)\le L(f+g,P)\le U(f+g,P)\le U(f,P)+U(g,P)
$$

then squeeze the sum integral to the sum of the integrals as the two gaps tend to zero. Positive scaling scales both sums; negative scaling interchanges upper and lower sums with the appropriate sign. These facts give linearity. If $f\le g$, every lower sum for $f$ is at most the corresponding lower sum for $g$, proving integral order.

Insert $c$ in a partition. Its upper and lower sums are sums of the corresponding quantities on the two subintervals. Refinement cannot increase the gap, and each subinterval gap is nonnegative, so integrability on the full interval implies integrability on each part. Taking partitions with gaps tending to zero yields interval additivity. Finally, $||f(x)|-|f(y)||\le|f(x)-f(y)|$ bounds the oscillation of $|f|$ by that of $f$, giving its integrability. Apply order to $-|f|\le f\le|f|$ and to $|f|\le\sup|f|$ to obtain the bounds.
