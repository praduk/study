For $f:(a,b)\to\R$ and $x\in(a,b)$, the derivative is

$$
f'(x)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}
$$

when this finite limit exists. Equivalently, $f(x+h)=f(x)+f'(x)h+r(h)$ with $r(h)/|h|\to0$. Differentiability implies continuity because both $f'(x)h$ and $r(h)$ tend to zero. A one-sided derivative at an endpoint uses only increments remaining in the domain.
