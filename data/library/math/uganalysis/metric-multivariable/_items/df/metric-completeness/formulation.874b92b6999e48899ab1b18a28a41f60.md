A metric $d:X\times X\to[0,\infty)$ is symmetric, vanishes exactly when its arguments agree, and satisfies $d(x,z)\le d(x,y)+d(y,z)$. An open ball is $B(x,r)=\{y:d(x,y)<r\}$; an open set contains a ball about each of its points. Convergence and the Cauchy condition use $d$ in place of absolute value.

A metric space is complete when every Cauchy sequence converges in it. A subset is compact when every cover by relatively open sets has a finite subcover. Closed bounded subsets of $\R^n$ are compact; the corresponding statement fails in general infinite-dimensional spaces. The Euclidean compactness theorem is used as a standard multivariable prerequisite here.
