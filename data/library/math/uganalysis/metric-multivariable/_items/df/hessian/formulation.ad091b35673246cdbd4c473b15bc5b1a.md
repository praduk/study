For $f\in C^2(U)$ on open $U\subseteq\R^n$, its Hessian at $a$ is the symmetric bilinear second derivative $D^2f(a)$, represented in Cartesian coordinates by $H_{ij}=\partial_i\partial_j f(a)$. The symmetry follows from equality of continuous mixed second partial derivatives. A critical point has $Df(a)=0$; a critical point is nondegenerate if its Hessian matrix is invertible.

On a @[smooth manifold]math:diffgeo:manifolds:df:smooth-manifold with a connection $\nabla$, define $\operatorname{Hess}_\nabla f(X,Y)=X(Yf)-(\nabla_XY)f$. In coordinates it is $\partial_i\partial_j f-\Gamma^k_{ij}\partial_kf$. It is a tensor because the derivatives of coefficient functions cancel, and it is symmetric when the connection is torsion-free. At a critical point the correction term vanishes, so the Hessian there is independent of the chosen connection.

Positive definiteness, negative definiteness, and degeneracy refer to @math:algebra:linear-foundations:df:bilinear-forms. A vanishing gradient alone does not imply an extremum: $f(x,y)=x^2-y^2$ has a critical point at the origin and opposite signs along the two coordinate axes. For a connection's definition see @math:diffgeo:connections:df:connection.
