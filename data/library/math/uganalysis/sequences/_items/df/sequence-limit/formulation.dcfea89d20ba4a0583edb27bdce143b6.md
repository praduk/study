A real sequence $(x_n)_{n\ge1}$ converges to $L\in\R$ when for every $\epsilon>0$ there is an integer $N$ such that $n\ge N$ implies $|x_n-L|<\epsilon$. It is Cauchy when for every $\epsilon>0$ there is $N$ such that $m,n\ge N$ implies $|x_m-x_n|<\epsilon$.

A subsequence has the form $(x_{n_k})$ where $n_1<n_2<\cdots$. Convergence controls the entire tail, not just infinitely many terms.
