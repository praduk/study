If $x_n$ had two distinct limits $x,x'$, eventually $|x_n-x|,|x_n-x'|<|x-x'|/3$, contradicting the triangle inequality. Sums follow from $|(x_n+y_n)-(x+y)|\le|x_n-x|+|y_n-y|$. A convergent sequence is bounded: its tail lies within $1$ of its limit and the remaining set is finite. Thus $|x_ny_n-xy|\le |x_n||y_n-y|+|y||x_n-x|\to0$. If $y\ne0$, eventually $|y_n|\ge|y|/2$, and $|1/y_n-1/y|\le2|y_n-y|/|y|^2\to0$. Apply the product result.
