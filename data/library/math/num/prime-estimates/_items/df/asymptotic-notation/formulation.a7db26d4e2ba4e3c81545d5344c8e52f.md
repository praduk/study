For real functions with $g(x)>0$ for sufficiently large $x$, $f(x)=O(g(x))$ means $|f(x)|\le Cg(x)$ eventually for some fixed $C$. The relation $f(x)=o(g(x))$ means $f(x)/g(x)\to0$, and $f(x)\sim g(x)$ means their ratio tends to $1$. The prime-counting function is $\pi(x)=\#\{p\le x:p\text{ prime}\}$. Infinitely many primes only says $\pi(x)\to\infty$; it provides no asymptotic rate.
