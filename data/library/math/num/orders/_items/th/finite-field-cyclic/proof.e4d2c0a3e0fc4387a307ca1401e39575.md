Let $m$ be the least common multiple of all element orders. For every prime power $\ell^b$ dividing $m$ maximally, some element has order $\ell^b c$ with $\ell\nmid c$. Its $c$th power has order $\ell^b$. The product of these chosen elements has order $m$: for commuting elements of coprime orders, a power of the product is $1$ only when both factors' corresponding powers are $1$, since the two cyclic subgroups have trivial intersection. Induct over the primes. Every element of $G$ is a root of $X^m-1$. A nonzero polynomial of degree $m$ over a field has at most $m$ roots (divide by $X-a$ and induct). Thus $|G|\le m$. The element of order $m$ already contributes $m$ distinct powers in $G$, proving equality and cyclicity. Take $G=(\Z/p\Z)^\times$, a group because Bézout gives inverses for every nonzero class.
