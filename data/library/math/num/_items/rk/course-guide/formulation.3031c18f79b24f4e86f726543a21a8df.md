This course develops elementary number theory from divisibility through @[quadratic reciprocity]math:num:quadratic-residues:th:quadratic-reciprocity, Diophantine equations, @[continued fractions]math:num:continued-fractions:df:simple-continued-fraction, and elementary analytic estimates. Integers and ordinary induction are assumed; no abstract algebra course is required. Throughout, $\N=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$; a positive integer is stated explicitly when zero must be excluded.

The sequence is divisibility and primes; congruences; multiplicative arithmetic; orders and primitive roots; @[quadratic residues]math:num:quadratic-residues:df:legendre-symbol; Diophantine equations; continued fractions; and elementary prime estimates and RSA. Each problem has a separate worked solution. Proofs and solutions are original internal derivations, using the exact hypotheses in their statements.

Karl-Dieter Crisman's [Number Theory: In Context and Interactive](https://math.gordon.edu/ntic/) was inspected as an upper-division scope benchmark: modular arithmetic, primes, quadratic reciprocity, arithmetic functions, cryptographic applications, and analytic connections. This is an independently organized course, not a transcription of that book. Algebraic number fields, class groups, analytic continuation of zeta functions, and proofs of prime distribution theorems belong to further courses.
