Replace every residue exceeding $p/2$ by its negative modulo $p$. The resulting numbers in $\{1,\ldots,m\}$ are distinct: an equality would imply $ai\equiv\pm aj$, whence $i=j$ because $1\le i,j\le m$. Their product is therefore $m!$. The original product is both $a^m m!$ and $(-1)^N m!$ modulo $p$. Cancel $m!$ and use Euler’s criterion. For $a=2$, the relevant residues are $2,4,\ldots,2m$ and $N=m-\lfloor p/4\rfloor$. Checking $p=1,3,5,7\pmod8$ gives the same parity as $(p^2-1)/8$.
