Put $m=(p-1)/2$ and $n=(q-1)/2$. First, for any positive odd $a$ coprime to $p$, write $ai=p\lfloor ai/p\rfloor+r_i$ for $1\le i\le m$. Let $s_i=\min(r_i,p-r_i)$. Let $N$ count the residues $r_i>p/2$. As in @math:num:quadratic-residues:th:gauss-lemma, the $s_i$ permute $1,\ldots,m$. Reducing the sum of the displayed equations modulo $2$, using that $a,p$ are odd and that $\sum r_i\equiv\sum s_i+N\pmod2$, gives $\sum_{i=1}^m\lfloor ai/p\rfloor\equiv N\pmod2$. Hence the symbol has sign $(-1)^{\sum\lfloor ai/p\rfloor}$.

Now count lattice pairs $1\le i\le m$, $1\le j\le n$. None satisfy $qi=pj$, since $p,q$ are distinct primes and $i<p$, $j<q$. Exactly $\lfloor qi/p\rfloor$ pairs for a fixed $i$ satisfy $pj<qi$; this floor is at most $n$. Exactly $\lfloor pj/q\rfloor$ pairs for fixed $j$ satisfy $qi<pj$. Therefore the two floor sums add to $mn$. The sign formula for $(q/p)$ and $(p/q)$ proves reciprocity.
