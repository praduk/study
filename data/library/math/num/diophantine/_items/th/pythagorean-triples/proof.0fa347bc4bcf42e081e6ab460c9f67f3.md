The legs cannot both be odd, since a square is $0$ or $1$ modulo $4$; they cannot both be even by coprimality. Take $b$ even and $a,c$ odd. Set $r=(c+a)/2$, $s=(c-a)/2$. They are positive, $rs=(b/2)^2$, and $\gcd(r,s)=1$: any common divisor divides $c,a$, which are coprime, since a prime dividing both would divide $b$ too. Coprime positive factors of a square are squares by prime factorization, so $r=u^2$, $s=v^2$. This gives the formulas and $\gcd(u,v)=1$; because $a$ is odd, their parities differ. Conversely direct expansion proves the equation. No odd prime can divide both legs without dividing both $u,v$, and $2$ cannot divide $u^2-v^2$, proving primitivity.
