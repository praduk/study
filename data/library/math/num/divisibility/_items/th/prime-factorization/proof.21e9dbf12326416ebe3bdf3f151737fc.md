If $p\nmid a$, its only possible common positive divisor with $a$ is $1$. Bézout gives $up+va=1$; multiplying by $b$ proves $p\mid b$. Existence of a factorization follows by strong induction: a composite $n$ is $ab$ with $1<a,b<n$, and both factors have prime factorizations. For uniqueness, a prime in one factorization divides the product in another, hence divides one of its prime factors and equals it. Cancel that common prime and repeat. The empty product accounts for $1$.
