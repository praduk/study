A first-order signature $L$ consists of disjoint sets of constant symbols, function symbols, and relation symbols, with a specified positive finite arity for each function and relation symbol. Constants may equivalently be treated as nullary function symbols. Equality $=$, connectives, quantifiers, variables, and punctuation are logical symbols and are not members of $L$.

An $L$-expression may use only the nonlogical symbols in $L$. A sublanguage $L_0 ⊆ L$ retains symbols with the same arities. An expansion from $L_0$ to $L$ adds interpretations for the new symbols; its reduct forgets those interpretations.
