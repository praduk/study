For each $n≥1$, a sentence can assert that at least $n$ distinct objects exist. The set of all these sentences therefore has exactly infinite structures as models.

By contrast, no first-order theory has precisely all finite $L$-structures as its models. If such a theory $T$ existed, adjoining every “at least $n$” sentence would be finitely satisfiable: choose a sufficiently large finite model of the finite part of $T$. Compactness would then give an infinite model of $T$, contradicting the specification. A single sentence defining finiteness is therefore also impossible.
