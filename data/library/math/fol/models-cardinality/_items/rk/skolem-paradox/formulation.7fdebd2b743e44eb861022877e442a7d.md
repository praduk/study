If a countable first-order set theory has an infinite model, downward Löwenheim–Skolem gives a countable model. Such a model may nevertheless satisfy a sentence saying that one of its sets is uncountable.

There is no contradiction. Externally, the model's domain and all of its elements can be countable. Internally, `uncountable` means that no function which the model recognizes as a function is a bijection from its internal natural numbers onto that set. An external enumeration need not be represented by any element of the model. Löwenheim–Skolem concerns model size, not the truth of Cantor's theorem inside a model.
