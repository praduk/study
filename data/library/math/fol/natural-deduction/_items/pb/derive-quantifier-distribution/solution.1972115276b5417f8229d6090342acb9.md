Assume $∀x(φ→ψ)$. Assume $∃xφ$. For existential elimination choose a fresh $y$ and temporarily assume $φ[y/x]$. Universal elimination on the first assumption gives $φ[y/x]→ψ[y/x]$. Because $x$ is not free in $ψ$, $ψ[y/x]=ψ$. Modus ponens yields $ψ$. The fresh $y$ occurs free in neither $ψ$ nor either undischarged outer assumption, so existential elimination discharges $φ[y/x]$ and yields $ψ$. Implication introduction discharges $∃xφ$, producing $∃xφ→ψ$, and a second implication introduction discharges the universal premise. No classical rule was needed.
