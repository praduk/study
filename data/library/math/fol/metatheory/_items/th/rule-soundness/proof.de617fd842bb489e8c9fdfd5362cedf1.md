Conjunction introduction and elimination, implication elimination, and disjunction introduction follow immediately from the classical truth conditions, with the premise contexts combined when necessary. For implication introduction, suppose $Δ∪\{φ\}⊨ψ$. If $𝔐,g⊨Δ$ and $𝔐,g⊨φ$, the premise consequence gives $𝔐,g⊨ψ$; hence $𝔐,g⊨φ→ψ$. Thus $Δ⊨φ→ψ$. For disjunction elimination, if $Δ⊨φ∨ψ$, $Σ∪\{φ\}⊨χ$, and $Π∪\{ψ\}⊨χ$, then any $𝔐,g$ satisfying $Δ∪Σ∪Π$ satisfies one of $φ,ψ$ and the corresponding branch consequence yields $χ$. Falsity elimination is vacuous because no assignment satisfies $⊥$. For classical reductio, if $Δ∪\{¬φ\}⊨⊥$ and some $𝔐,g⊨Δ$ made $φ$ false, classical semantics would make $¬φ$ true, contradicting the premise consequence; therefore $Δ⊨φ$.

For universal elimination, $Δ⊨∀xφ$ implies that any $𝔐,g⊨Δ$ satisfies $φ$ at $g[x↦t^𝔐[g]]$; semantic substitution gives $𝔐,g⊨φ[t/x]$. Existential introduction is the converse use of semantic substitution with witness $t^𝔐[g]$. For universal introduction, suppose $Δ⊨φ$ and $x$ is free in no member of $Δ$. Fix $𝔐,g⊨Δ$ and arbitrary $a∈M$. By coincidence, $𝔐,g[x↦a]⊨Δ$, so the premise consequence gives $𝔐,g[x↦a]⊨φ$. Since $a$ was arbitrary, $𝔐,g⊨∀xφ$.

For existential elimination, suppose $Δ⊨∃xφ$ and $Σ∪\{φ[y/x]\}⊨ψ$, where $y$ satisfies the stated freshness conditions. Fix $𝔐,g⊨Δ∪Σ$ and choose $a∈M$ witnessing $∃xφ$. Put $h=g[y↦a]$. Freshness and coincidence preserve every formula in $Δ∪Σ$; because $y$ does not occur in the existential premise, semantic substitution gives $𝔐,h⊨φ[y/x]$. The branch consequence yields $𝔐,h⊨ψ$, and because $y$ is not free in $ψ$, coincidence gives $𝔐,g⊨ψ$. Thus $Δ∪Σ⊨ψ$. Equality introduction uses reflexivity of identity. Equality elimination is sound because equal terms have the same denotation, and semantic substitution shows that replacing one by the other preserves the formula's truth.
