First prove the term-evaluation lemma. Given distinct variables $x_1,…,x_n$ and closed terms $t_1,…,t_n$, let $g_{\vec t}(x_i)=[t_i]$. For every term $u$ whose variables are among the $x_i$, induction on $u$ gives $u^{𝔐_Δ}[g_{\vec t}]=[u[t_1/x_1,…,t_n/x_n]]$. The variable and constant cases follow from the assignment and interpretation; the function case follows from the induction hypotheses and the definition $f^{𝔐_Δ}([s_1],…,[s_k])=[f(s_1,…,s_k)]$. Consequently, atomic relation formulas satisfy the truth lemma exactly by the definition of $R^{𝔐_Δ}$, and atomic equalities satisfy it exactly by the definition of the quotient.

Now induct on $φ$. First justify the Boolean membership laws. Negation-completeness and consistency give $¬α∈Δ$ iff $α∉Δ$. Introduction and elimination give $α∧β∈Δ$ iff both conjuncts belong. If one disjunct belongs, disjunction introduction puts $α∨β$ in $Δ$; if the disjunction belongs but neither disjunct does, then both negations belong and disjunction elimination derives $⊥$, impossible. For implication, either $¬α∈Δ$ or $β∈Δ$ suffices to derive $α→β$. Conversely, if $α→β∈Δ$ and $β∉Δ$, then $¬β∈Δ$; having $α∈Δ$ would derive $β$ and contradict consistency, so $α∉Δ$. These are exactly the classical truth clauses, and the induction hypotheses complete the Boolean cases.

For $∃xα$, if the term model satisfies the existential, its witness is some class $[t]$; induction gives $α(t)∈Δ$, and existential introduction plus deductive closure gives $∃xα∈Δ$. Conversely, if $∃xα∈Δ$, the Henkin property supplies a closed $c$ with $∃xα→α(c)∈Δ$. Deductive closure gives $α(c)∈Δ$, and induction makes $[c]$ a semantic witness.

For $∀xα$, membership of $∀xα$ puts every closed instance $α(t)$ in $Δ$ by universal elimination. Induction makes $α$ true of every domain element, since each is some $[t]$. Conversely, suppose $∀xα∉Δ$. Then $¬∀xα∈Δ$. The classical rules derive $¬∀xα→∃x¬α$: under $¬∀xα$, reductio on $¬∃x¬α$ makes an arbitrary fresh instance $α(y)$ true, universal introduction gives $∀xα$, and contradiction discharges the reductio. Thus $∃x¬α∈Δ$. Henkin witnessing gives a closed $c$ with $¬α(c)∈Δ$; induction makes $α$ false at $[c]$, so the universal is false. This completes the structural induction.
