For sentences, argue contrapositively. If $Γ⊬φ$, then $Γ∪\{¬φ\}$ is consistent. Otherwise implication introduction would give $Γ⊢¬¬φ$, and classical double-negation elimination would give $Γ⊢φ$. By model existence there is $𝔐⊨Γ∪\{¬φ\}$, so $𝔐$ satisfies every premise and falsifies $φ$. Hence $Γ⊭φ$. This proves $Γ⊨φ⇒Γ⊢φ$; soundness gives the reverse implication.

For arbitrary formulas, choose a distinct new constant $c_x$ for every variable free in a member of $Γ∪\{φ\}$, and replace each such free $x$ uniformly by $c_x$. Call the resulting sentences $Γ^c$ and $φ^c$. Assignments in an $L$-structure correspond exactly to expansions interpreting the constants $c_x$, so $Γ⊨φ$ iff $Γ^c⊨φ^c$. The sentence case gives a finite derivation $Γ^c⊢φ^c$. Before translating it back, alpha-rename all bound variables and rule eigenvariables away from the finitely many original free variables used in that derivation. Uniformly replace each $c_x$ by $x$. Every rule instance and side condition is then preserved, yielding $Γ⊢φ$. Thus the formula form follows without identifying $⊢$ with $⊨$.
