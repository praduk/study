A theory $H$ has the Henkin witness property when, for every formula $φ(x)$ with at most $x$ free, there is a closed term $t$ such that $(∃xφ(x)→φ(t))∈H$. A Henkin construction normally adds a fresh constant $c_φ$ and the witness sentence $∃xφ(x)→φ(c_φ)$.

The witness sentence does not assert $∃xφ(x)$. It says only that if a witness exists, the new name denotes one. Freshness is necessary in the consistency argument.
