In a @math:algebra:linear-foundations:df:vector-space $V$ over $K$, the span of a family $(v_i)_{i\in I}$ consists of its finite linear combinations. The family is linearly independent when every finite relation $\sum_{i\in F}a_iv_i=0$ has all coefficients zero. A basis is an independent spanning family; equivalently, every vector has exactly one finite expansion in that family.

A space is finite dimensional when it has a finite basis. Its dimension is the number of vectors in a basis; the elementary exchange lemma ensures that this number does not depend on the basis. Explicitly, replacing a basis vector having a nonzero coefficient in a new vector's expansion preserves spanning; repeating this replacement bounds the size of any independent family by the size of any finite spanning family, in both directions for two bases. The empty family is a basis of the zero space, of dimension zero.

For ordered bases $(e_j)$ of $V$ and $(f_i)$ of $W$, the matrix of a @[linear map]math:algebra:linear-foundations:df:vector-space $T$ has entries $A_{ij}$ defined by $T(e_j)=\sum_i A_{ij}f_i$. Its columns are the output coordinates of the input basis vectors. Matrix multiplication represents composition in compatible bases.
