A ring has an abelian additive group and associative, distributive multiplication with identity. It may be noncommutative and may be the zero ring. A two-sided ideal $I$ is an additive subgroup satisfying $rI\subseteq I$ and $Ir\subseteq I$ for all $r$. The quotient $R/I$ has $(a+I)(b+I)=ab+I$.

For commutative $R$, $(a_1,\ldots,a_n)=\{\sum r_i a_i:r_i\in R\}$. An ideal is proper if it differs from $R$, equivalently if $1\notin I$. A ring homomorphism preserves addition, multiplication, and identity.
