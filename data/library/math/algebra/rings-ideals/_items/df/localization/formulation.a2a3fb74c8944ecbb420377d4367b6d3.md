Let $R$ be commutative and $S\subseteq R$ contain $1$ and be closed under multiplication. The localization $S^{-1}R$ consists of pairs $r/s$, where

$$
r/s=r'/s'\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t(rs'-r's)=0\text{ for some }t\in S.
$$

Addition and multiplication use the usual fraction rules. This definition allows zero divisors; the map $R\to S^{-1}R$ need not be injective. Every homomorphism $f:R\to T$ sending $S$ to units factors uniquely by $r/s\mapsto f(r)f(s)^{-1}$.

A nonzero commutative ring is local if it has a unique maximal ideal. For a prime ideal $P$, write $R_P=(R\setminus P)^{-1}R$.
