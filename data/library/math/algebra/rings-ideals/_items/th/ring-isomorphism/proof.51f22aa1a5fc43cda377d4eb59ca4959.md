The kernel is a two-sided ideal. The map $r+\ker f\mapsto f(r)$ is well defined, multiplicative, additive, and unital; equality of images means the representatives differ by an element of the kernel, so it is injective, and it is surjective onto the image. For correspondence, the quotient projection pulls every ideal back to an ideal containing $I$. For $J\supseteq I$, its image $J/I$ is an ideal because the projection is surjective. Pulling this image back recovers $J$; taking the image of an inverse image recovers the original quotient ideal.
