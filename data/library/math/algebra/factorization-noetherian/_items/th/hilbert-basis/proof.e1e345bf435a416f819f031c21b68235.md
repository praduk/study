Let $I\subseteq R[x]$ be an ideal. For each $n\ge0$, let $A_n$ be the ideal of coefficients of $x^n$ in polynomials of $I$ of degree at most $n$. Multiplication by $x$ gives $A_n\subseteq A_{n+1}$. The chain stabilizes at $N$. For each $0\le n\le N$, choose finitely many generators of $A_n$ and polynomials $f_{nj}\in I$ of degree at most $n$ realizing those coefficients; skip zero generators.

These finitely many polynomials generate $I$. Indeed, given a nonzero $f\in I$ of degree $d$, if $d<N$ its leading coefficient is a combination of the degree-$d$ coefficients of the $f_{dj}$; if $d\ge N$, use $A_d=A_N$ and multiply the $f_{Nj}$ by $x^{d-N}$. Subtracting the chosen combination cancels the leading term. Induction on degree expresses $f$ in the generated ideal. Thus every ideal is finitely generated. Iterate the result, starting with a field.
