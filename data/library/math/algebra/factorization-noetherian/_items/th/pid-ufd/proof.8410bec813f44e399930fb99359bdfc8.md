First a PID has no infinite strictly ascending chain of ideals: the union of an ascending chain is an ideal $(a)$, and $a$ belongs to one member, which then equals the union. If a nonzero nonunit had no irreducible factorization, factor it into two nonunits; at least one factor still has no such factorization. Iteration gives an infinite strictly ascending chain of principal ideals, a contradiction.

If $p$ is irreducible, any ideal $(d)$ containing $(p)$ has $p=dc$, so $d$ is a unit or an associate of $p$. Thus $(p)$ is maximal and hence prime. Every irreducible is therefore prime. Given two irreducible factorizations, the first irreducible in one divides an irreducible in the other, so they are associates. Cancel and induct on the number of factors to obtain uniqueness.
