We first justify the needed form of Gauss's lemma. A primitive integer polynomial has coefficients with gcd $1$. A product of primitive polynomials is primitive: otherwise a prime dividing all product coefficients would make their reductions have zero product in the domain $\F_p[x]$, although neither reduction is zero. Clearing denominators and dividing contents now shows that a factorization over $\Q$ of a primitive integer polynomial yields a factorization over $\Z$: the rational scalar left over must be an integer because the product is primitive, and must have absolute value $1$ by primitiveness.

Divide $f$ by its content; this does not alter the Eisenstein hypotheses because its content is prime to $p$. If $f=gh$ with both degrees positive, reduction modulo $p$ is a nonzero multiple of $x^n$. Both leading coefficients remain nonzero modulo $p$, so both reduced factors have their original positive degrees. A divisor of $x^n$ in $\F_p[x]$ is a scalar times a power of $x$; thus both constant coefficients are divisible by $p$. Their product $a_0$ is then divisible by $p^2$, a contradiction.
