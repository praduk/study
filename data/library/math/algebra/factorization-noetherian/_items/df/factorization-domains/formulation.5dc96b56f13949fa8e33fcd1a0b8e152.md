In a domain, a unit is an invertible element; two elements are associates if one is a unit times the other. A nonzero nonunit is irreducible if every factorization has a unit factor, and prime if dividing a product forces it to divide a factor.

A Euclidean domain has a function $d:R\setminus\{0\}\to\N$ allowing $a=qb+r$ with $r=0$ or $d(r)<d(b)$ whenever $b\ne0$. A principal ideal domain (PID) is a domain whose every ideal has one generator. A unique factorization domain (UFD) is a domain where every nonzero nonunit factors into irreducibles, uniquely up to order and associates.
