Factor each invariant factor into powers of the relatively prime linear factors $x-\lambda$. Repeated use of the Chinese remainder theorem splits its cyclic module into $k[x]/((x-\lambda)^m)$. In the ordered basis $(x-\lambda)^{m-1},\ldots,(x-\lambda),1$, multiplication by $x$ has diagonal $\lambda$ and ones immediately above the diagonal. On this block, the kernel of $(T-\lambda I)^j$ has dimension $\min(j,m)$. Blocks for other eigenvalues contribute zero, because $T-\lambda I$ is invertible on them. Taking successive differences counts blocks of size at least $j$, which recovers the multiset and proves uniqueness.
