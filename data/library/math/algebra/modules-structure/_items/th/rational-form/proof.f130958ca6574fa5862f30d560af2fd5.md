A basis of $V$ over $k$ generates it over $k[x]$. The operators $1,T,T^2,\ldots$ lie in the finite-dimensional space $\End_k(V)$, so some nonzero polynomial annihilates $T$. Hence this module is torsion; the PID structure theorem gives the asserted decomposition with no free summand. On $k[x]/(d_i)$, multiplication by $x$ in the displayed basis is the companion matrix, whose characteristic polynomial is $d_i$ by expansion of its determinant. The annihilator of the direct sum is the intersection of the ideals $(d_i)$, equal to $(d_t)$ under divisibility. Characteristic polynomials multiply across block sums. The zero-dimensional case has an empty block sum.
