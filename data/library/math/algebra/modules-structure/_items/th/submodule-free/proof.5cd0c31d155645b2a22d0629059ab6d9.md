Induct on $n$, with $n=0$ immediate. Project onto the first coordinate. The image of $N$ is an ideal $(a)$. If $a=0$, regard $N$ as a submodule of $R^{n-1}$ and use induction. Otherwise choose $v\in N$ with first coordinate $a$. For $w\in N$, its first coordinate is $ra$ for a unique $r$ because $R$ is a domain, and $w-rv$ has first coordinate zero. Thus $N=Rv\oplus(N\cap(0\times R^{n-1}))$. The first summand is free of rank one, because $sv=0$ implies $sa=0$ in the first coordinate, hence $s=0$. Induction handles the second summand.
