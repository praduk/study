A left module $M$ over a ring $R$ is an @[abelian group]math:algebra:groups-actions:df:groups-homomorphisms with scalar multiplication satisfying $r(m+n)=rm+rn$, $(r+s)m=rm+sm$, $(rs)m=r(sm)$, and $1m=m$. A homomorphism is additive and preserves scalar multiplication. A submodule is an additive subgroup closed under scalars.

A basis is a family $(e_i)$ such that every element has a unique finite linear combination in these elements. A module with a basis is free. The direct sum $\bigoplus_i M_i$ contains tuples of finite support. A module is finitely generated if finitely many elements span it; generators need not be a basis. For example $\Z/2\Z$ is a finitely generated, nonfree $\Z$-module.

An $R$-linear combination of a family consists of a finite sum $\sum_i r_i m_i$ with coefficients in $R$. The family is linearly independent over $R$ when every such finite sum equal to zero has all coefficients zero. An $R$-linear map means a module homomorphism, so it preserves these combinations. The scalars here need not form a field; vector-space dimension arguments therefore require their own hypotheses.
