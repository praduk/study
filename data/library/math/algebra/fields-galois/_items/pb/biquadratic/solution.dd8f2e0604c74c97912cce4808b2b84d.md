The degree of $\Q(\sqrt2)$ is $2$. If $\sqrt3=a+b\sqrt2$ for rational $a,b$, squaring gives $3=a^2+2b^2+2ab\sqrt2$, so $ab=0$. The alternatives require a rational square to equal $3$ or $3/2$, both impossible by prime factorization of numerator and denominator. Hence $[L:\Q]=4$, with basis $1,\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\sqrt6$.

The four sign choices for $\sqrt2,\sqrt3$ define distinct automorphisms, checked by multiplication in this basis. They form $C_2\times C_2$. The extension is separable in characteristic zero. For normality, the orbit of any $a\in L$ under the four sign changes gives a polynomial $\prod_b(x-b)$, over its distinct orbit elements. Its coefficients are fixed by both sign changes and hence lie in $\Q$, as the displayed basis shows. The minimal polynomial of $a$ divides this polynomial and therefore splits in $L$. Thus $L/\Q$ is normal. The three subgroups of order two have fixed fields $\Q(\sqrt2)$, $\Q(\sqrt3)$, and $\Q(\sqrt6)$ respectively. By the Galois correspondence the full list is these three fields together with $\Q$ and $L$.
