In a @[splitting field]math:algebra:fields-galois:df:separable-normal write $f=(x-a)^m g$ with $g(a)\ne0$. Differentiation gives $f'=m(x-a)^{m-1}g+(x-a)^mg'$. Thus $m\ge2$ implies that $a$ is a common root. Conversely, if $m=1$, then $f'(a)=g(a)\ne0$. Common roots are equivalent to a nonconstant common divisor, by factorization and the Euclidean gcd identity. In characteristic zero, a nonconstant irreducible $f$ has $f'\ne0$ and $\deg f'<\deg f$, so its gcd with $f'$ is $1$.
