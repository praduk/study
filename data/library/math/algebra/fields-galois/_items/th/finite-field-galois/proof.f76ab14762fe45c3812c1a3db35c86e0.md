As a finite-dimensional $K$-vector space, $L$ has $q^{[L:K]}$ elements, so its degree is $n$. Every nonzero element of $L$ satisfies $a^{q^n-1}=1$ by Lagrange in $L^\times$. The polynomial $x^{q^n}-x$ splits into distinct linear factors over $L$, since it has all $q^n$ elements as roots and derivative $-1$. The minimal polynomial over $K$ of any element of $L$ divides this polynomial, so has distinct roots and splits in $L$. This proves separability and normality directly.

Writing $q=p^r$ in the characteristic $p$, the binomial identity shows $a\mapsto a^q$ is a field homomorphism. It is injective and thus surjective on a finite field. It fixes $K$ and satisfies $\sigma^n=\id$. If $\sigma^d=\id$ for $0<d<n$, then all $q^n$ elements would be roots of the nonzero degree-$q^d$ polynomial $x^{q^d}-x$, impossible. Hence it has order $n$. The finite Galois correspondence gives $|\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)|=[L:K]=n$, so these powers are the whole group.
