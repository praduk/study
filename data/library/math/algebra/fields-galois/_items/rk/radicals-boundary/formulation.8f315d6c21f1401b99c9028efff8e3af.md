For a polynomial over a field of characteristic zero, solvability by radicals means all its roots lie in a field obtained through finitely many adjunctions of elements whose positive integer powers lie in the preceding field. The polynomial is solvable by radicals exactly when its splitting-field Galois group is solvable.

The proof of this criterion is not included. It is checked in Milne's [Fields and Galois Theory, applications of Galois theory](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/FT.pdf). The group-theoretic definition is @math:algebra:finite-groups:df:solvable. A polynomial of degree five need not have nonsolvable Galois group: $x^5-2$ has its roots given using a fifth root and roots of unity. Degree alone cannot justify a claim of nonsolvability.
