Sylow gives $n_5\mid3$ and $n_5\equiv1\pmod5$, hence $n_5=1$. Similarly $n_3\mid5$ and $n_3\equiv1\pmod3$ imply $n_3=1$. Let the @[normal subgroups]math:algebra:groups-actions:df:normal-quotient be $P$ and $Q$, of orders $3$ and $5$. Their intersection is trivial by Lagrange. For $a\in P,b\in Q$, the commutator $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$ lies in both normal subgroups, hence equals $1$. Their generators therefore commute; the product of generators has order $15$, since $(ab)^k=1$ forces $a^k=b^{-k}\in P\cap Q$ and thus both $3$ and $5$ divide $k$.
