Let $X=\{(g_1,\ldots,g_p):g_1\cdots g_p=1\}$. There are $|G|^{p-1}$ such tuples because the last entry is determined by the first $p-1$. Cyclic rotation preserves $X$: moving $g_1$ to the end conjugates the product by $g_1^{-1}$. The cyclic group of order $p$ acts by rotation, with each orbit of size $1$ or $p$. The fixed tuples are $(g,\ldots,g)$ with $g^p=1$, so their number is congruent to $|X|=0$ modulo $p$. Since $g=1$ is one solution, there is another. Its order divides $p$ and is not $1$, so it is $p$.
