A homomorphism sends commutators to commutators. A surjective homomorphism sends each derived subgroup onto the corresponding derived subgroup, by induction on the derived index. If $(G/N)^{(r)}=1$, then $G^{(r)}\le N$. Consequently $G^{(r+s)}\le N^{(s)}=1$ for an $s$ witnessing solvability of $N$. For a subgroup $H\le G$, induction gives $H^{(i)}\le G^{(i)}$; for a quotient use the surjective statement. These prove both closure claims.
