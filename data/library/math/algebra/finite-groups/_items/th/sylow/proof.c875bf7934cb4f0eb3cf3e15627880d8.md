For existence, induct on $|G|$; the case $n=0$ is trivial. If a proper subgroup has index prime to $p$, induction inside it supplies a subgroup of order $p^n$. Otherwise every noncentral class has centralizer a proper subgroup of index divisible by $p$. The @[class equation]math:algebra:groups-actions:th:orbit-stabilizer then shows $p\mid |Z(G)|$. By Cauchy's theorem the center contains a subgroup $C$ of order $p$. Induction gives a subgroup of order $p^{n-1}$ in $G/C$; its inverse image has order $p^n$.

Fix such a $P$ and let a @[p-subgroup]math:algebra:finite-groups:df:sylow-subgroup $Q$ act on $G/P$ by left multiplication. All nontrivial orbits have size divisible by $p$, while $|G/P|=m$ is prime to $p$, so a coset $gP$ is fixed. Thus $g^{-1}Qg\le P$. For $|Q|=|P|$, containment is equality, proving conjugacy. Hence $n_p=[G:N_G(P)]$, which divides $[G:P]=m$.

Let $P$ act on the Sylow subgroups by conjugation. If $P$ fixes a Sylow subgroup $Q$, it normalizes $Q$, so $PQ$ is a subgroup of p-power order. Since $Q$ already has maximal p-power order, $PQ=Q$ and $P=Q$. Thus there is exactly one fixed point, and $n_p\equiv1\pmod p$. Conjugacy gives the normality criterion.
