The commutator subgroup $G'$ is generated by $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$. Define $G^{(0)}=G$ and $G^{(i+1)}=(G^{(i)})'$. The group is solvable if $G^{(r)}=1$ for some finite $r$.

Conjugating a commutator gives a commutator, so $G'\trianglelefteq G$. The quotient $G/G'$ is abelian. Any homomorphism from $G$ to an @[abelian group]math:algebra:groups-actions:df:groups-homomorphisms kills $G'$, so factors uniquely through this quotient, called the abelianization.
