If $G$ is abelian, each $\rho(g)$ commutes with every $\rho(h)$ and hence is an intertwining endomorphism. By Schur's lemma it is scalar. Every line is then invariant, so irreducibility forces dimension one. For $C_n$, the image of $g$ is a nonzero complex number $z$ satisfying $z^n=1$; conversely every such $z$ defines a one-dimensional representation. The $n$ roots listed are distinct, and one-dimensional representations are isomorphic exactly when they have the same value on the generator.
