Starting with $h:FA\to B$, the composite is

$$
\varepsilon_BFG(h)F(\eta_A)=h\varepsilon_{FA}F(\eta_A)=h,
$$

where the first equality is naturality of $\varepsilon$ and the second is a triangle identity. Starting with $k:A\to GB$, the other composite is

$$
G(\varepsilon_B)GF(k)\eta_A=G(\varepsilon_B)\eta_{GB}k=k,
$$

using naturality of $\eta$ and the other triangle identity. For changes of source and target, functoriality and naturality of $\eta$ give the naturality equation for the first assignment. The inverse assignment is then natural as the inverse of a natural family of bijections. Hence these data supply an adjunction.
