Suppose $F$ has a quasi-inverse $G$ and natural isomorphisms $u:\id\Rightarrow GF$, $v:FG\Rightarrow\id$. Essential surjectivity follows from $v$. The functors $F$ and $G$ are faithful because $GF$ and $FG$ are naturally isomorphic to identity functors. For an arrow $h:FA\to FB$, set $f=u_B^{-1}G(h)u_A$. Naturality of $u$ gives $GF(f)=u_B f u_A^{-1}=G(h)$, and faithfulness of $G$ yields $F(f)=h$. Thus $F$ is full.

Conversely choose for each $D$ an object $GD$ and isomorphism $v_D:F(GD)\to D$. Full faithfulness defines $G(h)$ uniquely by $F(G(h))=v_E^{-1}hv_D$ for $h:D\to E$. Uniqueness proves functoriality, and this equation makes $v$ natural. Define $u_A:A\to GFA$ as the unique lift of $v_{FA}^{-1}$. These are isomorphisms because fully faithful functors reflect isomorphisms. Applying $F$ and using the displayed equations verifies naturality of $u$; faithfulness then gives it in $\mathcal C$. Hence $G$ is a quasi-inverse.
