For a small category $\mathcal C$ and a locally small category $\mathcal D$, the functor category $[\mathcal C,\mathcal D]$ has functors as objects and natural transformations as morphisms. Naturality is preserved by pointwise composition, and associativity and identities hold componentwise.

An equivalence consists of functors $F:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D$ and $G:\mathcal D\to\mathcal C$ with natural isomorphisms $\id_{\mathcal C}\cong GF$ and $FG\cong\id_{\mathcal D}$. These are weaker requirements than equalities of functors. Equivalent categories may have different sets of objects while encoding the same objects up to isomorphism and the same morphism structure.
