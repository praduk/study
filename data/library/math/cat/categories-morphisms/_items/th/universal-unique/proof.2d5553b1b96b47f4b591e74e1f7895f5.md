Initiality supplies unique maps $f:I\to J$ and $g:J\to I$. There is only one endomorphism of an initial object, and its identity is one such map. Hence $gf=\id_I$ and $fg=\id_J$, proving $f$ invertible. The uniqueness is part of initiality. Reverse all arrows for terminal objects. The same argument applies whenever a universal construction is expressed as an initial or terminal object in a category of structured objects; the word unique then refers to maps preserving that structure.
