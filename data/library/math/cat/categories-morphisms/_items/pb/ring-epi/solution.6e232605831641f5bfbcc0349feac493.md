The inclusion is injective, hence monic because equality can be checked on underlying functions. Given unital homomorphisms $u,v:\Q\to R$, both agree on every integer. For each nonzero integer $n$, its image is a unit under either map, and a unit's inverse is unique. Hence $u(1/n)=v(1/n)$ and then $u(m/n)=v(m/n)$. Thus any two maps agreeing after the inclusion agree everywhere, proving it epic. The inclusion is not surjective since $1/2$ is missing, and an isomorphism of rings must be bijective. The example depends on the specified category of morphisms.
