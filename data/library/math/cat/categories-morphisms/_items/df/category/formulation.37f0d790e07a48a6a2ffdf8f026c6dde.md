A category $\mathcal C$ has objects; for each pair $A,B$, a collection $\Hom_{\mathcal C}(A,B)$ of morphisms $A\to B$; identity morphisms $\id_A$; and compositions $g\circ f:A\to C$ for $f:A\to B$, $g:B\to C$. The axioms are associativity $h(gf)=(hg)f$ and $f\id_A=f=\id_Bf$.

It is small if all its objects and morphisms form sets, and locally small if each $\Hom(A,B)$ is a set. Two morphisms can be composed only when the indicated intermediate objects agree. Objects need not themselves be sets, and morphisms need not be functions.
