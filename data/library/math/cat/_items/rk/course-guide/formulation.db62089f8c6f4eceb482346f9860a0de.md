Prerequisites are sets and functions, proof writing, basic groups, and linear algebra. This course develops categories and functors, naturality and equivalence, limits and colimits, representability and Yoneda, adjunctions, and a first encounter with monads. Algebraic and order-theoretic examples make the universal properties concrete.

Composition is written $gf=g\circ f$: the rightmost map is applied first. Categories are locally small, meaning each collection of morphisms between two fixed objects is a set. Indexing categories for limits are small. Whenever a functor category or presheaf category is used, its domain is small, or a larger fixed universe is understood; this avoids treating a proper class as a set. In the equivalence criterion below the categories are small, and the usual axiom of choice is used explicitly to select representatives.

The course gives proofs of the core universal-property results, Yoneda, the unit–counit description of adjunctions, and preservation of limits by right adjoints. Worked problems distinguish categorical properties from properties of underlying functions. Higher categories, enriched categories, sheaf theory, and advanced monadicity are beyond this first course.

The source inspected for the formulation of the central results is Tom Leinster's [Basic Category Theory](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.09375), especially the chapters on adjoints, representables, and limits; the [author's book page](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~tl/bct/) supplies provenance. Wording, proofs, and computations below are original reconstructions of standard arguments rather than reproduced passages.
