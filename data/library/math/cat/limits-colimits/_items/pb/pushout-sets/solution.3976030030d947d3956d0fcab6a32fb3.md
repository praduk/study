Start from the disjoint union $A\amalg B$ and impose $a_1\sim b$ and $a_2\sim b$. The generated equivalence relation identifies all three elements, so the quotient is a singleton $P$. For maps $u:A\to X$ and $v:B\to X$ agreeing on $C$, agreement means $u(a_1)=v(b)=u(a_2)$. There is a unique map $P\to X$ sending its one point to this common value. Thus $P$ is the pushout. The maps from $A$ and $B$ into the pushout need not be injective, even though the word injection is customary for coproduct maps in other examples.
