A diagram in $\mathcal C$ indexed by a small category $J$ is a functor $D:J\to\mathcal C$. A cone with vertex $X$ is a family $p_j:X\to D(j)$ such that $D(a)p_i=p_j$ for every arrow $a:i\to j$.

A limit is a cone $(L,\lambda_j)$ such that each cone $(X,p_j)$ has a unique map $u:X\to L$ satisfying $\lambda_j u=p_j$ for all $j$. Thus a limit is terminal in the category of cones. Its uniqueness is up to the unique isomorphism commuting with all cone maps, not up to arbitrary object isomorphisms.
