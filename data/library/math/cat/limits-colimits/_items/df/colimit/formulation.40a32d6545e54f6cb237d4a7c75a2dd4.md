A cocone on $D:J\to\mathcal C$ has maps $D(j)\to X$ compatible with all arrows. A colimit is a cocone from which every cocone factors uniquely. This is the dual of a limit.

A pushout is the colimit of a span $A\leftarrow C\to B$. A coequalizer of $f,g:A\to B$ is a map $q:B\to Q$ with $qf=qg$, universal among such maps out of $B$. In sets, construct a coequalizer by quotienting $B$ by the smallest equivalence relation containing each pair $(f(a),g(a))$. The equivalence closure matters: the listed pairs alone need not be an equivalence relation.
