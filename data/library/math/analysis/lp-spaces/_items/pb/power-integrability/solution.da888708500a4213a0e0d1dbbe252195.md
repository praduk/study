The integrand is $x^{-\alpha p}$. Integrating on $[\epsilon,1]$ gives a finite limit as $\epsilon\downarrow0$ exactly when $\alpha p<1$; equality gives the divergent logarithm. Integrating on $[1,R]$ gives a finite limit as $R\uparrow\infty$ exactly when $\alpha p>1$, with equality again logarithmically divergent. @[Monotone convergence]math:analysis:lebesgue-integration:th:monotone-integrals identifies these improper integrals with the nonnegative Lebesgue integrals. The opposite thresholds show why $L^q\subseteq L^p$ need not hold on spaces of infinite measure.
