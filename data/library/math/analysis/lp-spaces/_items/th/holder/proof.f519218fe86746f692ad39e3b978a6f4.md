For $1<p<\infty$, elementary calculus gives Young's inequality $ab\le a^p/p+b^q/q$ for $a,b\ge0$: minimize $a^p/p-ab+b^q/q$ in $a$, with minimum zero at $a=b^{q-1}$. If both norms are nonzero, apply Young to $|f|/\norm f_p$ and $|g|/\norm g_q$, then integrate to obtain an upper bound $1/p+1/q=1$. If a norm is zero, the corresponding function vanishes almost everywhere. The endpoint case follows from $|fg|\le |f|\norm g_\infty$ almost everywhere, and the other endpoint is symmetric.
