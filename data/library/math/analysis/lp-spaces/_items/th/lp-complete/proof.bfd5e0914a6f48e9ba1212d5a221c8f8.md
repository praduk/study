First let $p<\infty$ and let $(f_n)$ be Cauchy in norm. Select a subsequence with $\norm{f_{n_{k+1}}-f_{n_k}}_p\le2^{-k}$. Put $h_k=|f_{n_{k+1}}-f_{n_k}|$. Minkowski bounds every finite sum of the $h_k$ by $\sum2^{-k}$ in $L^p$. @[Monotone convergence]math:analysis:lebesgue-integration:th:monotone-integrals of their $p$th powers gives $H=\sum h_k\in L^p$, so $H$ is finite almost everywhere. The telescoping series therefore converges absolutely almost everywhere to a measurable $f$ (set it to zero on the measurable divergence set). Its tail is bounded by $\sum_{j\ge k}h_j$, whose $L^p$ norm is at most $\sum_{j\ge k}2^{-j}$ by Fatou and the finite-sum bound. Thus $f_{n_k}\to f$ in $L^p$, and the Cauchy property gives convergence of the original sequence.

For $p=\infty$, take the same subsequence. Outside the union of countably many null exceptional sets, each pointwise increment is bounded by $2^{-k}$. The series converges uniformly there, yielding an essentially bounded measurable limit with the same norm-tail bound. Again the original @[Cauchy sequence]math:pst:metric:df:complete-total-bounded converges.
