If $\ell=0$, use $y=0$. Otherwise $M=\ker\ell$ is closed. Choose $v$ with $\ell(v)\ne0$ and decompose it orthogonally; its component in $M^\perp$ is nonzero. Scale that component to a vector $z\in M^\perp$ with $\ell(z)=1$. For every $x$, $x-\ell(x)z\in M$, so $\iprod{x}{z}=\ell(x)\norm z^2$. Therefore $y=z/\norm z^2$ represents $\ell$. @[Cauchy–Schwarz]math:analysis:hilbert-spaces:th:cauchy-schwarz gives $\norm\ell\le\norm y$, and evaluating at $y/\norm y$ gives equality when $y\ne0$. If two representatives exist, their difference has zero @[inner product]math:analysis:hilbert-spaces:df:hilbert with every vector, including itself, so they agree.
