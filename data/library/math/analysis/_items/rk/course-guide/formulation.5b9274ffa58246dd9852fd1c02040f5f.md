Prerequisites: a proof-based real analysis course, metric-space completeness and compactness, and linear algebra over $\R$ and $\C$. Topics progress from measure construction through convergence theorems and $L^p$ spaces to Banach spaces, Hilbert spaces, and compact self-adjoint operators.

The statements, proofs, and solved problems are concise original presentations. Full proofs are supplied where the local development supports them. The measure-extension theorem, Tonelli's theorem, Radon–Nikodym existence, Hahn decomposition existence, Hahn–Banach, open mapping, Banach–Alaoglu, and the compact spectral theorem are explicitly marked when their major existence proofs are not included; those cards do not pretend that a proof outline is a complete proof.

Inspected author sources: John K. Hunter, [Measure Theory](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~hunter/measure_theory/measure_notes.pdf), and Hunter and Bruno Nachtergaele, *Applied Analysis*, [Banach spaces](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~hunter/book/ch5.pdf), [Hilbert spaces](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~hunter/book/ch6.pdf), together with Richard Melrose, [MIT 18.102, Lecture 20 on compact spectral theory](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-102-introduction-to-functional-analysis-spring-2009/e49973bf614df04d15634addfbb1a082_MIT18_102s09_lec20.pdf). These are mathematical reference texts, not primary research claims. The course establishes a graduate core; it does not claim to cover all of harmonic analysis, distributions, or PDE theory.

Conventions: measures are nonnegative unless explicitly called signed; $0\cdot\infty=0$ in nonnegative integration; equality of $L^p$ elements means equality almost everywhere. Complex inner products are linear in the first argument.
