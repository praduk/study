For measurable spaces $(X,\mathcal A)$ and $(Y,\mathcal B)$, the product sigma algebra $\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal B$ is generated by rectangles $A\times B$. If $\mu,\nu$ are sigma-finite measures, there is a unique measure $\mu\otimes\nu$ on this sigma algebra satisfying

$$
(\mu\otimes\nu)(A\times B)=\mu(A)\nu(B),
$$

with $0\cdot\infty=0$. Existence and uniqueness follow from the premeasure extension theorem.

The product of complete measures need not be complete. A product-measurable function has measurable sections for every fixed coordinate; a function measurable only for the completed product measure may have exceptional nonmeasurable sections on a null set of coordinates.
