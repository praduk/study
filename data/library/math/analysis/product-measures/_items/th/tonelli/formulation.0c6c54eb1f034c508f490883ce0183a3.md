Let $(X,\mathcal A,\mu)$ and $(Y,\mathcal B,\nu)$ be sigma-finite. For an $\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal B$-measurable $f:X\times Y\to[0,\infty]$, the section integrals are measurable and

$$
\int f\,d(\mu\otimes\nu)=\int_X\left(\int_Y f(x,y)\,d\nu(y)\right)d\mu(x)
=\int_Y\left(\int_X f(x,y)\,d\mu(x)\right)d\nu(y).
$$

Every expression may be infinite. The proof, including the extension from rectangles to all product-measurable sets, is not included; see Hunter, [Measure Theory, Chapter 5](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~hunter/measure_theory/measure_notes.pdf). Nonnegativity is what permits interchange before proving integrability.
