Every finite signed measure $\nu$ admits a measurable partition $X=P\sqcup N$ such that $\nu(A)\ge0$ for every measurable $A\subseteq P$ and $\nu(A)\le0$ for every measurable $A\subseteq N$. It has a unique representation

$$
\nu=\nu^+-\nu^-
$$

by finite positive mutually singular measures, with $\nu^+(A)=\nu(A\cap P)$ and $\nu^-(A)=-\nu(A\cap N)$. Its total variation is $|\nu|=\nu^++\nu^-$.

The existence proof of the Hahn partition is not included; see Hunter, [Measure Theory, Section 6.7](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~hunter/measure_theory/measure_notes.pdf). The partition itself need not be unique, but the positive and negative measures are.
