If $\mu,\nu$ are sigma-finite positive measures on the same measurable space and $\nu\ll\mu$, there exists a measurable $h:X\to[0,\infty]$, unique $\mu$-almost everywhere, such that

$$
\nu(A)=\int_A h\,d\mu\quad(A\in\mathcal A).
$$

Write $h=d\nu/d\mu$. More generally, a finite signed measure $\nu$ and sigma-finite $\mu$ have a unique decomposition $\nu=h\mu+\nu_s$ with $h\in L^1(\mu)$ and $|\nu_s|\perp\mu$.

Existence is a major theorem whose proof is not included here; see Hunter, [Measure Theory, Sections 6.8–6.9](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~hunter/measure_theory/measure_notes.pdf). The hypotheses cannot be discarded freely: a measure need not admit a density merely because it is defined on the same sets as $\mu$.
