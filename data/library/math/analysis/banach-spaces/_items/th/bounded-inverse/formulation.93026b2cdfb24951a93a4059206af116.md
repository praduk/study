If $T:X\to Y$ is a bounded linear bijection between Banach spaces, then its inverse is bounded. This is the bounded-inverse consequence of the open mapping theorem; its existence estimate is not proved here. See Hunter and Nachtergaele, [Applied Analysis, Theorem 5.23](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~hunter/book/ch5.pdf).

Consequently, an everywhere-defined linear map $S:X\to Y$ between Banach spaces is bounded whenever its graph $\{(x,Sx):x\in X\}$ is closed in $X\times Y$. To verify this consequence, equip the product with the sum norm. The closed graph is Banach, and its first-coordinate projection onto $X$ is a bounded linear bijection. Its inverse is bounded by the preceding theorem; composing that inverse with the second-coordinate projection gives $S$.
