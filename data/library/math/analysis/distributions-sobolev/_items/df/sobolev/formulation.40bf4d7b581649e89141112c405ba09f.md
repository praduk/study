Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be open. For $f\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$, a locally integrable function $g$ is its weak derivative $\partial^\alpha f$ when $\int g\phi=(-1)^{|\alpha|}\int f\partial^\alpha\phi$ for every @math:analysis:distributions-sobolev:df:distribution test function. Derivatives are defined as @[equivalence classes]math:set:relations-functions:df:equivalence-relation almost everywhere.

For an integer $k\ge0$ and $1\le p\le\infty$, the Sobolev space $W^{k,p}(\Omega)$ consists of $f\in L^p(\Omega)$ whose weak derivatives of every order at most $k$ also belong to $L^p$. For $p<\infty$ use norm $(\sum_{|\alpha|\le k}\|\partial^\alpha f\|_p^p)^{1/p}$; for $p=\infty$ use the maximum of those norms. Write $H^k=W^{k,2}$. The underlying function classes are those of @math:analysis:lp-spaces:df:lp.

For example, $|x|$ on $(-1,1)$ has weak first derivative $\operatorname{sgn}x$ and belongs to $H^1$ despite not being classically differentiable at zero. Splitting integration by parts at zero proves that derivative identity. Boundary trace theorems require domain hypotheses; Sobolev notation alone does not prescribe boundary conditions or an operator domain.
