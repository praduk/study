For a nonnegative simple function $s=\sum_{j=1}^m a_j1_{A_j}$ written on disjoint measurable sets, define $\int s\,d\mu=\sum_j a_j\mu(A_j)$, with $0\cdot\infty=0$. For measurable $f\ge0$ define

$$
\int f\,d\mu=\sup\left\{\int s\,d\mu:0\le s\le f,\ s\text{ simple}\right\}.
$$

For real $f$, write $f^+=\max(f,0)$ and $f^-=\max(-f,0)$. It is integrable when $\int|f|<\infty$, and then $\int f=\int f^+-\int f^-$. For complex functions integrate real and imaginary parts. The expression $\infty-\infty$ is never a defined integral.
