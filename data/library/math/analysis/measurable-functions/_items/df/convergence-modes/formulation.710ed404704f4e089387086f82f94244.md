Throughout this entry, $f_n$ and $f$ are measurable finite real- or complex-valued functions. A sequence of measurable functions converges almost everywhere to $f$ if pointwise convergence fails only on a null set. It converges in measure if, for every $\epsilon>0$,

$$
\mu(\{|f_n-f|>\epsilon\})\longrightarrow0.
$$

For $1\le p<\infty$, convergence in $L^p$ means $\int|f_n-f|^p\,d\mu\to0$. Essential-uniform convergence means $\norm{f_n-f}_\infty\to0$, where the norm ignores null sets. These definitions have different quantifier and integrability content; none should be substituted for another without a theorem.
