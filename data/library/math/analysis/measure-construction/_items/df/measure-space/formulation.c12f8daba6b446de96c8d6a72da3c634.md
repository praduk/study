A sigma algebra $\mathcal A$ on $X$ contains $X$, is closed under complements in $X$, and is closed under countable unions. A measure is a map $\mu:\mathcal A\to[0,\infty]$ with $\mu(\varnothing)=0$ and

$$
\mu\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu(A_n)
$$

for pairwise disjoint $A_n\in\mathcal A$. The triple $(X,\mathcal A,\mu)$ is a measure space.

It is finite if $\mu(X)<\infty$, sigma-finite if $X$ is a countable union of finite-measure sets, and complete if every subset of a measurable null set is measurable. The Borel sigma algebra of a topological space is the sigma algebra generated by its open sets.
