Mathematics is organized as a sequence of core courses. **Algebra, Analysis, Topology, and Differential Geometry are graduate coursework.** Their starting assumptions and subject matter differ from the undergraduate courses with related names.

## Foundations and a categorical language

- @math:prop:rk:course-map — propositional syntax, semantics, deduction, and metatheory.
- @math:fol:rk:course-guide — first-order syntax, semantics, completeness, compactness, and models.
- @math:set:rk:course-guide — axiomatic sets, cardinality, ordinals, Choice, and the independence boundary.
- @math:cat:rk:course-guide — categories, naturality, universal constructions, Yoneda, and adjunctions; a companion to graduate algebra and topology.

## Undergraduate courses

- @math:num:rk:course-guide — divisibility, congruences, arithmetic functions, primitive roots, quadratic reciprocity, Diophantine equations, and approximation.
- @math:uganalysis:rk:course-guide — completeness, convergence, continuity, differentiation, integration, uniform limits, and introductory metric and multivariable analysis.
- @math:diffeq:rk:course-guide — first-order and linear equations, existence and uniqueness, systems and stability, Laplace and series methods, and introductory boundary-value and PDE methods.
- @math:pst:rk:course-guide — topological constructions, separation, compactness, connectedness, and metric-space foundations.
- @math:complex:rk:course-guide — holomorphic functions, Cauchy theory, series, singularities, residues, zero counting, and conformal maps.

## Graduate courses

- @math:algebra:rk:course-guide — groups and actions, Sylow theory, rings, factorization, Noetherian conditions, modules and tensors, fields and Galois theory, and introductory representations.
- @math:analysis:rk:course-guide — measure and Lebesgue integration, convergence theorems, $L^p$ spaces, product and signed measures, and Banach, Hilbert, and compact-operator theory.
- @math:top:rk:course-guide — algebraic topology: homotopy, fundamental groups, covering spaces, CW complexes, homology, cohomology, and duality. Point-set foundations have their own course above.
- @math:diffgeo:rk:course-guide — smooth manifolds, tangent and vector bundles, flows, differential forms, Stokes and de Rham theory, connections, geodesics, and Riemannian curvature.

## Suggested dependencies

Begin with proof fluency and the logical and set-theoretic foundations. Undergraduate Real Analysis supports graduate Analysis and the rigorous parts of Differential Equations and Complex Analysis. Point Set Topology supports graduate Topology and Differential Geometry. Graduate Algebra assumes undergraduate linear and abstract algebra; its group and module material supports algebraic topology. Differential Geometry also assumes multivariable calculus and linear algebra. Category Theory can be studied alongside these graduate subjects as its examples become familiar.

Read each course in its authored topic order. Definitions fix conventions; theorem hypotheses are part of what must be learned. Problems have separate worked solutions. Main proof supplements contain arguments for the exact stated theorem, while longer structural results used without proof explicitly identify that boundary and an inspected source. These courses provide substantive cores, not an exhaustive treatment of every specialty in mathematics; individual guides describe their limits.
