By definition, formulas are finite tagged trees. An atom, each nullary constant, the unary constructor, and each binary constructor have different root tags, so no object produced by one case can be produced by another. The unary constructor stores exactly one child; equality $\neg A=\neg B$ of tagged trees therefore implies $A=B$. A binary constructor stores its connective tag and its ordered pair of children; equality $(A\circ B)=(C\bullet D)$ therefore implies $\circ=\bullet$, $A=C$, and $B=D$. Hence exactly one case applies and all displayed immediate constituents are unique.

For fully parenthesized surface strings, the same tree is recovered uniquely: the first symbol distinguishes atoms, constants, and negation from a parenthesized binary formula. In the binary case, scanning from the opening parenthesis while tracking parenthesis depth locates the unique connective at depth one; the substrings on its two sides recursively determine the two children. Thus the display notation is uniquely readable as well.
