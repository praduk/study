The **object language** consists of propositional variables, logical constants, connectives, and the formulas built from them. The **metalanguage** is the mathematical language used to define and reason about those formulas.

In this course, $A,B,C$ are metavariables ranging over formulas. Symbols such as $\Gamma$ and $\Delta$ range over sets of formulas. Thus $A\to B$ is an object-language formula, while $A\models B$, $A\vdash B$, and a prose phrase such as 'therefore' are metalinguistic assertions. The distinction matters: $\to$, $\models$, and $\vdash$ do not mean the same thing.
