Collect the finitely many atoms occurring in the input; suppose there are $n$. Enumerate the $2^n$ valuations of those atoms and recursively evaluate each input formula. Locality shows that these rows cover every semantically distinct total valuation.

A formula is satisfiable exactly when at least one row gives value $1$, and a tautology exactly when every row does. Two formulas are equivalent exactly when their columns agree. For finite $\Gamma$, $\Gamma\models A$ exactly when there is no row making all members of $\Gamma$ true and $A$ false. Every search is over a finite list and therefore halts. Finally, finite-premise soundness and completeness identify $\Gamma\vdash A$ with $\Gamma\models A$, so the same test decides derivability. This proves decidability; it does not claim that exhaustive truth tables are always efficient.
