Work backward from a conclusion only as a strategy, then verify the completed derivation forward. A target conjunction suggests $\land I$; a target conditional suggests a subproof for $\to I$; a target negation suggests $\neg I$; and an available disjunction is useful only when both cases yield the same target.

A formula proved inside a subproof may depend on its temporary assumption. Once that subproof closes, the formula cannot be cited on its own. What survives is the conditional, negation, case conclusion, or RAA conclusion licensed by the closing rule. Many plausible-looking invalid proofs are scope errors rather than algebra errors.
