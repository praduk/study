Sets $A$ and $B$ are **equinumerous**, written $A\approx B$, when there is a bijection $A\to B$.

Write $|A|\le |B|$ when there is an injection $A\to B$, and $|A|<|B|$ when $|A|\le|B|$ but $A\not\approx B$. These symbols initially compare sizes without treating $|A|$ as a particular set; cardinal numbers will later provide canonical representatives for well-orderable sizes.

Equinumerosity is an equivalence relation. The comparison relation is reflexive and transitive. Its antisymmetry up to equinumerosity is the Cantor--Schröder--Bernstein theorem and requires proof.
