For each $n$, send a tuple $(a_0,\ldots,a_{n-1})\in\omega^n$ to the finite set of its coordinates. This is a surjection from the countable set $\omega^n$ onto the subsets of size at most $n$. Taking the explicitly enumerated union over $n\in\omega$ shows that $[\omega]^{<\omega}$ is countable.

Every subset of $\omega$ is either finite or infinite, so

$$
\mathcal{P}(\omega)=[\omega]^{<\omega}\cup[\omega]^\omega.
$$

If $[\omega]^\omega$ were countable, the union on the right would be a union of two countable sets and hence countable. That would contradict the uncountability of $\mathcal{P}(\omega)$. Thus $[\omega]^\omega$ is uncountable.
