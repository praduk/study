A linear order $(W,<)$ is a **well-order** if every nonempty subset $A\subseteq W$ has a least element.

For $w\in W$, the strict initial segment below $w$ is

$$
W_{<w}=\{x\in W:x<w\}.
$$

An initial segment of $W$ is a subset $I$ such that $y\in I$ and $x<y$ imply $x\in I$. It is **proper** if $I\ne W$. Every segment $W_{<w}$ is proper. A fundamental theorem proved later says every proper initial segment of a well-order has this form.

Well-foundedness alone does not imply linearity. A well-order is both well-founded and linear.
