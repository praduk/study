Divisibility is reflexive and transitive. If $a\mid b$ and $b\mid a$ for positive integers, then $a\le b\le a$ in the ordinary numerical order, so $a=b$; hence it is antisymmetric. It is not linear because neither $2$ nor $3$ divides the other.

The integer $1$ divides every positive integer, so it is least. If $n$ were greatest, every positive integer would divide $n$, but $n+1$ does not; hence no greatest element exists.

Let $S$ be nonempty and choose its least member $m$ in the ordinary numerical order. If $s\in S$ and $s\mid m$ with $s\ne m$, then $s<m$, contradicting the choice of $m$. Thus $m$ is divisibility-minimal. Nevertheless the divisibility order is not a well-order because it is not linear. This example separates well-founded behavior from being a well-order.
