A set $T$ is **transitive** if every member of a member of $T$ is a member of $T$:

$$
\forall x\in T\,\forall y\in x\,(y\in T).
$$

Equivalently, $\bigcup T\subseteq T$.

A **von Neumann ordinal** is a transitive set $\alpha$ such that $\in$ linearly well-orders $\alpha$. For ordinals, write $\alpha<\beta$ for $\alpha\in\beta$ and $\alpha\le\beta$ for $\alpha\subseteq\beta$; the equivalence of these comparisons follows from ordinal trichotomy.

Natural induction shows every $n\in\omega$ is an ordinal and
$n=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$. The set $\omega$ is the first infinite ordinal.
