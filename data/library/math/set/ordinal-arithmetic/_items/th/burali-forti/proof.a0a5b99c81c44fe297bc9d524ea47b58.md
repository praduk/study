Suppose $O$ were the set of all ordinals. The union
$\gamma=\bigcup O$ would be an ordinal by the union-of-ordinals theorem. Therefore its successor $\gamma+1$ would also be an ordinal and hence would belong to $O$.

Every member of $O$ is a subset of $\bigcup O=\gamma$, so $\gamma+1\subseteq\gamma$. But $\gamma\in\gamma+1$ while $\gamma\notin\gamma$, contradicting that inclusion. Hence the ordinals do not form a set.
