For (1),
$1+\omega=\sup_{n<\omega}(1+n)=\omega$, because the finite ordinals $1+n$ are cofinal in $\omega$. The successor $\omega+1$ contains $\omega$ as an element, so it is strictly larger.

For (2), associativity gives

$$
(\omega+3)+\omega=\omega+(3+\omega)=\omega+\omega=\omega\cdot2,
$$

using $3+\omega=\omega$ and the successor clause for multiplication.

For (3), the limit clause gives

$$
2\cdot\omega=\sup_{n<\omega}(2\cdot n)=\omega,
$$

since the even finite ordinals are cofinal in $\omega$. But
$\omega\cdot2=\omega\cdot1+\omega=\omega+\omega$, which properly contains its initial segment $\omega$.

For (4), this is precisely the limit clause in the definition of exponentiation, since $\omega$ is a nonzero limit ordinal and the base is nonzero.
