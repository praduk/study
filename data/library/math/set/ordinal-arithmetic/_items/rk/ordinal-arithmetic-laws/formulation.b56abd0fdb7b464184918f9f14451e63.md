Transfinite induction gives associativity of ordinal addition and multiplication, and left distributivity

$$
\alpha(\beta+\gamma)=\alpha\beta+\alpha\gamma.
$$

The functions $\beta\mapsto\alpha+\beta$, $\beta\mapsto\alpha\beta$ for $\alpha>0$, and $\beta\mapsto\alpha^\beta$ for $\alpha>1$ are continuous at limit ordinals by definition. The first is strictly increasing; multiplication and exponentiation require the displayed positivity hypotheses for strictness.

Commutativity fails. Directly from the recursion equations,

$$
1+\omega=\sup_{n<\omega}(1+n)=\omega,
\qquad
\omega+1=\omega\cup\{\omega\}>\omega.
$$

Likewise $2\cdot\omega=\omega$ but $\omega\cdot2=\omega+\omega>\omega$. Right distributivity fails in general. Do not import familiar integer algebra without checking which side of an ordinal identity is recursive.
