For sets $A$ and $B$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A\subseteq B &\iff \forall x(x\in A\to x\in B),\\
A\cup B &= \{x:x\in A\lor x\in B\},\\
A\cap B &= \{x\in A:x\in B\},\\
A\setminus B &= \{x\in A:x\notin B\},\\
A\triangle B &= (A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A).
\end{aligned}
$$

Union and Pairing give $A\cup B$; Separation gives the remaining subsets. For a set-indexed family $\mathcal A$, $\bigcup\mathcal A$ always exists. If $\mathcal A\ne\varnothing$, then $\bigcap\mathcal A$ exists by separating from any chosen member of $\mathcal A$. An unrestricted empty intersection would be a universal set, so it is not defined without a specified ambient set.
