For a theory $T$, a sentence $\varphi$ is **independent of $T$** when neither $\varphi$ nor $\neg\varphi$ is provable from $T$, normally under the metatheoretic assumption that $T$ is consistent. The strongest standard first-course summary is:

- Gödel's constructible-universe argument gives the relative-consistency implication
  $\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZF})\Rightarrow
  \operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZF}+\mathrm{AC}+\mathrm{GCH})$.
- Cohen's forcing argument gives
  $\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC})\Rightarrow
  \operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC}+\neg\mathrm{CH})$.

Consequently, if ZFC is consistent, then ZFC proves neither CH nor its negation. These are relative consistency results, not proofs inside ZFC that ZFC itself is consistent, and not a claim that truth is merely subjective. Models of the same axioms can disagree about CH.

This course records the exact logical status but does not supply fake “short proofs” of the deep results. Establishing them requires the constructible universe and forcing, each normally a substantial later course.
