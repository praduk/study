The **cardinality of the continuum** is

$$
\mathfrak c=|\mathbb R|=2^{\aleph_0}=|\mathcal{P}(\omega)|.
$$

The equality with binary sequences follows from characteristic functions; the standard comparison between binary sequences and real numbers gives the same cardinal even though binary expansions are not unique at dyadic rationals.

The **Continuum Hypothesis (CH)** is

$$
2^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_1.
$$

Equivalently, there is no cardinal strictly between $\aleph_0$ and $\mathfrak c$. The **Generalized Continuum Hypothesis (GCH)** asserts

$$
2^\kappa=\kappa^+
$$

for every infinite cardinal $\kappa$, where $\kappa^+$ is the least larger cardinal. Cantor's theorem proves only $\kappa<2^\kappa$; it does not determine which larger cardinal $2^\kappa$ is.
