Let $\lambda=\bigcup A$. If $x\in y\in\lambda$, then $y\in\alpha$ for some $\alpha\in A$. Since $\alpha$ is transitive, $x\in\alpha$, hence $x\in\lambda$. Thus $\lambda$ is transitive.

If $x,y\in\lambda$, choose $\alpha,\beta\in A$ containing them. Ordinal trichotomy makes $\alpha$ and $\beta$ comparable by inclusion; both $x$ and $y$ therefore lie in one ordinal and are comparable by membership. Any nonempty $X\subseteq\lambda$ contains an element $x$; choose $\alpha\in A$ with $x\in\alpha$. The set $X\cap\alpha$ is nonempty and has an $\in$-least member $m$. If $y\in X$ and $y\in m$, transitivity puts $y\in\alpha$, contradicting minimality; hence $m$ is least in all of $X$. So $\lambda$ is an ordinal.

For $\alpha\in A$, every member of $\alpha$ belongs to $\bigcup A$, so $\alpha\subseteq\lambda$. If every $\alpha\in A$ is a subset of $\beta$, then every member of $\lambda$ lies in some $\alpha\in A$ and hence in $\beta$. Therefore $\lambda\subseteq\beta$, proving leastness.
