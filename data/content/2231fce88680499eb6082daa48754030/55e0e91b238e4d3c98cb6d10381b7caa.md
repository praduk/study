The **successor ordinal** of $\alpha$ is

$$
\alpha+1=\alpha\cup\{\alpha\}.
$$

It is the least ordinal strictly greater than $\alpha$. A nonzero ordinal that is not a successor is a **limit ordinal**. Equivalently, nonzero $\lambda$ is limit when $\alpha+1<\lambda$ for every $\alpha<\lambda$.

For a set $A$ of ordinals, its supremum is

$$
\sup A=\bigcup A.
$$

The union-of-ordinals theorem proves that this is an ordinal and the least ordinal greater than or equal to every member of $A$.
