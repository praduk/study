Several principles weaker than full Choice are common:

- **Countable Choice** selects from every countable family of nonempty sets.
- **Dependent Choice** builds a countable sequence following a serial relation.
- The **Boolean Prime Ideal Theorem** is weaker than Choice but still proves many compactness results.

These principles are not interchangeable in ZF. For example, “a countable union of countable sets is countable” needs a choice principle when enumerations of the members are not supplied. Likewise, “every infinite set has a countably infinite subset” is not a theorem of ZF.

Whenever a proof says “choose one for each index,” identify whether the choices are finite, definable, supplied as data, or genuinely use an axiom of choice. Later ZFC results are labeled so they are not accidentally presented as ZF theorems.
