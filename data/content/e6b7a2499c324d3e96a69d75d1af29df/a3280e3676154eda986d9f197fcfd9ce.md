First eliminate $↔$ and $→$ using their classical Boolean definitions. Then push negation inward using double negation, De Morgan laws, and the quantifier dualities $¬∀xφ↔∃x¬φ$ and $¬∃xφ↔∀x¬φ$.

Formally, define recursive translations $N^+(φ)$ and $N^-(φ)$ representing NNF versions of $φ$ and $¬φ$. For every base formula $B$—an atomic formula or $⊥$—put $N^+(B)=B$ and $N^-(B)=¬B$. For negation, put $N^+(¬α)=N^-(α)$ and $N^-(¬α)=N^+(α)$. For conjunction, put $N^+(α∧β)=N^+(α)∧N^+(β)$ and $N^-(α∧β)=N^-(α)∨N^-(β)$; use the dual clauses for disjunction. After replacing $α→β$ by $¬α∨β$, apply these clauses. For quantifiers, preserve the quantifier under $N^+$ and swap $∀$ with $∃$ under $N^-$ while recursively translating the matrix. Structural induction proves $N^+(φ)↔φ$ and $N^-(φ)↔¬φ$. Every output is in NNF, and the recursion never binds a new free variable, so free variables are preserved.
