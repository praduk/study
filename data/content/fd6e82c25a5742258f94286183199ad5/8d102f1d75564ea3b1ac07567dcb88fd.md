Define the construction depth of an expression. For terms, use ordinary induction on depth. Depth $0$ terms are variables or constants, so the base hypotheses apply. If $t=f(t_1,…,t_n)$ has positive depth, every $t_i$ has smaller depth; the induction hypothesis gives the property for each $t_i$, and closure under the function constructor gives it for $t$.

For formulas, again induct on depth. Depth $0$ formulas are atomic formulas or $⊥$. At positive depth, unique outer form gives one of $¬φ$, $(φ◦ψ)$ with $◦∈\{∧,∨,→\}$, $∀xφ$, or $∃xφ$. Every immediate constituent has smaller depth, so the corresponding closure hypothesis applies. The grammar says every term and formula is obtained after finitely many constructor steps, so the induction covers all expressions.
