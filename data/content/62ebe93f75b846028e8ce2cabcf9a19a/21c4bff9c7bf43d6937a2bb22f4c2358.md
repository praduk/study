A natural-deduction derivation is a finite tree whose leaves are assumptions or permitted zero-premise rules and whose internal nodes follow inference rules. Rules such as implication introduction and existential elimination discharge marked assumption occurrences.

Write $Γ⊢φ$ when there is a derivation of $φ$ whose undischarged assumptions all belong to $Γ$. Because every derivation is finite, only finitely many members of $Γ$ occur in it. Write $⊬φ$ for failure of derivability. This is a syntactic relation and is defined without reference to structures.
