The reverse direction is resolution soundness. For the forward direction, discard tautological clauses and induct on the number of atoms occurring in the finite clause set $S$. If no atom occurs, the only non-tautological clause is the empty clause. Unsatisfiability therefore means that the empty clause is already present, which is a refutation.

For the induction step, choose an atom $p$. Partition $S$ into clauses $C\cup\{p\}$, clauses $D\cup\{\neg p\}$, and clauses containing neither literal. Form $E$ from the third group together with every non-tautological resolvent $C\cup D$ of a positive and a negative parent. Then $E$ contains fewer atoms.

We claim $S$ is satisfiable exactly when $E$ is. A model of $S$, restricted to the remaining atoms, satisfies the unchanged clauses and all resolvents by soundness, so it satisfies $E$. Conversely, let $u$ satisfy $E$. If $u$ satisfies every positive residual $C$, extend it by setting $p=0$; all positive clauses remain satisfied through $C$, and all negative clauses through $\neg p$. If some positive residual $C_0$ is false under $u$, then every negative residual $D$ must be true, because the resolvent $C_0\cup D$ belongs to $E$ unless tautological, in which case $D$ contains the complement of a literal in $C_0$ and is also true when $C_0$ is false. Extend $u$ by setting $p=1$; all positive clauses are then true through $p$, and all negative clauses are true through their residuals. Thus the claim holds.

If $S$ is unsatisfiable, $E$ is unsatisfiable. By induction, $E$ has a resolution refutation. Each non-original clause of $E$ is itself a one-step resolvent of clauses in $S$. Replacing its use in the refutation by that one-step derivation gives a resolution refutation from $S$. This completes the induction.
