A theory $T$ is syntactically consistent when $T⊬⊥$. It is negation-complete when for every sentence $φ$ in its language, either $φ∈T$ or $¬φ∈T$. It is deductively closed when $T⊢φ$ implies $φ∈T$. A maximal consistent theory is a consistent theory with no proper consistent extension in the same language.

In classical logic, a maximal consistent theory is negation-complete and deductively closed. `Complete theory` in this sense should not be confused with completeness of a proof system.
