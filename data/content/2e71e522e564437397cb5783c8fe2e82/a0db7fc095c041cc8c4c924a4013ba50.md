By definition, $0=\varnothing$ belongs to every inductive set, so $0\in\omega$. If $x\in\omega$, then $x$ belongs to every inductive set $J$; because each $J$ is closed under successor, $S(x)$ belongs to every such $J$. Also $S(x)\in I$, since the chosen $I$ is inductive. Hence $S(x)\in\omega$, and $\omega$ is inductive.

If $J$ is any inductive set and $x\in\omega$, the defining condition for membership in $\omega$ says in particular that $x\in J$. Thus $\omega\subseteq J$.
