Add a new constant $c$ and the sentences $\bar n<c$ for every standard natural number $n$. Any finite subset mentions only $\bar 0<c,…,\bar N<c$ up to some largest $N$ and is satisfied by expanding the standard structure with $c=N+1$. Thus $\operatorname{Th}(ℕ)∪\{\bar n<c:n∈ℕ\}$ is finitely satisfiable.

By compactness it has a model $𝔐$. The element $c^𝔐$ is larger than the interpretation of every standard numeral. Consequently $𝔐$ cannot be isomorphic to the standard model, even though its reduct satisfies every first-order sentence true in $ℕ$. This does not put a nonstandard element into the actual standard structure; it constructs a different model of the same complete theory.
