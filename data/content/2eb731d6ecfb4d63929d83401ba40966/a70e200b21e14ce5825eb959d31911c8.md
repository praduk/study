Church's theorem says that no algorithm decides validity for arbitrary first-order sentences in general: no total procedure returns the correct yes-or-no answer on every input. By completeness, theoremhood in any sound complete effective calculus is therefore not decidable, even though validities can be enumerated.

The boundary matters. Propositional validity is decidable by finite truth tables, and restricted first-order fragments can be decidable; unrestricted quantification over relational structures crosses into undecidability. General satisfiability is not semidecidable. If it were, then on input $φ$ one could dovetail the validity semidecider on $φ$ with the assumed satisfiability semidecider on $¬φ$. The first halts exactly when $φ$ is valid; the second halts exactly when $φ$ is not valid. One would therefore decide validity, contradicting Church's theorem. Finite-model questions require separate theorems and must not be inferred from ordinary compactness.
