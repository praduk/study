For a sentence $φ$, write $𝔐⊨φ$ when $𝔐,g⊨φ$ for any assignment $g$. A structure $𝔐$ is a model of a theory $T$, written $𝔐⊨T$, when it satisfies every sentence in $T$.

A formula is satisfiable when it is true in some structure under some assignment. A theory is satisfiable when it has a model. A sentence is valid, written $⊨φ$, when every structure for its language satisfies it. Unsatisfiable means having no such structure or assignment; it does not mean merely false in the intended example.
