For terms, induct on string length. A one-token variable or constant has only its declared lexical category. Otherwise the first token must be a function symbol $f$ with known arity $n$, followed by a parenthesized comma-separated list. Scanning while counting nested parentheses identifies the unique top-level commas and closing parenthesis, hence the unique immediate subterms; the induction hypothesis parses each subterm uniquely.

For formulas, atomic relation applications are parsed by the same arity-and-parenthesis argument, and equality has its distinguished top-level equality symbol between two terms. The token $⊥$, a leading negation, a leading quantifier together with its variable, and an outer parenthesized binary connective have disjoint forms. In the binary case, scanning at parenthesis depth one locates the unique main connective. Immediate constituents are shorter and therefore uniquely parsed by induction. Disjoint symbol classes prevent any case from being mistaken for another.
